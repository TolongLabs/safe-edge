Seven rules for people in frames.

Safe Edge is a defect-inspection aid. It does not identify people, and it does not certify a water edge safe.

  • Edges, not people.

    Patrols aim at assets. Frames that contain people are discarded before storage, so stored evidence never shows a person. There is no face recognition, tracking or audio.

  • Fly when the park is empty.

    Patrols run at first light or another agreed window, and hold for lightning and wet surfaces.

  • 50 m from people.

    Plans keep CAAM's 50 m separation from people, with zoom lenses and stand-off distance, and 30 m during take-off and landing. A camera station with people near it waits for a clear pass.

  • No capture over homes.

    Geofences mark homes and playgrounds as no-capture zones. The planner drops any camera station whose frame would cover one.

  • No location in public photos.

    The team's real photos were taken with permission and no people in frame, and their location data is stripped from the public build.

  • Two people per fix.

    The person who records a repair cannot accept it. The reviewer must differ from the installer and from the person who confirmed the finding.

  • Simulated means labelled.

    Every simulated flight, detection and repair in the demo carries a visible label, and every seeded record carries simulated: true.

Before live capture.

This page describes the product's design. It is not a privacy policy, and it makes no compliance claim.

Malaysia's PDPA can treat an identifiable photo as personal data. A data protection officer and an impact assessment are likely before live capture, and the one-asset pilot lists a data protection impact assessment among the approvals it needs. See the drone phases.

Blur, notices or a permit alone would not make a patrol privacy-safe. Until a pilot flies under its approvals, the phone patrol carries the loop: the walk crew photographs the same fixed viewpoints.
