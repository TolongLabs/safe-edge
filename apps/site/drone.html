Every segment gets a score.

The planner splits each water edge into segments and ranks them, so the next patrol flies where the risk has grown.

Each segment has a side, a length, the views it needs and the items it should hold: fence runs, covers, gratings, gates, ring buoys and signs. From the camera footprint and overlap, the planner derives offset flight lines and camera stations for every selected segment.

The parts of a segment's priority score
Part Weight What it reads
Expiry 0.25 The segment's pass expiry
Reviewed flag 0.35 The highest human-reviewed open flag on the segment
Rain 0.25 Recent rain, in the JPS one-hour categories: light 1–10 mm, moderate 11–30 mm, heavy 31–60 mm, very heavy above 60 mm
Water level 0.15 The latest water level reading

Each part runs from 0 to 1, and the screen shows every part. A missing or stale input shows “unknown” and asks for review; it never scores zero. The weights are a product hypothesis, to tune with SDP.

A route the battery can fly.

The route grows one segment at a time, taking the best priority per watt-hour, until the battery says stop.

  • Each step adds the feasible task with the highest added priority per added watt-hour.
  • The drone always keeps a return path to its dock, with a battery reserve.
  • Geofences are hard constraints: no-fly areas, and homes and playgrounds as no-capture zones.
  • Segments left out are listed, each with its reason.

The battery model in the simulation is a stated assumption until an aircraft is chosen.

When the plan changes

The planner re-plans the part of the route not yet flown, and re-orders only uncommitted stops, when:

  • a JPS heavy-rain reading or a data.gov.my weather warning comes in;
  • a manager confirms a new flag;
  • a pass comes within 30 days of expiry;
  • someone reports damage;
  • a repair is completed, which queues a re-test patrol.

Fly when the park is empty.

Every plan fits an agreed flight window, and holds when the weather or the airspace says no.

Flight windows and holds
Window or hold What the plan does
First light The default window, while the park is empty.
Next dry window No launch above the dock's 2 mm/h rain limit. After heavy rain, the next dry morning comes first.
Lightning hold No launch during a lightning warning.
Altitude cap The plan carries the altitude cap that air traffic control sets.

Subang averaged 211.8 rain days a year from 2020 to 2024 and logged 289 lightning days in 2024, so patrols fly the next dry window, usually the next morning.

50 m from people. A pilot who can always intervene.

Flights are automated, never fully autonomous. CAAM does not allow operations where the remote pilot cannot intervene.

A plan becomes a flight only after a user with the pilot role approves it. A patrol from an unapproved plan cannot start: the API refuses it and names the rule, plan_not_approved.

The whole flight keeps the distances of CAR 2016 regulation 143:

Separation distances the planner keeps
Distance From When
50 m People, vehicles and structures not under the pilot's control At camera stations, along flight lines and on transits
30 m People During the climb from the dock and the descent onto it
150 m An open-air assembly of more than 1,000 persons The whole flight

A camera station with people near it waits for a clear pass or moves to a longer zoom stand-off. A stop whose transit passes people waits for them, and the dock holds take-off while its climb or transits are not clear. The pilot or observer confirms the path is clear.

Missions export, marked not flight-ready.

A planned route exports as a DJI WPML KMZ and a QGroundControl .plan, both stamped “not flight-ready”.

Mission export formats
Format Files For
DJI WPML KMZ wpmz/template.kml, wpmz/waylines.wpml DJI Pilot 2
QGroundControl plan .plan JSON QGroundControl

Every file says NOT FLIGHT-READY: a format sample generated from a Safe Edge patrol plan, not validated on any aircraft or ground station. Neither format holds a time window or a hold, so the notice carries the plan's window and the places that wait for a person to confirm the path clear. A remote pilot reviews and approves every live flight.

Shell, from the repository 
# Plan a demo patrol over the real Elmina water edges; write the KMZ and the .plan
bun run export:mission ./missions

# Open the .plan with MAVSDK's QGroundControl importer, as CI does
bun run check:qgc-plan

No drone? The phone patrol.

The protocol, not the aircraft, is the product. Where a drone cannot fly, the walk crew captures the same fixed viewpoints on a phone.

A phone screen walks the crew to each fixed viewpoint, ghosts the baseline photo over the camera, and sends the new photo into Edge Watch exactly like a drone image. The Inbox, Fix Lens and the proof stay the same. It runs today with no aviation approval, so a pilot can start before the first flight.

The proposed thresholds to stop or scale back the drone layer: false flags stay above 10 per patrol by week 4, less than 80% of the planned edge length is inspected, or an approval is refused. The phone patrol then carries the loop.

Elmina sits on the Subang approach.

A mapped point in Elmina Central Park is about 6.5 km from Subang airport, inside its Class C control zone.

In August 2023 an aircraft crashed in Elmina two minutes before its scheduled landing at Subang. Expect altitude caps, per-flight calls to air traffic control and refused windows. None of CAAM's published dock approvals is in a residential control zone.

Docks already fly in Malaysia: CAAM has published dock-based BVLOS approvals at a Rawang warehouse (2025) and at Bintulu Port and Samalaju (2026). Each is site-specific, so Elmina needs its own case.

The core stays vendor-neutral: the same core drives a simulator, a DJI dock through the Cloud API or an open autopilot through MAVLink. See the provider adapters.
