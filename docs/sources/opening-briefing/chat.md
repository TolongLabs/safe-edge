## Answers

Answers to the questions above, synthesized from every file in `docs/sources/`: the [transcript](transcript.md) (cited by time), the [slides](slides.md) (cited by number), and the official [Township of Tomorrow page](/docs/sources/tot.kolaxus.net/township-of-tomorrow.md) and [terms and conditions](/docs/sources/tot.kolaxus.net/terms-and-conditions.md) ("T&C"). The organisers said questions not answered live would be answered on Telegram.

- **Strictly a PPT, a demo video and code?** ([sender not visible]) Four components are due by 11:59 PM, 23 October 2026: a pitch deck of at most 10 main slides plus an appendix, a 5-minute video or live demo link, and a link to the prototype or code are required, and supporting documents are optional (slide 23). The prototype needn't be software; physical builds and storyboards also count (48:58 to 49:22).
- **Must a physical prototype be handed in, or are video or photos enough?** (Cheney Law Yih Xin) Show it through the required video or demo link (slide 23). If the team reaches the top 10, the organisers will arrange how to bring the physical item (Dr Lam, 58:38).
- **A certificate even if not in the top 10?** (Wan Alya Adriana) Yes. Every participant gets a soft-copy certificate, whether or not they win (Dina, 55:16).
- **Is this a one-off, or will there be future editions?** (Claudell Lema Chang Shiah Lin) No source says.
- **Is there a page limit for the proposal document?** (Chit Ko Ko) The only limit given is for the pitch deck: at most 10 main slides, with supporting details in an appendix (slide 23; Dina, 28:46). No source limits supporting documents, but Dr Lam warned against submitting a 50-page piece of existing university research (54:11).
- **Missed the email; will the challenge details be resent?** (sarah grace) The slides, recording and materials will be shared on the Telegram channel, <https://t.me/+cJZO7xI1KJM4NmVl> (Dina, 29:08; Siva, 46:05; slide 35). The challenge itself is on slides 13 to 18 and the official page.
- **Will the recording be shared on Telegram?** (ang zi chen) Yes, with the other materials (Siva, 46:05).
- **If our product doesn't win, does Sime Darby Property own it?** (Koa CC, following Shakirah) Yes, every submission becomes the organiser's intellectual property on submission, whether it wins or not, and the organiser may use, modify and commercialise it (Siva, 50:46; slide 33). Siva said Sime Darby Property owns it; the T&C name the organiser as IKolaxus Sdn Bhd and assign it all worldwide IP rights, perpetually and royalty-free, with moral rights waived (T&C sections 1 and 7).
- **Is a solution new to Sime Darby Property but existing elsewhere acceptable?** (Muhammad Danesh Hakimi Lou) The speakers differ. Dr Lam: new in Malaysia is enough, even if it exists in other countries, as is a known idea from a new angle (23:02). Dina: it shouldn't already be implemented elsewhere, and adding AI to an existing app isn't innovation (31:04). Judges ask for "genuine originality or a differentiated approach rather than simply repackaging an existing solution", worth 20% of the score (slide 26).
- **Does a drainage problem count as a township problem?** (Khor Hau Yang) Not answered. The Planet theme includes Water Conservation ("Protecting and managing water resources") and Climate Resilience ("Adapting to climate change impacts") (slide 17), and an idea must be within Sime Darby Property's reach (Dr Lam, 22:14).
- **Is it okay to pitch a product we've already built?** (Amir Redzuan) The sources point to no. Submissions must be original and "created specifically for this Challenge" (T&C section 4), and Dr Lam said to start fresh rather than submit existing work such as university research (54:11).
- **Which programming languages are permitted?** (Muhammad Ariq bin Mohamad Tajri) No source restricts languages. A prototype can be a mobile or web app, an AI assistant, an IoT concept, a dashboard or a physical model, among others (official page, "What should my prototype look like?"), and vibe-coded prototypes are fine (Siva, 48:58).
- **Can a member who can't attend the physical finals stay, and can we change members after registering? Whom do we email?** (Chua Pei Ying) Every member of a top 10 team must attend both physical days, 14 November and 3 December; if anyone can't, the organisers "will have to choose someone else" (Siva, 54:53; slide 34). To change members, re-register; no email address was given (Siva [?], 59:10). Each student registers individually on Kolaxus, the team leader bundles members into one submission, and each member must accept the team invitation (official page, "How does the participation mechanism work?"). Registration closes on 21 October (slide 22).
- **Is low model accuracy from too little data acceptable in a data science project?** (Adib) Not answered directly. The data and the problem must be real, not synthetic (Dina, 55:40; Dr Lam, 56:00), judges score measurable impact and evidence (slide 26), and a prototype "doesn't have to be perfect" (Dina, 50:07).
- **So a coded prototype isn't compulsory?** (Nur Ateqah) Correct, per the briefing: cardboard, a model, a storyboard or software all count (49:22; Dr Lam, 57:38), though some prototype is required (slide 23). The official page is stricter: each team needs at least one teammate who can code "so you can build and demo a working prototype" (official page, "Do I need a team?").
