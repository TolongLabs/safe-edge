# Handover Loop

"We help new City of Elmina homeowners and their renovators get rid of renovation debris legally, cheaply and sorted for recycling, in the first year after handover." That is when the debris is heaviest and dumping is easiest.

## Problem

- **Renovating at handover is normal.** A UiTM paper on terraced houses in Section 7, Shah Alam, describes "the adhered normal practice to renovate or modify houses even as early as when the owner is given vacant possession" ([Sazally et al.][uitm]).
- **Renovation is what gets dumped.**
  - In a UTHM and SWCorp interview study, "most of the respondents mentioned that illegal dumping activities are from the renovation works" ([IOP Conference Series][uthm]).
  - In a 2014 written reply, the Selangor government listed contractors and individuals renovating premises first among the causes of illegal dumpsites. It named Persiaran Elmina among Kuala Selangor council's dumpsites. Among the councils' strategies, it listed requiring housing developers to appoint council-registered contractors for disposal ([Selangor State Assembly][dun dumping]).
  - Klang's council runs 507 CCTV cameras at dumping hotspots ([CONTEXT]).
- **The legal route is clumsy:**
  - MBSA doesn't collect construction materials ([Komuniti City of Elmina][elmina waste], community site).
  - MBSA's small 3-tonne debris bin costs "RM 290.00/ Sekali Tarik" (per pull). The application needs "Kelulusan Pelan Tambahan pindaan OSC", an approved extension plan ([MBSA][mbsa roro]). Our read: small or unapproved jobs fall outside it.
- **New estates elsewhere have the same problem.** In June 2026, AsiaOne reported Tampines Town Council in Singapore as saying the situation was "a common challenge for newly completed BTO developments that undergo extensive home renovation works" ([AsiaOne][tampines], _secondary_).

## Target user

- **Primary:** owners of newly handed-over landed homes in one Elmina phase, and the renovation contractors they hire.
- **Secondary:** SDP's township team, who keep the phase clean until it is handed to the council.

## Solution and how it works

1.  **Forecast.** SDP's key-handover bookings in the SDP App show which units, of which house type, change hands each week. From these, Handover Loop forecasts the weekly debris wave for each street.
2.  **Drop.** For the first 12 months after vacant possession, SDP runs a sorted drop point on a vacant lot inside the phase. Renovators book a slot against the unit, drop concrete and tile, timber, metal and packaging into separate bays, and each load is weighed and logged to the unit. It should cost less than an RM290 MBSA bin.
3.  **Recycle.** Inert debris goes to a construction and demolition recovery facility that sells aggregates ([Worldwide Holdings][worldwide]); cardboard goes to recyclers.
4.  **Recognise.** Renovators who log every load join a "clean renovator" list that SDP can show buyers in the SDP App. Residents see their own unit's diversion record.

A compulsory deposit is probably out. Regulation 11(2) of the housing regulations bars collecting "any payment by whatever name called except as prescribed by the contract of sale" ([Johor Bar commentary][hdr]). We haven't checked how it applies to a debris deposit after handover. Either way, Handover Loop runs on price and convenience, not penalties.

## Why it's new

| Closest analogue                                                                                                        | What it does                                                        | What it lacks                                                                                                                       |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Shanghai's decoration-waste rules ([Shanghai government][shanghai])                                                     | Property managers must provide stacking sites, and producers pay    | No forecast and no per-unit weighing; sorting at source is only encouraged                                                          |
| Hong Kong Housing Authority ([Hong Kong government][hk ha])                                                             | Debris removal charge and a designated dumping point at new estates | A fee by flat size, with an opt-out, and a fixed point                                                                              |
| Hong Kong's trip-ticket system ([EPD][trip ticket]); SWCorp's construction-waste system ([Seremban City Council][cwms]) | Record construction-waste trips by truck or licensed collector      | Track trucks or collectors, not homes. SWCorp's system sits under the national waste law, which Selangor hasn't adopted ([CONTEXT]) |
| South Korea's "minus option" ([Edaily][minus], _secondary_)                                                             | Buyers drop finishes they will replace                              | 1 of 507 homes took it at the first complex in 2008, and about 12% at one complex in 2016; no debris handling                       |

- **New in Malaysia:** unclear, leaning yes. No Malaysian developer runs a handover-timed, weighed drop point.
- **New globally:** no. Shanghai mandates the core.
- **Our read:** what's new is forecasting the wave from handover bookings and recording diversion per unit.

## Impact and how we measure it

- **Primary outcome:** illegal debris piles found on monthly walks of the pilot phase, against a comparison phase handed over at about the same time.
- **Secondary:** tonnes of renovation debris diverted per unit; the share of renovating units using the drop point.
- **Baseline:** walk one recently handed-over phase now and photograph and count the piles. No Malaysian figure for renovation waste per home was found, so the pilot's weigh logs would be the first.

## Fit with Sime Darby Property

- **Pillar:** waste, where SDP aspires to zero waste to landfill ([CONTEXT]).
  - **Caveat:** SDP reports waste from construction sites and operational assets only ([SR2025] p. 76). Residents' renovation waste isn't in its numbers, so pitch this as a new number SDP could start reporting.
- **SDP's own process:** it uses the key-handover bookings SDP already runs. It also builds on the Pre-Handover Vacant Possession initiative, which trains HQ staff in quality inspection before handover ([SR2025] p. 113).
- **Value to two sides:**
  - Residents pay less to dispose of debris and live in a cleaner street.
  - SDP keeps phases it is still responsible for clean ([Selangor State Assembly][dun dev]) and protects its brand.
  - Recyclers get sorted inert feedstock.
- **Scale:** every new landed phase SDP hands over.

## Prototype by 23 October

- **Build:**
  - A forecast view: units by house type and handover week, giving the expected debris per street per week.
  - A booking and weigh-log flow for renovators.
  - A phase dashboard showing tonnes diverted and dumping reports.
- **Data:**
  - SDP's published unit counts, house types and completion dates per phase, from its press releases and brochures.
  - MBSA's bin price.
  - Recycler locations.
  - **Caveat:** handover bookings aren't public. Model the weekly split from the real published unit counts and completion dates, label it as modelled, and ask SDP for the real schedule. The rules ask for real data ([brief]).
- **Evidence before submission:**
  - A photo count of debris piles in one recently handed-over Elmina phase.
  - Interviews with five homeowners and three renovation contractors about how they disposed of debris last time, and what it cost.

- **Biggest weakness:**
  - Nothing makes contractors use the drop point after handover: inert disposal is cheap, and private skip operators likely compete (our read).
  - The forecast runs on simulated bookings.
  - Renovation waste isn't a number SDP reports.
- **The change that would raise it most:**
  - Interview contractors and homeowners at The Nine about what they paid and where their debris went.
  - Get MBSA's position on a drop point.
  - Ask SDP for the real handover schedule.

## Risks and open questions

- **The killer question:** "After handover on individual title we have no contract hook, and inert disposal is cheap. Why would contractors use our drop point?" The answer has to be price and convenience compared with MBSA's bin and private skip and lorry operators, proved in contractor interviews.
- **Unknown:** how much SDP spends clearing dumped debris from phases it still maintains.
- **Unknown:** whether MBSA permits a temporary sorted drop point on private land.

## How the idea changed across rounds

- **Round 1:** seeded as a waste forecast from handover bookings, plus a reuse exchange for near-new fittings between identical house types.
- **Round 4:** research found no taker for the reuse half: every neighbour with the same house type already owns the same cabinet (our read). The reuse half was dropped, and so was the fit-out opt-out, after South Korea's weak early uptake.
- **Round 6:** rebuilt as a weighed drop point. Research then found that Shanghai mandates such points, that a compulsory deposit is likely unlawful, and that Kuala Selangor's council already sells standard extension plans at RM100 each ([MPKS][mpks]).
- **Round 8:** dropped the deposit and the extension designs; it now runs on price and convenience.
- **Round 9:** judges scored it 56 to 63. All three asked why contractors would use it.
