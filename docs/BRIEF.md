## The challenge

> How might we reimagine the Township of Tomorrow where people, businesses and nature thrive together through innovation, technology and sustainable living?

Choose **one** focus area (slide 14) and submit to one track only (official page, "Can we compete in multiple tracks?"):

| People                                                                                   | Planet                                                                                  |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Enable every resident to live healthier, safer, more connected and more fulfilling lives | Create townships that regenerate the environment and make sustainable living effortless |
| Community wellbeing                                                                      | Waste management                                                                        |
| Safety and security                                                                      | Circular economy                                                                        |
| Accessibility and inclusion                                                              | Energy efficiency                                                                       |
| Mobility                                                                                 | Water conservation                                                                      |
| Digital living                                                                           | Green transport                                                                         |
| Neighbourhood connection                                                                 | Biodiversity                                                                            |
| Healthy lifestyles                                                                       | Climate resilience                                                                      |

Sources: slides 15 and 17.

- **Solve one meaningful problem well.** "If you try to solve everything, you solve nothing" (Dr Lam, 16:00; slide 15). Planet ideas should change behaviour, systems or outcomes, not just raise awareness (slide 17).
- **Frame it as one sentence:** "We want to help _[group]_ do _[thing]_ better", or "I want to reduce _[problem]_, and this is how we check it improved" (Dr Lam, 15:12).
- **Bridging both areas is welcome,** but you still choose one (Dr Lam, 53:10).
- **The slide examples are taken:** pre-booked parking, one reporting channel, secure parcel collection (slide 16), recycling guide, solar opportunity mapper, material comparison AI agent and waste-derived material prototype (slide 18). "If I've already put it in here, that means I know enough about them" (Dr Lam, 18:49).
- **A township** is a planned area of homes, offices, shops, roads, parks, schools and hospitals; a university campus is a close analogue to study (Dr Lam, 06:48).

### Fit Sime Darby Property's business

An idea must be within Sime Darby Property's reach: "We build homes, we build offices, we do shopping malls, we do a lot of things, but we don't do everything" (Dr Lam, 22:14). Ask who benefits: homeowners, investors, or visitors to its assets (Dr Lam, 06:17).

- **Property development** is 93% of revenue: 28 townships in Malaysia, such as City of Elmina and Bandar Bukit Raja, and 2 overseas developments, Battersea Power Station in London and one in Melbourne (slides 7, 8; Dr Lam, 07:27).
- **Investment and fund management** earns recurring income from investment properties, concessions and private funds (slide 7).
- **Asset management** runs KL East Mall, Elmina Lakeside Mall, KLGCC Mall, a hyperscale data centre, commercial buildings and a convention centre (slide 7) and golf courses (Dr Lam, 05:37).
- **Sustainability strategy 2025 to 2030** has four pillars: energy and low carbon, urban biodiversity, climate resilience and community experience. It targets a 40% cut in carbon emissions by 2030 and net zero by 2050 (slide 9; Dr Lam, 08:05). The 15% strategic fit criterion asks for "an SDP township priority" (slide 26).
- **Recent firsts:** the first developer to work with Google on a data centre in its township, with two hyperscale data centres built, and the nation's first residential rooftop solar programme, in Elmina (slide 6; Dr Lam, 04:41).

## Eligibility and teams

- **Who:** students of recognised Malaysian universities or colleges (T&C section 2), undergraduate or postgraduate, any age (Dr Lam, 53:48). Under-18s need written parental consent (T&C section 2). Non-Malaysians based in Malaysia on a PR, work or student visa can join (official page). It is free (official page).
- **Team:** 3 to 5 students with a team leader (official page, "Do I need a team?"; slide 36). At least one must code "so you can build and demo a working prototype" (official page).
- **Registration:** each student registers individually on Kolaxus. The leader bundles the members into one submission, selects one track and submits; each member must accept the team invitation first (official page, "How does the participation mechanism work?").
- **Changing members:** re-register (Siva, 59:10).
- **One idea, one track:** submitting the same idea to several tracks gets you asked to choose, and repeat offences may disqualify you (official page).

Before submitting, test every link, check every file and have someone outside the team review it. "Don't wait until 11.58 PM" (Dina, 28:02).

### Pitch deck

Cover 8 areas and carry one clear problem through every slide (slides 24, 25). Slide 25 walks the parking example through all 8.

| The story                                     | The proof                                                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1. Problem, found through background research | 5. How it works                                            |
| 2. Target user: specific, not "everyone"      | 6. Impact, and how it is measured (for example time saved) |
| 3. Solution: for whom, and the outcome        | 7. Feasibility: implementable within one or two years      |
| 4. Innovation: not "an existing app plus AI"  | 8. Implementation: where and how to pilot, what to measure |

Sources: slide 24; Dina, 29:46 to 33:01.

The official page frames the pitch as two parts: the **mechanism** (the solution, how it works, who benefits, why it creates value) and the **enabler** (how technology enables it and supports implementation at township scale).

### Video and prototype

- **Video:** show the solution, not the team. Any number of members can appear, and testers can be outsiders (Dina, 51:18; Dr Lam, 51:53).
- **Prototype form:** software, a cardboard model, a physical build or a storyboard all count (Dina, 49:22; Dr Lam, 57:38). The official page lists apps, AI assistants, IoT concepts, dashboards, service prototypes and physical models, among others.
- **Effort:** it "doesn't have to be perfect" (Dina, 50:07). Vibe-coded prototypes are fine and raise your chances (Siva, 48:58). Don't spend much; simulations are fine (Siva, 56:54). "Fail fast, fail cheap" (Dr Lam, 58:04).
- **Physical prototypes:** show them in the video; if you reach the Top 10, the organisers will arrange the rest (Dr Lam, 58:38).

## How it is judged

| Weight | Criterion                           | Judges ask                                                                     |
| ------ | ----------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 25%    | Impact and sustainability value     | Meaningful, measurable outcomes for residents, community or environment?       |
| 20%    | Innovation and differentiation      | Genuine originality, rather than repackaging an existing solution?             |
| 20%    | Feasibility and deliverability      | Can it realistically be piloted and implemented in a township?                 |
| 15%    | Strategic fit and theme relevance   | Clearly People or Planet, and a Sime Darby Property township priority?         |
| 10%    | Commercial and value multiplication | Value for Sime Darby Property, township businesses or residents; can it scale? |
| 10%    | Team, evidence and pitch quality    | Problem researched and validated, solution credible, pitch clear?              |

Source: slide 26. A strong solution is relevant, impactful, innovative, feasible, scalable and evidence-based, and scores well on all six (slide 20). Dr Lam's gloss:

- **Impactful:** reaches as many people as possible, for as long as possible, and is commercially sound (22:43).
- **Feasible:** estimate the cost, effort and approvals, and check compliance and safety; a big pilot cost needs a big impact (23:22 to 23:43).
- **Scalable:** works in other townships, ideally other countries (23:55).
- **Evidence-based:** validated with end users, investors and the people affected, not just AI chat output (24:09 to 24:26).

Judging decisions are final and can't be appealed (T&C section 5). Don't email asking why you weren't selected (Siva, 44:20).

## Rules

- **IP:** every submission becomes the organiser's exclusive property on submission, **win or lose**. The assignment is worldwide, perpetual and royalty-free; the organiser may modify, commercialise and share it with sponsors without compensation, and you waive moral rights (T&C section 7; slide 33; Siva, 50:46).
- **Media:** photos, videos and recordings may be used for promotion without further consent or payment (T&C section 8; Siva, 44:49).
- **Originality:** original, lawful, and created specifically for this challenge (T&C section 4). No existing products or university research; start fresh (Dr Lam, 54:11).
- **No plagiarism:** credit external sources and technologies (slide 33). Don't paste the problem statement into ChatGPT or Claude and submit the output (Siva, 43:33).
- **Real data:** use real problems and real data, not synthetic data (Dina, 55:40; Dr Lam, 56:00).
- **Accuracy and confidentiality:** false or misleading information disqualifies you (T&C section 3). Don't disclose confidential sponsor or challenge information (T&C section 11).
- **Conduct:** act professionally and respect mentors, organisers and partners (slide 33; Siva, 43:51). Breaches, plagiarism or misrepresentation can disqualify you (T&C section 14).
- **Personal data:** used for registration, judging, communications and event management (slide 33). Kolaxus's privacy policy also lists direct marketing among its purposes (privacy policy section 2).
- **Certificates:** every participant gets a soft-copy certificate, win or lose (Dina, 55:16).
- **Changes:** the organiser may amend the T&C with notice on official platforms (T&C section 17).

## Advice from the organisers

- **Start from an everyday annoyance** on your daily route from home to work, study, food and back (Dr Lam, 00:49, 13:02). Identifying the problem is "50 percent of the hackathon" (14:05).
- **Find the root cause and talk to people:** friends, family, residents, people on campus. Be willing to change your idea when you do (Dr Lam, 16:20 to 16:49, 21:10 to 21:27). Follow observe, identify, understand, create, build (slide 19).
- **Aim past the obvious:** "If you Google, if you check GPT … your solution is not there. That is the solution that we're looking for" (Dr Lam, 19:01).
- **Measure the improvement:** what improved because of your idea? If it reduces waste, which waste, how is it measured, and compared with what (Dr Lam, 08:26)?
- **Keep the problem simple** and close to the customer's heart (Dina, 35:31). Don't worry about funding (Dr Lam, 20:13).
