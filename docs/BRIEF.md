## The challenge

> How might we reimagine the Township of Tomorrow where people, businesses and nature thrive together through innovation, technology and sustainable living?

Choose **one** focus area:

| People                                                                              | Planet                                                                                  |
| ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Help every resident live healthier, safer, more connected and more fulfilling lives | Create townships that regenerate the environment and make sustainable living effortless |
| Community wellbeing                                                                 | Waste management                                                                        |
| Safety and security                                                                 | Circular economy                                                                        |
| Accessibility and inclusion                                                         | Energy efficiency                                                                       |
| Mobility                                                                            | Water conservation                                                                      |
| Digital living                                                                      | Green transport                                                                         |
| Neighbourhood connection                                                            | Biodiversity                                                                            |
| Healthy lifestyles                                                                  | Climate resilience                                                                      |

- Solve **one** meaningful problem well. "If you try to solve everything, you solve nothing."
- A solution may touch both areas, but the submission must still pick one.
- Don't reuse the slide examples as your idea: parking app, one reporting channel, parcel collection, recycling guide, solar mapper, material comparison agent, waste-derived materials. Dr Lam: "If I've already put it in here, that means I know enough about them."
- Frame it as: "We want to help _[group]_ do _[thing]_ better", or "I want to reduce _[problem]_, and this is how we check it improved."
- Sime Darby Property builds townships, homes, offices, malls, industrial parks and data centres, and runs malls, golf courses and funds. Ideas must be within reach of that business. Think about who benefits: homeowners, investors or visitors.

## What to submit

Due **11:59 PM, Friday 23 October 2026**, via the submission link (QR code on slide 23). Incomplete submissions are not considered.

| Component            | Required? | Rules                                                                                                               |
| -------------------- | --------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Pitch deck           | Required  | Maximum 10 main slides; an appendix is allowed                                                                      |
| Video or demo link   | Required  | A 5-minute video or a live demo link. Show the solution, not the team. Testers can be outsiders                     |
| Prototype or code    | Required  | Link to the prototype, codebase or GitHub repository. A physical model, cardboard mock-up or storyboard also counts |
| Supporting documents | Optional  | Evidence: interviews, data, research                                                                                |

The deck should cover 8 areas:

| The story                                     | The proof                                  |
| --------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------ |
| 1. Problem                                    | 5. How it works                            |
| 2. Target user (specific, not "everyone")     | 6. Impact (how you measure it)             |
| 3. Solution                                   | 7. Feasibility (cost, approvals, safety)   |
| 4. Innovation (not "an existing app plus AI") | 8. Implementation (a realistic pilot plan) |

Before submitting, test every link, check every file, and have someone outside the team review it. Don't leave it to 11:58 PM.

## How it is judged

| Weight | Criterion                           | Judges ask                                                                         |
| ------ | ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 25%    | Impact and sustainability value     | Meaningful, measurable outcomes for residents, community or environment?           |
| 20%    | Innovation and differentiation      | Genuinely original, not a repackaged solution?                                     |
| 20%    | Feasibility and deliverability      | Can it realistically be piloted in a township?                                     |
| 15%    | Strategic fit and theme relevance   | Clearly People or Planet, and a Sime Darby Property priority?                      |
| 10%    | Commercial and value multiplication | Value for Sime Darby Property, township businesses or residents, and can it scale? |
| 10%    | Team, evidence and pitch quality    | Problem validated, solution credible, pitch clear?                                 |

A strong solution is **relevant, impactful, innovative, feasible, scalable and evidence-based**, and scores well on all six, not one or two.

## Rules

- **Team:** 3 to 5 students. Any student qualifies: undergraduate or postgraduate, any age.
- **IP:** every submission becomes Sime Darby Property's intellectual property, **even if you don't win**. The organiser can use, modify and commercialise it. Photos and videos taken during the event also belong to them.
- **Originality:** no plagiarism. Credit external sources and technologies. Don't paste ChatGPT or Claude output, and don't submit existing university research; start fresh.
- **Data:** use real data and real problems. No synthetic data or invented problems.
- **Finals attendance:** every member must attend both physical days (14 Nov and 3 Dec), or the team swaps that member out.
- **Costs:** students pay their own flights, transport, accommodation and meals. Physical prototypes aren't expected to cost much: simulations, cardboard and storyboards are fine.
- **Team changes:** re-register.
- **Selection:** decisions are final; don't email asking why you weren't picked.
- **Conduct:** professional; respect mentors, organisers and partners.
- **Certificates:** every participant gets a soft-copy certificate.

## Advice from the organisers

- Start from an everyday annoyance in your own life. "Identifying the problem is 50% of the hackathon."
- Interview real users (residents, people on campus) and be willing to change your idea.
- If ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude can already give the answer, it isn't the solution they want.
- Innovative means new in Malaysia; it doesn't have to be new everywhere (Dr Lam, but see [conflicts](#conflicts-and-open-questions)).
- Feasible means implementable within one to two years, and the cost must be matched by the impact.
- A prototype raises your chances; vibe-coded prototypes are fine. "Fail fast, fail cheap."
- Funding isn't your problem at this stage; good ideas get subject matter experts to work with you.
