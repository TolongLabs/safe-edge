## Summary (5 bullets max)

- On 16 October 2026, a 30-year-old man was found dead at Tasik Bukit Raja after he was reported missing while out fishing. Utusan says the body was seen at 12:45 pm; The Star says it was found at about 1:15 pm. [1] [2]
- The cited sources do not establish the exact lake parcel's owner, operator, area, lighting, fence, signage, or fishing rules. MBDK's 2024 award covers retention ponds and recreation fields at Jalan Makyong and Jalan Canggung; its 2026 Makyong award covers landscaping and recreation fields. Neither names the fatality site as the contracted pond. [3] [4]
- Analyst's read: Bandar Bukit Raja Wetland Townpark is the best documented public pilot candidate in this pack. The Star's April 2026 StarPicks article reports that its 60-acre park is fully open to the public and lit with solar-powered lighting. SDP's 2025 report describes a wider 180-acre wetland system and says it can retain over 50,000 m³ in extreme rainfall; the linked PDF was not directly accessible, so those report details are snippet-only. No cited source provides a current water-edge safety audit. [7] [14]
- The Elmina constructed-wetlands report gives safety recommendations, including warning signs, planted edge barriers, and life rings in deeper water. It does not show that these measures were installed. [8]
- Analyst's read: Tasik Bukit Raja's October 2026 fatality is the only confirmed water-edge incident in this source pack. The other sites' incident histories remain unknown; the sources do not establish that they are incident-free. [1] [2]

### Reported drowning and earlier incidents

Utusan reported that a 30-year-old man was reported missing after going out fishing at Tasik Bukit Raja. The fire and rescue service received an emergency call at 10:37 pm on 15 October 2026. The man's motorcycle, fishing gear, and slippers were found by the lakeside. [1]

The search involved personnel from Balai Bomba dan Penyelamat Klang Utara and water-rescue teams from Port Klang, KLIA, and Shah Alam. Utusan reports 15 fire officers and personnel, supported by 16 police personnel and four Angkatan Pertahanan Awam Malaysia members. It says an aluminium boat and equipment identified only as “HDPA” were used; the article does not expand the acronym. [1]

Utusan says the body was seen floating at 12:45 pm on 16 October, about 300 metres from the fire service control post. It says the body was brought ashore at 1:00 pm, identified by next of kin, and handed to police. The Star says the body was found at about 1:15 pm, nearly 300 metres from the operations post, then identified and handed to police. The accounts therefore differ by about 30 minutes on discovery time. [1] [2]

Earlier incidents: The cited sources do not provide a historical incident register for this lake. The previous claim that searches found no earlier drowning is removed; the site's earlier incident history is unknown.

## Resident voice

- In 2019 posts on a Lowyat thread opened in 2018, a buyer questioned whether the planned Elmina lake garden would be maintained; another poster said MBSA would maintain it. These are forum comments, not a verified handover record or current council statement. [18]
- A Reddit discussion about buying at Elmina mentions parks and cycling amenities, but does not discuss lake safety. The accessible page labels the post “4y ago”; an exact publication date is not exposed there. [19]
- The University of Malaya's 2023 Sustainability Report says UM Water Warriors and the Elmina Gardens Residents' Association formalized the “Friends of Elmina Lake” partnership at a July 2023 Open Day. Activities included a lake cleanup and water-quality awareness. This documents community stewardship, not a safety problem or current operator. The linked PDF was too large to open directly, so this detail is snippet-only. [20]
- A user-submitted FishAngler page records a catch at Bandar Bukit Raja Lake on 20 May 2023. This supports reported fishing use, not legal permission. [22]
- Taman Subang Ria visitor reviews include conflicting comments about fishing access, as noted above; they do not establish an official rule. [17]

## Who runs parks after handover

SDP's 2024 Sustainability Report says it maintains biodiversity value in green spaces before handover to local authorities and works with MBSA on park design and maintenance protocols. It does not publish a current operator list for each lake. [11]

SDP's 2024 Integrated Annual Report describes regular meetings with management bodies and residents' associations, plus dedicated township community liaisons. That describes engagement; it does not establish that those bodies maintain public lakes. [21]

The available examples show different arrangements rather than one portfolio-wide rule: a 21-year leaseback for Elmina Central Park is reported; MBDK procurement covers named scopes around Jalan Makyong; and the Elmina wetlands study recommended a management manual before municipal handover. These sources do not establish who currently operates every park. [3] [4] [8] [9] [10]

## Gaps

- For Tasik Bukit Raja, confirm the lot boundary, title holder, asset-register name, and whether any pond in MBDK's Makyong contract is the fatality location. Request current inspection, lighting, fencing, signage, rescue-equipment, and fishing-rule records from the asset owner and MBDK. [1] [3] [4]
- For each candidate, confirm whether a reported area means total park, habitat area, wetland area, or actual water surface. These figures are not interchangeable. [6] [7] [8] [14] [15]
- Current public fishing rules, edge-protection inventories, park handover documents, and operator contacts are not established for most named sites. The Elmina wetlands recommendations date from the 2019–2020 assessment and cannot be treated as evidence of current installation. [8]
- Analyst's read: a pilot should begin with owner-approved site audits and incident/near-miss records. The Bukit Raja fatality makes that site salient, but its owner and operating boundary remain uncertain. The BBR Wetland Townpark has clearer public-access and lighting evidence, but no published lake-edge safety audit. [1] [7] [14]
