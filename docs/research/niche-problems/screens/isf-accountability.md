## One-line problem

Analyst's read: After a township asset moves between a developer, council and other agencies, residents often cannot see one reliable record linking the asset's owner, maintenance duty, reported fault, repair and close-out; ISF does not by itself promise that a contribution is spent back in the contributing township. [The Act](https://www.ppj.gov.my/storage/7476/133---STREET%2C-DRAINAGE-AND-BUILDING-ACT-1974.pdf) gives councils discretion over the fund, and Selangor's 2025 answer and South Klang reporting describe incomplete handovers and overlapping road owners. [Selangor Assembly](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/penyelenggaraan-infrastruktur/) · [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/metro/metro-news/2024/12/06/south-klang-residents-flooded-with-problems)

## Scale and 2024 to 2026 evidence (bullets with numbers and sources)

- **What ISF is:** Section 132 of the Street, Drainage and Building Act 1974 establishes an Improvement Service Fund in each local authority. It accepts statutory payments and contributions for beautifying, building or laying out streets, drains, culverts, gutters and watercourses. The council administers it “at its absolute discretion” and may pay expenses incurred in carrying out the Act. Analyst's read: Section 132 does not create a per-township spending guarantee. [Act 133, s.132](https://www.ppj.gov.my/storage/7476/133---STREET%2C-DRAINAGE-AND-BUILDING-ACT-1974.pdf)
- **Rates vary by council:** MBDK's published form charges RM250 per housing unit priced below RM100,000 and RM500 per unit priced above RM100,000; it lists drainage ISF at RM5,000 per development acre, chargeable only to the original developer. MBDK requires 100% payment before confirmation of planning permission. These are MBDK rates, not a national schedule; its form does not state the housing rate for a unit priced exactly RM100,000. [MBDK ISF form](https://www.mbdk.gov.my/sites/default/files/media/document/2024-03/9.%20SENARAI%20SEMAK%20IMPROVEMENT%20SERVICE%20FUND%20%28ISF%29.pdf)
- **Material fund balances, limited spend detail:** A Selangor Assembly table reports RM533,150,829.56 in the ISF column across the state's local authorities for January–October 2024. It lists MBPJ at RM140,210,759, MBSJ at RM104,268,748 and MBDK at RM37,681,864. The table is headed as local-authority cash holdings and developer contributions; it is not a project-by-project expenditure report. MPKS's 2023 financial statement table (snippet only) gives a fund-level example: RM16,771,576 opening balance, RM1,587,269 receipts, RM4,001,962 payments and RM14,356,883 closing balance. Not found: I searched National Audit Department/LKAN sources for “Kumpulan Wang Perkhidmatan Kemajuan”, “Improvement Service Fund” and local-authority trust funds for 2018–2026; no recent report specifically naming Selangor PBT ISF surfaced. The closest official result was a 2004–2005 review at Mersing and Tangkak, which found no evidence supervisors reviewed deposit/trust registers, though both councils prepared ISF cashbooks (outside this screen's period). [Selangor Assembly table](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Bertulis-No.-322-Lampiran-A.pdf) · [MPKS 2023 financial statement](https://www.mpks.gov.my/sites/default/files/2024-11/PENYATA%20KEWANGAN%202023.pdf) · [National Audit Department, older review](https://lkan.audit.gov.my/laporan/downloads/112?document=202)
- **Complaint volume is high, but not specific to facilities:** Selangor's Assembly reported 2023 and January–October 2024 total complaint counts of 27,771 and 28,097 for MBSA; 28,794 and 26,021 for MBSJ; and 23,251 and 20,385 for MBDK. These totals cover all complaint types, not just public-facility maintenance or unresolved cases. [Selangor Assembly, complaint totals](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/aduan/)
- **Residents describe concrete maintenance and ownership failures:** In December 2024, The Star reported that a residents' coalition representing about 10 South Klang neighbourhoods complained of clogged drains, flooding, potholes, neglected parks and slow maintenance. Residents said contractors sometimes cleaned drains or cut grass only once a month instead of every two weeks, and one underground retention pond had not been cleaned in over 10 years. The report notes roads in the same affected area were variously under MBDK, JKR or the developer; these are resident reports, not an audit finding. [The Star, 6 December 2024](https://www.thestar.com.my/metro/metro-news/2024/12/06/south-klang-residents-flooded-with-problems)

## Why now (a 2025 or 2026 trigger, if any)

Analyst's read: Selangor now has a live policy opening. A 2025 state answer addresses older housing areas where developers have disappeared and roads, drains and ponds remain unhanded-over; the answer says pre-handover upkeep is the developer's duty, while councils may check technical records and deposits, use available deposits for basic repairs, and direct a developer that still exists to repair defects. The State also says it is coordinating ownership of unowned roads and drains through inventory work and a road-asset system. The two Assembly pages loaded only in search results in this screen, so treat their detail as **snippet only**. [Selangor Assembly, unhanded-over infrastructure](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/penyelenggaraan-infrastruktur/) · [Selangor Assembly, road ownership coordination](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/penyelarasan-dan-pemilikan-jalan-di-bawah-pelbagai-agensi-kerajaan/)

The Sun reported in September 2025 that the State was mapping roads, slopes, retention ponds and bridges without a clear agency owner, after cataloguing over 500 kilometres of unowned drains using a one-off RM5 million allocation. Separately, the 2026 Selangor Budget provides RM5 million for maintenance of retention ponds under JPS Selangor's control. [The Sun, 4 September 2025](https://thesun.my/news/malaysia-news/selangor-maps-unowned-infrastructure-to-tackle-flooding-and-maintenance-ko14830857/) · [Selangor Budget 2026](https://www.selangor.gov.my/selangor/modules_resources/bookshelf/Teks_Ucapan_Belanjawan_Selangor_2026/Teks_Ucapan_Belanjawan_Selangor_2026.pdf)

## Who suffers and who owns the fix

- Residents and residents' associations bear the safety, flood, access and amenity costs. In the South Klang account, volunteers and residents helped clear fallen trees while waiting for agencies. [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/metro/metro-news/2024/12/06/south-klang-residents-flooded-with-problems)
- Before formal surrender, Selangor says the developer is responsible for infrastructure and public amenities. After transfer, the owner can be a local authority, JKR, JPS or another agency; the correct owner depends on the asset and its handover record. In one Selangor report, some drains discharging to rivers fall to JPS, while urban drainage systems fall to local authorities. [Selangor Assembly, snippet only](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/penyelenggaraan-infrastruktur/) · [The Sun](https://thesun.my/news/malaysia-news/selangor-maps-unowned-infrastructure-to-tackle-flooding-and-maintenance-ko14830857/)
- Analyst's read: The council owns the ISF governance and records; the State coordinates inter-agency gaps; the developer owns construction quality, defects and complete handover evidence for assets it has not yet surrendered. Residents' associations can report and verify service, but should not have to infer legal ownership from a complaint form. [Act 133, s.132](https://www.ppj.gov.my/storage/7476/133---STREET%2C-DRAINAGE-AND-BUILDING-ACT-1974.pdf) · [Selangor Assembly, snippet only](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/penyelenggaraan-infrastruktur/)

## SDP's lever (what SDP controls: before handover, defect period, township management, its own assets)

Analyst's read: SDP can control the quality of its own asset inventory, as-built records, pre-handover inspections, defect tracking, service records and handover pack; it can operate and maintain assets it retains and agree a shared close-out process with the receiving authority. SDP's 2025 Sustainability Report describes 27 active townships, including City of Elmina, Bandar Bukit Raja and Serenia City. SDP has a real pilot surface and can make the handover chain auditable, but it cannot direct council ISF allocations or guarantee a council's repair response. [SDP Sustainability Report 2025](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/SDP%20SR2025%20%28Mobile%29.pdf) · [Act 133, s.132](https://www.ppj.gov.my/storage/7476/133---STREET%2C-DRAINAGE-AND-BUILDING-ACT-1974.pdf)

## What already exists (products, services, programmes, hackathon projects)

- **Complaint intake:** SISPAA accepts reports about inadequate public amenities and service failures; MBPJ's guide shows ticket submission and status lookup. Selangor's portal also lists SPAB, KDEB's iClean for waste/cleaning complaints and Waze for potholes. Analyst's read: These route or track complaints; they do not, by themselves, publish asset ownership, handover status or ISF spending. [Malaysia.gov SISPAA](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/my/feedback/sistem-pengurusan-aduan-bersepadu-sispaa) · [MBPJ SISPAA guide](https://www.mbpj.gov.my/sites/default/files/tatacara_sispaa_v2_pdf.pdf) · [Selangor complaint channels](https://www.selangor.gov.my/index.php/pages/view/134)
- **Road issue routing:** KKR's MyJalan app accepts location/photo reports for roads, streetlights and public facilities and lets users check complaint status; the app is integrated with SISPAA. Analyst's read: This makes a generic “report a pothole” app a crowded direction. [Malaysia.gov, MyJalan](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/topics/myjalan-kkr-application)
- **GIS, budgets and participation:** MBSA runs the iMapSA GIS portal. MBPJ exposes a public ArcGIS REST FeatureServer with building-condition, building-inventory and defect-table layers, but the inspected layer list did not show a drain/park/road-ownership or ISF-spending layer. MBPJ's 2025 budget used an outcome-based approach and a community town hall; Shah Alam's 2021 Voluntary Local Review describes participatory budgeting. Analyst's read: These are useful participation/data precedents, not an asset-level maintenance ledger. [iMapSA](https://imapsa.mbsa.gov.my/) · [MBPJ FeatureServer](https://services3.arcgis.com/Pm8D5pANQ4gpdLsD/ArcGIS/rest/services/MBPJ/FeatureServer) · [MBPJ budget town hall](https://www.mbpj.gov.my/sites/default/files/edisi_jun_2025.pdf) · [Shah Alam VLR](https://sdgs.un.org/sites/default/files/2021-07/VLR%20SDGs%20Shah%20Alam.pdf)
- **Products and student work:** MYHackathon's 2025 ServeIT Smart City pilot at Majlis Daerah Tanjong Malim digitises waste and streetlight services; FieldEx and Fixello sell maintenance/work-order tools for strata JMBs and MCs. APU's 2022 report describes the student hackathon app Kommunity for housing-community management and safety, while a UTHM paper describes SparkTrack for campus restroom cleanliness. Not found: a direct ISF spending or public-asset handover tracker in searches of MYHackathon, KitaHack, Devpost and university-project results. [MD Tanjong Malim / ServeIT](https://mdtm.gov.my/index.php/informasi/aktiviti/majlis-pelaksanaan-projek-rintis-myhackathon-sistem-servit-smart-city-untuk-majlis-daerah-tanjong-malim) · [MYHackathon](https://www.myhackathon.gov.my/maklumat-program) · [FieldEx](https://www.fieldex.com/my/solutions/property-strata-maintenance) · [Fixello](https://www.fixello.com.my/en) · [APU report: Kommunity](https://publicwebsite-media.apu.edu.my/assets/2024-03/2022-APU-Sustainable-Development-Report.pdf) · [UTHM: SparkTrack](https://periodical.uthm.edu.my/index.php/mari/article/view/21608)
- **International analogues:** England requires local planning authorities receiving CIL or Section 106 contributions to publish an annual Infrastructure Funding Statement, with reporting on receipts, expenditure, unspent funds and infrastructure priorities; government also provides a developer-contributions data format. New York City's public Capital Project Tracker publishes project phase, progress fields, dates and total funding. Analyst's read: These show how to report developer money and project delivery, not a direct Malaysian legal equivalent. [UK GOV.UK, developer-contributions data](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/publish-your-developer-contributions-data) · [UK GOV.UK, CIL and Section 106 reporting](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/community-infrastructure-levy) · [NYC Open Data, Capital Project Tracker](https://data.cityofnewyork.us/w/4hcv-tc5r/25te-f2tw?cur=UoG_8DOXdmm)

## Real data a prototype could use (open datasets or APIs, with URLs)

- **For a real SDP pilot:** Analyst's read: SDP's actual as-built asset list, land/road reserve boundaries, approval and surrender letters, defect register, maintenance schedules, contractor work orders and inspection photos are the likely starting records. They are not public datasets; SDP and the receiving council would need to supply records for one real phase. [SDP Sustainability Report 2025](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/SDP%20SR2025%20%28Mobile%29.pdf)
- **Public finance and need baselines:** the Selangor Assembly's ISF table and annual complaint counts, plus the MPKS 2023 statement. The first two are openly downloadable but aggregate; the MPKS statement exposes fund-level receipts/payments/balance only in the extracted table (snippet only). [ISF table](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Bertulis-No.-322-Lampiran-A.pdf) · [complaint counts](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/aduan/) · [MPKS statement](https://www.mpks.gov.my/sites/default/files/2024-11/PENYATA%20KEWANGAN%202023.pdf)
- **Public map/API candidates:** MBPJ's ArcGIS FeatureServer accepts JSON queries and exposes building-condition/defect layers; MBSA's iMapSA is a public GIS entry point. Not found: an open drainage/asset API in these portals in this screen. Malaysia's [data.gov.my catalogue](https://data.gov.my/) and [API query docs](https://developer.data.gov.my/request-query) support discovery and programmatic access, but I did not find a specific township-maintenance dataset there. [MBPJ FeatureServer](https://services3.arcgis.com/Pm8D5pANQ4gpdLsD/ArcGIS/rest/services/MBPJ/FeatureServer) · [iMapSA](https://imapsa.mbsa.gov.my/)
- **Basemap only:** OpenStreetMap's Overpass API can return mapped roads, parks and other tagged features for a pilot boundary; it is community-mapped and must not be treated as the authoritative owner or asset register. [Overpass API guide](https://wiki.openstreetmap.org/wiki/Overpass_API/Language_Guide) · [API endpoint](https://overpass-api.de/api/interpreter)
- **Not found:** I searched data.gov.my, MBPJ and MBSA GIS pages, council financial-report pages, SISPAA and English/Malay queries for ISF spending by project/location. I did not find a public, machine-readable Selangor council dataset linking ISF collection → allocation → payment → project location/asset, or an open bulk SISPAA feed with issue status and close-out evidence. The accessible SISPAA flow is for individual reports/tickets. [Malaysia.gov SISPAA](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/my/feedback/sistem-pengurusan-aduan-bersepadu-sispaa) · [Selangor Assembly ISF table](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Bertulis-No.-322-Lampiran-A.pdf)

## What a strong solution could be

Analyst's read: Build a **handover and maintenance ledger** for one real SDP township phase: map each actual road, drain, pond, park and streetlight; attach its verified owner, surrender status, warranty/defect status, inspection date and next maintenance duty; route real resident reports to SDP, the council or the named agency; and require dated work-order evidence before closure. A student team can prototype the workflow in seven days from a small, real SDP asset sample and a handful of real, de-identified reports; SDP can pilot it within the brief's one to two year window and export the reconciled handover register to the receiving council. Add ISF receipts and project-level spend only if a council partner provides those records. That makes the product useful even if the council's ISF stays pooled under its statutory discretion. [SDP hackathon brief](https://simedarbyproperty.com/universityhackathon/) · [Act 133, s.132](https://www.ppj.gov.my/storage/7476/133---STREET%2C-DRAINAGE-AND-BUILDING-ACT-1974.pdf)

## Kill risks (3 bullets)

- **Wrong problem boundary:** Analyst's read: Not every “public” facility transfers to a council, and most resident evidence found is from South Klang rather than a representative national sample. If a pilot phase has clear ownership and a functioning handover process, this may be a complaint-response issue, not an ownership gap.
- **Weak SDP authority:** Analyst's read: SDP cannot make councils publish project-level ISF data or close public tickets. A council/agency partner and an asset-level pilot are needed; an ISF-only product is outside SDP's control.
- **Crowded and data-dependent:** Analyst's read: SISPAA, MyJalan, council GIS/complaint systems and the ServeIT pilot already cover parts of reporting and service tracking. If SDP cannot share real asset, defect and work-order records, a seven-day demo risks becoming a generic duplicate app. Recent financial evidence is fund-level, not evidence of misuse.

## Scores (1 to 5 each, one line of reason each): Pain, SDP lever, Evidence available, Crowding (5 = very crowded). Then: Edge = Pain x Lever x Evidence / Crowding.

- Analyst's read: **Pain: 4/5** — recurring floods, unsafe roads and neglected amenities are documented, but there is no statewide count of affected public assets or unresolved maintenance cases. [The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/metro/metro-news/2024/12/06/south-klang-residents-flooded-with-problems) · [Selangor Assembly](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/question/penyelenggaraan-infrastruktur/)
- Analyst's read: **SDP lever: 4/5** — SDP controls its own pre-handover records, defects and retained assets, but not council asset acceptance or ISF allocations. [SDP Sustainability Report 2025](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/SDP%20SR2025%20%28Mobile%29.pdf) · [Act 133, s.132](https://www.ppj.gov.my/storage/7476/133---STREET%2C-DRAINAGE-AND-BUILDING-ACT-1974.pdf)
- Analyst's read: **Evidence available: 5/5** — statute, published MBDK rates, Selangor fund and complaint tables, council financial statements, official State answers and resident reporting are available; asset-to-spend linkage is the gap. [MBDK](https://www.mbdk.gov.my/sites/default/files/media/document/2024-03/9.%20SENARAI%20SEMAK%20IMPROVEMENT%20SERVICE%20FUND%20%28ISF%29.pdf) · [Selangor Assembly](https://dewan.selangor.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/Bertulis-No.-322-Lampiran-A.pdf)
- Analyst's read: **Crowding: 4/5** — generic civic reporting and maintenance tracking are crowded, including MyJalan and ServeIT; no direct ISF-to-asset handover ledger was found. [MyJalan](https://www.malaysia.gov.my/en/topics/myjalan-kkr-application) · [ServeIT pilot](https://mdtm.gov.my/index.php/informasi/aktiviti/majlis-pelaksanaan-projek-rintis-myhackathon-sistem-servit-smart-city-untuk-majlis-daerah-tanjong-malim)

**Edge = 4 × 4 × 5 ÷ 4 = 20.** Analyst's read: Worth deeper research if reframed around handover and maintenance proof; weak if pitched as another complaint inbox or as a way for SDP to control council ISF spending.
