## One-line problem

Analyst's read: Construction sites can hold Aedes larvae in unfinished buildings and water-holding containers; nearby households may then sit beside a preventable breeding source, while public hotspot maps and contractor inspection records are not joined into one response workflow. The site types are documented in the [MOH construction-site guide](https://jknkelantan.moh.gov.my/htumpat/v1/images/ukkp/garis-panduan-pengawalan-nyamuk-aedes-di-tapak-pembinaan-.pdf).

## Scale and 2024 to 2026 evidence (bullets with numbers and sources)

- Malaysia recorded **65,979 cumulative dengue cases through epidemiological week 35 of 2026**, versus **39,616 through the same week of 2025**, a reported **66% increase**. Reported dengue deaths were **62 versus 32**, a **93.8% increase**. These are cumulative-to-week comparisons, not weekly incidence counts ([Bernama, 9 September 2026](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2605035)).
- **Not found:** a public downloadable historical table with weekly incidence for each week of **2026** and matching weeks of **2025**. [iDengue](https://idengue.mysa.gov.my/ide_v3/introduction.html) describes weekly PPA case/outbreak map layers, but I did not find a historical export for that direct comparison.
- Malaysia recorded **55,399 dengue cases in 2025**, versus **122,423 in 2024**, a reported **54.7% decline** ([Bernama, 23 February 2026](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2526693)).
- In **2024**, Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya health authorities inspected **242 construction sites**; **126 sites (52%)** were Aedes-positive. The agency's overall enforcement totals were **298 Section 6 notices, 61 Section 8(1) notices, 17 Section 8(3)(aa) closure orders, 17 closures, 19 prosecutions and 192 compounds totalling RM96,000**. Those enforcement totals cover the agency's work and are not identified as construction-site-only. The report is listed on the [official JKWPKL&P annual-report page](https://jknkl.moh.gov.my/jkwpklp/ms/penerbitan-dan-pelaporan/laporan-tahunan); the figures are legible in a [copy of its 2024 report mirrored on Heyzine](https://cdnc.heyzine.com/files/uploaded/v3/51317cfa7b379c98da9cc77545a38114dd01416b.pdf).
- A Kuala Lumpur operation reported **85 Aedes-positive construction sites among 137 inspected by 4 May 2024**. At Taman Naga Emas, inspectors found **8 breeding locations: 6 inside a building and 2 outside**. The report said many of the inspected sites were near housing ([Kosmo, 23 May 2024](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2024/05/23/nyamuk-aedes-berlambak-di-tapak-projek/)). This is an inspection positivity rate, not the share of all Malaysian Aedes breeding sites that are on construction sites.
- Analyst's read: The reports connect site findings with nearby housing and local dengue cases, but I found no matched spatial study showing that construction sites caused surrounding clusters or quantifying their contribution ([Kosmo, 2024](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2024/05/23/nyamuk-aedes-berlambak-di-tapak-projek/), [DBKL, 2026](https://www.dbkl.gov.my/berita-acara/dbkl-ambil-tindakan-tegas-tangani-risiko-pembiakan-nyamuk-aedes-di-tapak-pembinaan)).
- The MOH construction-site guide reports an older national baseline: in **2014**, **1,124 of 7,768 inspected sites (14.5%)** were positive and **809** were compounded ([MOH-hosted guide](https://jknkelantan.moh.gov.my/htumpat/v1/images/ukkp/garis-panduan-pengawalan-nyamuk-aedes-di-tapak-pembinaan-.pdf)). This is not a current national estimate.
- **Not found:** a current national or Selangor estimate of the share of all Aedes breeding sites located at construction sites, or a comparable Selangor series of construction-site inspections, positive sites, compounds, prosecutions, closures or stop-work orders for **2024–2026**. I searched MOH and CIDB guidance, JKN Selangor materials, state and local-authority pages, and Selangor legislative records. The available Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya figures above cannot stand in for Selangor.

## Why now (a 2025 or 2026 trigger, if any)

The reported national case count had fallen sharply in **2025**, then was **66% higher through week 35 of 2026** than through the same week of **2025** ([Bernama's week-35 update](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2605035), [Bernama's 2025 annual total](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2526693)).

In **2026**, DBKL described an inspection at a construction site on Jalan Setiakasih, Bukit Damansara, after **4 local dengue cases**. It reported **4 positive breeding locations**, a mosquito reading of **3 per minute**, **4 larva-collection notices** under the Act, and a **14-day closure order** ([DBKL](https://www.dbkl.gov.my/berita-acara/dbkl-ambil-tindakan-tegas-tangani-risiko-pembiakan-nyamuk-aedes-di-tapak-pembinaan)). This is a Kuala Lumpur case, not a Selangor count.

Analyst's read: The rebound and the DBKL case make this a timely prevention problem, but the quick screen finds no evidence that construction sites caused the national increase.

## Who suffers and who owns the fix

- Nearby residents and site workers face exposure where breeding and dengue transmission occur. Health offices and local authorities bear the inspection and response work. The [2024 Kuala Lumpur report](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2024/05/23/nyamuk-aedes-berlambak-di-tapak-projek/) and [2026 DBKL operation](https://www.dbkl.gov.my/berita-acara/dbkl-ambil-tindakan-tegas-tangani-risiko-pembiakan-nyamuk-aedes-di-tapak-pembinaan) document construction-site findings beside residential areas or local cases.
- The [Destruction of Disease-Bearing Insects Act 1975](https://www.moh.gov.my/images/04-penerbitan/akta-kesihatan/destruction%20of%20disease-bearing%20insects%20act%201975.pdf) lets inspectors order owners or occupiers to remove breeding receptacles, cover tanks, improve drainage or fill depressions, and close all or part of premises until work is complete (sections 8(1)–(4)). Section 11 allows health authorities to require plans and impose conditions on building and land-development work.
- The Act's Section 22 creates potential owner/occupier and corporate-officer liability when another person commits an offence, subject to the stated due-diligence defence. Section 25 allows a compound of up to **RM500** for prescribed compoundable offences ([Act 154, sections 22 and 25](https://www.moh.gov.my/images/04-penerbitan/akta-kesihatan/destruction%20of%20disease-bearing%20insects%20act%201975.pdf)). The Act uses the term **closure order**; a separate “stop-work order” label may rely on other powers. A reported Kuala Lumpur notice of “RM4,000” in the 2024 news coverage is not broken down, so it should not be treated as one compound under section 25 ([Kosmo](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2024/05/23/nyamuk-aedes-berlambak-di-tapak-projek/)).
- In Selangor, the [MPAJ 2026 work calendar](https://www.mpaj.gov.my/sites/default/files/2_takwim_mpaj_2026_-_02_feb.docx.pdf) schedules construction-site operations once a month. This is planned frequency, not a count of completed inspections or enforcement. The [MBPJ March 2024 mayor's message](https://www.mbpj.gov.my/sites/default/files/3._perutusan_datuk_bandar_mac_2024.26.3.2024_latest_1.1.pdf) lists notices, compounds and construction-site closures among dengue enforcement actions but gives no construction-specific totals in the material found.
- **Guidance:** The [MOH construction-site guide](https://jknkelantan.moh.gov.my/htumpat/v1/images/ukkp/garis-panduan-pengawalan-nyamuk-aedes-di-tapak-pembinaan-.pdf) is the specific contractor-facing Aedes guide found. [CIDB's published health-and-safety guidance](https://www.cidb.gov.my/jombinasempurna/panduan/garis-panduan-kesihatan-dan-keselamatan/) and [Act 520 information](https://www.cidb.gov.my/eng/act-520-of-the-malaysian-construction-industry-development-board-cidb/) are broader construction-industry guidance; I did not find a current CIDB Aedes-specific procedure.

## SDP's lever (what SDP controls: before handover, defect period, township management, its own assets)

The **2025** [SDP Integrated Annual Report](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/SDP%20IAR2025%20Mobile.pdf) describes **27 active townships** and identifies projects including City of Elmina, Bandar Bukit Raja and Serenia City.

Analyst's read: SDP's strongest lever is its role as landowner/developer and client for its own construction work. Before handover it can make Aedes prevention part of tender requirements, method statements, site access, inspection evidence, escalation and contractor close-out. During the defect period it can require documented rectification where its contract and access rights allow. Township management can manage SDP-owned land and assets, maintain drainage, and route resident reports to the right team. It can coordinate with authorities over neighbouring third-party sites, but it cannot direct those sites unless it has the relevant ownership, contract or statutory authority. The Act's owner/occupier duties make documented due diligence useful, but the legal result depends on the facts and roles in each site ([Act 154](https://www.moh.gov.my/images/04-penerbitan/akta-kesihatan/destruction%20of%20disease-bearing%20insects%20act%201975.pdf)).

## What already exists (products, services, programmes, hackathon projects)

- **Public surveillance:** [iDengue](https://idengue.mysa.gov.my/ide_v3/introduction.html) says it updates daily and provides hotspot, daily cluster, weekly PPA case/outbreak layers, state and district case information, and a public feedback channel. It is a public information portal, not a contractor inspection system.
- **Resident alerting:** [MySejahtera's Infectious Disease Tracker](https://www.mkn.gov.my/web/ms/2022/05/10/penambahbaikan-fungsi-hotspot-tracker-untuk-aplikasi-mysejahtera/) was announced in **2022** with dengue among the diseases shown. Current availability and coverage were not verified in this screen.
- **Commercial/research tools:** [Hayat AI.rbo](https://myhayat.my/solutions/airbo/) markets arbovirus surveillance and outbreak prediction. [MozzHub](https://www.riec.unimas.my/mozzhub) is a UNIMAS research system for dengue hotspot detection. [AedesTech Mobile App](https://ejournal.ukm.my/serangga/article/view/69168) counts Aedes eggs from ovitrap images. These are adjacent to site work; none of the pages reviewed describes a verified SDP-contractor workflow.
- **Contractor tools:** Malaysian site apps such as [BuildTrack](https://buildtrack.com.my/products/jobs-and-site-tracking) and [i-Supervision](https://www.i-supervision.com/) support general site tracking and records; no Aedes-specific feature was found in the pages reviewed.
- **Research and student work:** A **2024** UiTM/CIDB Selangor-linked paper, “Strategy for Dengue Prevention and Control at Construction Sites,” discusses the problem with public-health and construction stakeholders ([journal paper](https://jurnal.maeh4u.org.my/index.php/home/article/view/88)). The [ADVA/Jr. Achievement 2024 Dengue Slayers Challenge](https://www.adva.asia/adva-ja-dengue-slayers-challenge-2024-asia-pacific-grand-finals-winners-share-their-personal-connections-to-dengue-more/) included Malaysian team WingWard's dengue outreach; a **2026** report describes Malaysian student team Vector Vixens pitching a technology-led dengue idea ([The Sun](https://thesun.my/news/malaysia-news/malaysian-students-pitch-tech-driven-dengue-solution-at-asia-dengue-summit/)). These are not construction-site management products.
- **Crowding check:** Searches of [MYHackathon's programme and winner pages](https://www.myhackathon.gov.my/pemenang-2022), [KitaHack 2024](https://www.monash.edu.my/it/news-and-events/articles/2024/kita-hack), [KitaHack 2026](https://kitahack2026.my/) and Devpost for dengue/Aedes/construction-site combinations did not surface a Malaysian project specifically managing breeding on live construction sites. Devpost does have adjacent dengue and mosquito projects, including [DengueRadar](https://devpost.com/software/dengueradar) and [Culicid](https://devpost.com/software/mozzy). This was a quick screen, not an exhaustive competition archive review.
- **Breeding detection:** The MOH guide lists basements, lift wells, drums, formwork, water tanks, floors and slabs, canvas covers, balconies and food containers as inspection targets ([MOH construction-site guide](https://jknkelantan.moh.gov.my/htumpat/v1/images/ukkp/garis-panduan-pengawalan-nyamuk-aedes-di-tapak-pembinaan-.pdf)). A **2023** National Institutes of Health poster reporting a **2018** survey of **19 Kuala Lumpur construction sites** found breeding on floors from ground level through the **14th floor**, with more larvae on the ground through second floors. Positive containers were flooded floors (**43.9%**), drains (**35.1%**), drums (**12.3%**), pails (**5.3%**) and plastic containers (**3.5%**) ([NIH poster](https://iku.nih.gov.my/images/poster2023/02.pdf)). Rebar caps were specifically searched in MOH/CIDB/state guidance; **not found** as a documented breeding source in the sources reviewed.
- **Inspection frequency and burden:** MPAJ's **2026** calendar schedules construction-site operations **once a month** ([MPAJ calendar](https://www.mpaj.gov.my/sites/default/files/2_takwim_mpaj_2026_-_02_feb.docx.pdf)). A MOH MaHTAS assessment says a ground inspection takes **20–25 minutes per premises** and an **8-person** team can cover about **36 premises in 3 hours** ([MOH assessment](https://www.moh.gov.my/images/04-penerbitan/penerbitan-klinikal/mahtas/FR%20-%20UNMANNED%20AIRCRAFT%20SYSTEMS%20FOR%20DENGUE%20SURVEILLANCE.pdf)). These sources suggest a gap between monthly public inspections and more frequent contractor self-checks, but actual frequencies vary by site and outbreak.

## Real data a prototype could use (open datasets or APIs, with URLs)

- [iDengue](https://idengue.mysa.gov.my/ide_v3/introduction.html): daily public portal with hotspot and weekly case/outbreak layers. I found no documented public API in the quick screen, so treat it as a map/data source to verify before building automated ingestion.
- [JKN Selangor weekly dengue report, ME26 2025](https://jknselangor.moh.gov.my/images/2025/Denggi%20ME/Minggu_Epid_26.2025_Situasi_Semasa_Kes_Demam_Denggi_Negeri_Selangor_Sehingga_28_Jun_2025.pdf): reports **398 cases in that week** and **13,275 cumulative cases** through **28 June 2025**.
- [MBSJ dengue FeatureServer](https://eis.mbsj.gov.my/server/rest/services/Portal/DASHBOARD_DENGGI_LATEST/FeatureServer): a real local-authority GIS endpoint surfaced in search. Confirm layer fields, update cadence and access before relying on it.
- [MPAJ 2026 calendar](https://www.mpaj.gov.my/sites/default/files/2_takwim_mpaj_2026_-_02_feb.docx.pdf): a real schedule for monthly construction-site operations; it does not expose inspection outcomes.
- [data.gov.my dengue data request](https://data.gov.my/data-request): a request for weekly Selangor dengue cases from **2015 to 2025**, submitted **16 September 2026**, was marked **Under Review** at the time of this screen. This is not yet a published open dataset.
- Analyst's read: The pilot needs actual SDP/contractor data: site boundaries and phase, dated inspection records, geotagged evidence, positive/negative findings, assigned action and verified close-out. Use a consenting live site and real checks; public case data alone cannot establish whether a particular contractor site is breeding.

## What a strong solution could be (one paragraph; must be something SDP could pilot in 1 to 2 years and a student team could prototype in 7 days)

Analyst's read: Build a risk-triggered Aedes assurance workflow for SDP's construction sites. A **7-day** student prototype could map a consenting pilot site's real boundary and inspection records against iDengue or JKN Selangor hotspot information, then give the site supervisor a mobile checklist for the documented breeding points, dated photos, corrective tasks and close-out evidence. In a **1–2 year** live pilot, SDP could add its main-contractor and pest-control-operator process, assign owners and deadlines, and escalate repeat findings to the project manager before handover. Track repeat-positive locations, time to verified close-out and the share of scheduled checks completed; do not claim reduced dengue cases unless a longer evaluation supports that conclusion.

## Kill risks (3 bullets)

- Analyst's read: Public dengue locations and site boundaries may be too coarse or delayed to attribute risk to one site; data access and household privacy can block matching.
- Analyst's read: Contractor checklists can become paperwork. Without photo checks, spot audits and an accountable site owner, the system may measure reporting rather than actual breeding control.
- Analyst's read: Nearby dengue cases do not prove that the construction site caused them. Other premises may be the source, and monthly public inspection plans do not show actual coverage or outcomes.

## Scores (1 to 5 each, one line of reason each): Pain, SDP lever, Evidence available, Crowding (5 = very crowded). Then: Edge = Pain x Lever x Evidence / Crowding.

- **Pain: 4/5.** National cases rose through week 35 of **2026**, and Kuala Lumpur inspection records show material positivity at construction sites; current Selangor-specific burden remains unquantified ([Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2605035), [JKWPKL&P report listing](https://jknkl.moh.gov.my/jkwpklp/ms/penerbitan-dan-pelaporan/laporan-tahunan)).
- **SDP lever: 4/5.** SDP has direct control over its construction contracts and owned assets across **27 active townships**, but limited control over unrelated neighbouring sites ([SDP IAR 2025](https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/sites/default/files/2026-04/SDP%20IAR2025%20Mobile.pdf)).
- **Evidence available: 3/5.** Public weekly cases, hotspot layers and some site inspections exist, but there is no current open Selangor construction-site outcome series ([iDengue](https://idengue.mysa.gov.my/ide_v3/introduction.html), [data.gov.my request](https://data.gov.my/data-request)).
- **Crowding: 3/5.** Dengue maps, research tools and student ideas exist, while the direct contractor/site-control niche looked less crowded in the quick screen ([AI.rbo](https://myhayat.my/solutions/airbo/), [MozzHub](https://www.riec.unimas.my/mozzhub), [Devpost examples](https://devpost.com/software/dengueradar)).
- **Edge = 4 × 4 × 3 / 3 = 16.** Analyst's read: Worth deeper research if SDP can secure site-level inspection data and a contractor willing to pilot; otherwise the idea risks becoming another hotspot map.
