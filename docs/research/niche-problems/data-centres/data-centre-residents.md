# What data-centre neighbours experience

Deep-dive evidence on what people living near data centres experience, in Malaysia and abroad, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 60 claims: 33 confirmed, 19 corrected, 2 removed and 6 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings.

## Summary

- **Analyst's read:** In the Malaysian resident accounts reviewed, reported disturbance is mostly tied to construction: dust, soil runoff, noise, late or weekend work, and problems with sleep, cleaning and laundry. Residents also report coughs, allergies and skin irritation. The reports do not establish clinical diagnoses or prove that a data-centre project caused those symptoms. ([1][r1], [2][r2], [5][r3])

- Residents in Johor and Selangor have raised concerns about water, power, traffic, safety, visual change and property impacts. Most are concerns about proposals or construction, rather than measured impacts from operating facilities. Bernama reported that an Iskandar Puteri authority gave contractors two weeks to comply with five conditions, including halting earthworks. Gelang Patah residents later attended a town hall where dust-control measures were discussed. ([2][r2], [3][r4], [6][r5])

- In Elmina, public-map markers put the Google data-centre marker about 1.0 km straight-line from the Aman Putri area marker and about 1 km from SK Merbau Sempak. These are point-to-point estimates, not distances from the site boundary to the nearest home or school building. An August 2025 Bernama interview records one Elmina resident's concern about water allocation; it does not report a measured impact or formal complaint. ([22](https://mapcarta.com/W1448535359), [23](https://mapcarta.com/W823054705), [48][r6])

- **Analyst's read:** Foreign evidence varies by impact and method. Virginia's JLARC summary discusses resident concerns about low-frequency noise, while a 2026 Phoenix-area study measured a local downwind heat signature. Water-use numbers are largely company-reported site data; some regulator measurements cover ambient air rather than emissions attributed to data centres. ([30][r7], [31][r8], [49][r9])

- **Analyst's read:** I did not find a representative Malaysian public-opinion poll specifically about data centres. Local objections and petitions are case-level signals, not a national measure. The Ministry of Digital's 2025 national survey covered digital initiatives and transformation broadly, not data centres specifically. ([6][r5], [9][r10], [45][r11])

## City of Elmina: distance to homes and any resident voice

Sime Darby Property's 2019 brochure includes a master layout that places Elmina Business Park at the northern end of City of Elmina. I use it only for relative layout; the distance estimates below come from public map markers. ([21][r17])

Sime Darby Property announced a 49-acre first data-centre site at Elmina Business Park in May 2024; Google confirmed its first Malaysian data centre and cloud region there in October 2024. SDP announced a separate 77-acre site nearby in December 2024. It awarded the construction contract for that later phase in October 2025, with completion targeted for Q2 2027. ([14][r18], [15][r19], [16][r20], [17][r21])

- **Nearby homes:** Public OpenStreetMap-derived markers place the Google data-centre marker at 3.22303° N, 101.51854° E and the Aman Putri area marker at 3.21475° N, 101.52259° E. **Analyst's read:** the straight-line point-to-point distance is approximately 1.0 km. This does not identify the closest house, site boundary or actual walking/road route. ([22](https://mapcarta.com/W1448535359), [23](https://mapcarta.com/W823054705))

- **Elmina residential-phase markers:** Public map points put Elmina Gardens about 3.6 km from the data-centre marker and Elmina Ridge / Elmina Peak about 7.5 km away, measured straight-line between mapped place points. Mapcarta identifies the Ridge/Peak feature as a construction feature, so that point is not presented as a residential-home marker. **Analyst's read:** Aman Putri is the closest of these checked residential-area markers; none of these measurements is a surveyed boundary distance. ([22](https://mapcarta.com/W1448535359), [24](https://mapcarta.com/W704723792), [25](https://mapcarta.com/W220414885))

- **School:** Mapcarta lists SK Merbau Sempak about 1 km southwest of the data-centre marker. This is a public-map distance, not a site survey. ([22](https://mapcarta.com/W1448535359))

- **Resident voice:** An Aug 2025 Bernama feature interviewed Mohd Faris Mohd Razali, a resident of Elmina Green 4 and 5 who lived close to the Google facility. He worried that water demand might be prioritised for the project and hoped the government and developer had planned for local needs. This is a resident's concern, not evidence of measured harm or a formal complaint. I did not find a corroborated Elmina petition or council complaint record in the reviewed sources. ([48][r6])

- **Status and noise evidence:** Public status reports use different phase and timing descriptions. SDP said the Google facility was scheduled for completion in Q2 2026; a Selangor Assembly answer says phase one had operated since 2025 and recorded 0.2 MLD actual use as of March 2026; in Aug 2026, Google's spokesperson told Media Selangor that no operation date was set and projected operations within nine months. **Analyst's read:** these accounts do not establish one exact commissioning date; no independent source found here resolves the difference. ([18][r22], [20][r23], [19](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390529))

- Selangor's 2026 Assembly answer forecasts 53 MLD across three Elmina data-centre phases: 10 MLD for Google's phase one, 20 MLD for Google's phase two and 23 MLD for future facilities. Air Selangor required a dedicated pipeline from Bukit Tadun balancing pond rather than the existing resident-supply network. These are supply plans, not measured household-pressure effects. ([20][r23])

- The same Assembly answer reports actual use of 0.2 MLD by the operating data centre as of March 2026. This is an official reported figure, not an independent meter audit. ([20][r23])

- Google's spokesperson told Media Selangor the company would conduct regular operational noise tests and described 50–65 decibels as typical readings. The report does not specify the weighting or measurement location, and it publishes no Elmina measurement. He also described inward-facing generators, setbacks and noise-dampening fans as mitigation measures. These are company statements, not independent results. ([19](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390529))

The city government said household flow had been restored and a dedicated pipe was being built. This describes a connection-testing disruption, not proof that data-centre consumption caused a shortage. ([38][r32])

In Oct 2026, Sambau residents protested after household water had reportedly been unavailable for three months. Residents linked the problem to data-centre expansion, but the article reports that link as their allegation and does not establish causation. Separately, on 24 Sep 2026—before the protest—BP Batam urged data-centre investors to build seawater reverse-osmosis facilities for their operations and said public supply would continue to rely on reservoirs. A company representative said it would provide a written response within September. The cited report does not say residents reported 24-hour piling, dust or filling of a public canal. A later community forum outlined planned noise, water and disclosure steps; these are commitments, not verified completed remedies. ([41][r35], [42][r36])

Near a data-centre site in Bangkok's Rama IX area, residents reported petroleum odour and oily residue in a public drain. On 2 Sep 2026, Huai Khwang District said it had asked the operator for wastewater and air-quality tests and was coordinating checks to identify the source; the notice does not say the district arranged drain cleanup. ## Public opinion in Malaysia

**Analyst's read:** I did not find a national or representative Malaysian survey specifically measuring public attitudes toward data centres. Searches covered English and Malay terms for public opinion/tinjauan awam, polls/pungutan suara, resident attitudes/pendapat penduduk and data centres/pusat data, including government, DOSM, polling organisations and major news sources. The Ministry of Digital's survey, launched on 15 Jul 2025, asked about digital access, literacy and emerging technologies; it was not a data-centre opinion poll. ([45][r11])

The local counts found were about 290 objections to the Kota Damansara proposal, more than 50 people at the Gelang Patah protest who said they represented nearly 1,000 residents, and 108 verified signatures shown on the Cyberjaya petition page when checked. These counts describe specific cases and cannot be generalized to Malaysia. ([6][r5], [1][r1], [9][r10])

## Gaps

- **Malaysia:** **Analyst's read:** I did not find independent, site-specific operational noise or infrasound measurements; verified operational heat measurements; measured water-pressure or power-cut impacts attributed to a data centre; local measurements of generator emissions; or an analysis of nearby home prices in the reviewed sources. Searches covered government and local-authority material, peer-reviewed sources and English/Malay reporting for data-centre/pusat data with bunyi/noise, infrasound, habuk/dust, suhu/heat, tekanan air/water pressure, gangguan elektrik/power cuts, harga rumah/property value and lampu/light spill.
- **Elmina:** **Analyst's read:** I found no public baseline or post-commissioning boundary noise dataset, household water-pressure series, school exposure study, resident petition or council complaint record. One resident's water-allocation concern is documented above. Public sources describe phase-one status differently; no independent commissioning confirmation was found by 16 Oct 2026. ([18][r22], [20][r23], [19](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390529))
- **Abroad:** **Analyst's read:** I did not find a calibrated infrasound study in the reviewed jurisdictions; measured noise data rarely isolate the data-centre contribution. I found no site-specific field measurement of neighbourhood heat impacts in Ireland, the Netherlands or Chile.
