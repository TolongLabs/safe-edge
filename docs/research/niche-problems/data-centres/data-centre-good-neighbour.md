## Summary

- Oracle's Project Jupiter plan includes a public dashboard and twice-yearly independent reports comparing results with baseline conditions and relevant standards. Oracle says these will start after the data centres operate. UCCS's DC-IRE Lab is also an upcoming project; its current timeline targets initial baseline monitoring in summer–fall 2026 and a public dashboard in spring 2027. Neither source reports operating results yet. [Oracle, 8 Sep 2026][r1] · [UCCS DC-IRE Lab][r2]
- Loudoun County publishes its noise-study and complaint process, including a 55 dBA residential property-line limit and enforcement steps. An Irish EPA licence example for Microsoft Ireland requires annual noise surveys and review of a site Noise Management Plan. EU data-centre reporting applies at 500 kW of installed IT power demand; public database results are aggregated at Member State and EU level, while individual-centre data are confidential. [Loudoun County][r3] · [Microsoft Ireland noise plan, 8 Sep 2023][r4] · [EU Delegated Regulation 2024/1364, 14 Mar 2024][r5]
- Community support examples include Frederick County's announced community-benefit agreement, operator grants and training, and Johor programmes. A follow-up search found STT GDC's 2026 Johor training and youth initiatives and a Malaysian Data Centre Task Force rule requiring at least 30% local content for approved projects. Local content is not the same as a hiring quota for nearby residents. [Frederick County, 1 Sep 2026][r6] · [STT GDC, 14 Apr 2026][r7] · [Bernama, 14 Oct 2026][r8]
- Reclaimed-water use is reported in Singapore and Johor, and AWS says it uses reclaimed water for cooling in West Java and has contracted for reclaimed-water supply in Malaysia. Singapore's ministry says most water supplied to data centres is NEWater. Johor's 24 June 2026 parliamentary answer reports 12 million litres per day of reclaimed water supplied for data-centre cooling. **Analyst's read:** The documented heat-reuse cases found here are in Norway and Sweden; no operating tropical neighbourhood heat-reuse case was verified in the sources searched. [Singapore MSE, 26 Feb 2026][r9] · [Amazon, 1 Oct 2026][r10] · [Malaysia Hansard, 24 Jun 2026][r11]
- Public costs range from snippet-only Malaysian equipment rental rates to published UK monitor hire, air-sensor purchase prices, a research infrasound instrument, and Thai noise-monitoring services. These are listed prices, not full Malaysian installation or compliance-study budgets. [KP Acoustics 2024 price list][r12] · [AirGradient](https://www.airgradient.com/outdoor) · [Geonoise Thailand][r13]

## Planning and siting tools

- **Noise contours:** CadnaA is commercial noise-prediction and mapping software. Its current product page lists ISO 9613-2 and CNOSSOS-EU support, GIS import, and export of calculated noise maps. A model can compare equipment layouts and receptor exposures before a site is fixed. [CadnaA product page][r34]
- **Malaysian buffer rules:** Johor's guideline, published in April 2024, specifies a minimum 50-metre buffer from the data-centre building line, including related chiller and generator structures, to residential lot boundaries. It also calls for a noise barrier if generators are near homes. The national PLANMalaysia GPP's official English PDF could not be opened; its search snippet indicates a 50-metre buffer with a noise-based alternative. Treat the precise national condition as snippet-only until the PDF is checked. [Johor GPP][r35] · [National GPP, English PDF, snippet-only][r36]
- **Development review:** Loudoun requires pre- and post-construction noise studies and publishes an interactive map of existing and proposed data centres. PLANMalaysia's i-Plan provides current land-use, zoning, and committed-land-use information for screening locations. [Loudoun standards and locations](https://www.loudoun.gov/DataCenterStandards) · [Loudoun noise standards][r3] · [PLANMalaysia i-Plan FAQ][r37]
- **Not found:** I did not find a public self-service calculator that combines data-centre source spectra, terrain, building massing, residential setbacks, and Malaysian planning limits in the sources searched. This is a scoped search result, not proof that no such tool exists. [PLANMalaysia planning portal][r38] · [CadnaA][r34]

**Analyst's read:** Use Johor's 50-metre rule as a local planning input, then model sound at actual receptors. The buffer rule does not replace site-specific noise analysis. The national guideline snippet appears to include a noise-based alternative, so do not describe the national buffer as an unconditional minimum without checking its full text. [Johor GPP][r35] · [National GPP snippet][r36] · [Loudoun County][r3]

## What no one has done yet

- **Analyst's read:** In the sources reviewed, monitoring, grants, water reuse, utility protections, and community engagement appear as separate pieces. I did not locate one published host-community agreement that combines independent environmental monitoring, resident oversight, public complaint resolution, local grant funding, and automatic bill relief tied to measured performance. Examples show elements separately: [Oracle][r1], [Frederick County][r6], [Google](https://www.datacenters.google/our-approach/), and [Loudoun County][r3].
- **Analyst's read:** I did not verify an operating tropical neighbourhood heat-reuse project in the English and official sources searched for Singapore, Johor, and Malaysia. The documented live examples found were aquaculture in Norway and district heating in Sweden. This is a scoped search result, not proof no project exists. [Green Mountain][r30] · [Stockholm Exergi][r31]
- **Analyst's read:** Follow-up searches surfaced Japan's Environment Ministry programme for data-centre decarbonisation and regional coexistence, and Microsoft's pledge for local benefits around its planned Jakarta-region data centres. Those pages do not show a recurring host-neighbour grant amount or a formal community-benefit agreement. The original absence claim is limited to those specific mechanisms, not to all public programmes or pledges. [Japan Ministry of the Environment, page updated Jul 2026][r43] · [Microsoft Indonesia][r44]
- **Analyst's read:** For an SDP township, a practical pilot could publish baseline noise and air data, use a university or accredited third party to validate measurements, maintain a resident committee and complaint log with response deadlines, ring-fence local grants and training, publish water sources and withdrawals, and set out utility-cost commitments. Reclaimed water and utility protections have closer operating precedents in warm climates than neighbourhood heat export. [UCCS DC-IRE Lab][r2] · [Johor water programme][r11] · [Google's approach](https://www.datacenters.google/our-approach/)
