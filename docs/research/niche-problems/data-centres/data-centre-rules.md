# Data centres in townships: footprint and rules

Deep-dive evidence on the physical footprint of hyperscale data centres and the Malaysian rules that govern them, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 52 claims: 22 confirmed, 13 corrected, 0 removed and 17 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings.

## Summary

- Malaysia’s planning guidance treats a data centre above 1 MVA as a planning use and gives layout provisions, while water, noise, energy, and environmental requirements come from separate instruments and state conditions. [PLANMalaysia Circular 4/2024][r1] [PLANMalaysia planning guideline][r2]
- Analyst's read: cooling choice can change water demand by orders of magnitude. An August 2026 review reports about 0–2.5 litres per kWh of IT energy across dry, free, hybrid, and evaporative systems; an October 2026 study reports closed-circuit cooling-tower WUE approaching or exceeding 2 litres per kWh in some southern Chinese cities. The papers' pages could not be opened; these values are from indexed source excerpts and are not Malaysian measurements. [2026 review](https://www.mdpi.com/2311-5521/11/8/201) [2026 hot-humid climate study][r3]
- Public records identify Elmina’s Google data-centre land parcels, a 53 MLD phase allocation, and a dedicated supply pipeline from Kolam Imbangan Bukit Tadun. They do not disclose the water's quality/category or site IT MW. Google’s infrastructure principal said in August 2026 that its data centre uses evaporative cooling; exact site engineering details and reclaimed-water use remain undisclosed. The state-reported allocation is not the same as consumption. [SDP Elmina release][r4] [Selangor Assembly answer][r5] [Media Selangor, 21 August 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390837)
- The main nearby-resident controls are planning buffers/setbacks, state water-supply conditions, and DOE receiver-based noise limits. The reviewed material did not establish a general data-centre noise level at a fence or fixed distance, generator exercise interval, or perimeter-lighting lux limit. [PLANMalaysia guideline][r2] [DOE noise guideline][r6]
- Analyst's read: counts and forecasts use different boundaries and definitions. SDP’s cited market research estimates 65 operating Malaysian centres with 1,243.8 MW of IT capacity in 2025; TNB reported a 7.5 GW secured-project maximum-demand pipeline at December 2025. [SDP IAR 2025][r7] [TNB 4QFY2025 briefing][r8]

## Google and Elmina specifics

Google’s global figures are fleet-level metrics, not Elmina disclosures:

- Google reports a 2025 global average PUE of 1.09 for its data centres. [Google data-centre efficiency][r12]
- Google reports that its 2025 average carbon-free energy share was about 65% across data centres and offices, and states a 24/7 carbon-free energy goal for 2030. [Google sustainability](https://ai.google/sustainability/)
- Google says it replenished about 7.7 billion gallons of water through 165 projects in 97 watersheds in 2025, equivalent to about 78% of its 2025 freshwater consumption. The 2026 report highlights page identifies 2025 as the reporting year. [Google 2026 Environmental Report highlights][r13]
- Google signed more than 12 GW of net-new clean-energy agreements in 2025. In its 2025 report covering 2024, Google said its data-centre electricity use grew 27% year over year while data-centre energy emissions fell 12%; these are changes, not absolute MWh. [Google 2026 Environmental Report][r13] [Google 2025 Environmental Report announcement][r14]
- The accessible Google report highlights did not provide an absolute global data-centre electricity total that I could verify. I could not open the linked full 2026 report PDF, so this is a limitation of the reviewed pages, not a claim that the full report omits the figure. Google reports water stewardship at company-wide scale; its operating-sustainably page says 87% of 2025 freshwater withdrawals came from low- or medium-water-risk sources. [Google 2026 Environmental Report][r13] [Google operating sustainably][r15]

Elmina public disclosures:

- SDP says its first Google hyperscale data-centre project is at Elmina Business Park and broke ground in October 2024; the release said it targeted early 2026 and referred to large-scale water and energy solutions without specifying the technologies. [SDP release, 2 October 2024][r4]
- SDP’s earlier release describes the first site as about 49 acres; SDP later announced a second data-centre project on a 77-acre site. Analyst's read: 49 + 77 = 126 acres of land parcel area, not building floor area or IT capacity. [SDP first project release][r16] [SDP second project release][r17]
- SDP’s 2025 Integrated Annual Report scheduled the first data centre for 2026 and the second for 2027. In a 26 May 2026 release, SDP said it had completed and handed over its first Elmina hyperscale data centre and that rental commenced in Q2 FY2026. Handover and rental do not establish that Google had begun operating it. [SDP IAR 2025][r7] [SDP Q1 FY2026 release, 26 May 2026][r18]
- An indexed excerpt of a Selangor Assembly answer from its April 2026 sitting gives a 53 MLD total across Elmina phases: 10 MLD for Google Phase 1, 20 MLD for Google Phase 2 expected in 2028, and 23 MLD for other future centres. It reports actual use of 0.2 MLD as of March 2026 by data centres described as operating; the excerpt does not assign that use specifically to Google. Analyst's read: allocation, future reservation, and actual consumption are different measures. The page could not be opened. [Selangor Assembly answer, 2026 sitting (snippet-only)][r5]
- The same indexed state answer says a dedicated new pipeline supplies Elmina from Kolam Imbangan Bukit Tadun in Kuang, bypassing the existing consumer network. It reports Air Selangor capacity of 6,500 MLD against average demand of 5,370 MLD, a stated reserve of 1,130 MLD or 17.4%. These are state-reported figures, not independent measurements. The excerpt does not identify whether the water is potable, raw, or reclaimed. The page could not be opened. [Selangor Assembly answer, 2026 sitting (snippet-only)][r5]
- In August 2026, Google’s Asia Pacific infrastructure principal told Media Selangor that Elmina construction was progressing and operations were expected within nine months, with no exact start date set. On 21 August he said Google’s Malaysian data centre was not yet operational. This conflicts with the Assembly answer’s statement that Google Phase 1 had operated in 2025; public sources do not reconcile the milestones. Analyst's read: no later primary-source operational confirmation was located through 16 October 2026. [Media Selangor, 20 August 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390529) [Media Selangor, 21 August 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390837) [Selangor Assembly answer][r5]
- In the same August interview, Google’s principal described Google data-centre cooling as evaporative and said cooling water is recirculated before discharge. The public statement gives no Elmina-specific cooling design or water-volume figures. An indexed excerpt of a November 2025 state answer says reclaimed water was encouraged but not mandatory; the page could not be opened. No Elmina commitment to use reclaimed water was found. [Media Selangor, 21 August 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390837) [Selangor Assembly, 2025 sitting (indexed excerpt)][r19]
- Google said it was working toward replenishing 120% of freshwater consumed in Malaysia by 2030 and had begun a Taman Aman lake project with the Global Environment Centre and MBPJ. It also said solar power-purchase agreements led by Shizen and TotalEnergies would add 50 MW of clean energy to the grid. These are company statements reported by the state media outlet, not site-level water-consumption or supply measurements. [Media Selangor, 21 August 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390837)
- On noise, Google’s infrastructure principal told Media Selangor that its data-centre noise “typically” needs to be within 50–65 decibels and that the company tests it regularly. He did not specify the measurement location, weighting, or whether this range is for Elmina. Treat it as a company statement, not a verified Elmina boundary level or replacement for DOE receiver-based criteria. [Media Selangor, 20 August 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/08/390529)

Analyst's read: the reviewed material does not disclose Elmina’s IT MW or grid maximum demand; detailed evaporative-cooling design and site water volumes; whether the dedicated pipeline's water is potable, raw, or reclaimed; or a Google or SDP commitment to use reclaimed water at the site. An indexed state excerpt says reclaimed water is encouraged but not mandatory. [SDP Elmina release][r4] [SDP IAR 2025][r7] [Google 2026 Environmental Report][r13] [Selangor Assembly answer][r5] [Selangor Assembly answer on reclaimed water (snippet-only)][r19]

## National and Selangor picture

- SDP’s 2025 Integrated Annual Report cites DC Byte/Knight Frank market research estimating 65 operating Malaysian data centres with 1,243.8 MW of IT capacity in 2025. It attributes 37 centres and 171.3 MW to Klang Valley, and 22 centres and 1,069.6 MW to Johor. This is a market estimate cited by SDP, not a government census. [SDP IAR 2025][r7]
- TNB’s 4QFY2025 briefing reported 56 secured data-centre projects with 7.5 GW cumulative maximum demand, 35 projects “in system” with 4.5 GW, and 850 MW actual load utilisation at December 2025. These are pipeline and load figures, not a count of operating facilities. [TNB 4QFY2025 analyst briefing][r8]
- MIDA projects Malaysian data-centre power demand above 5,000 MW by 2035 in an article dated 24 March 2025. [MIDA, “Powering Up: Navigating the Energy Crossroads of Malaysia’s Data Centre Boom”][r32]
- An indexed federal parliamentary answer says that, based on planning approvals through December 2025, projected 2025–2030 water demand for Selangor, Johor, and Negeri Sembilan was 396.65 million litres/day; it also confirms SPAN issued its data-centre water guideline effective 1 September 2025. The parliamentary PDF could not be opened, so this row is snippet-only. [Parliament written answer, 3 March 2026][r20]
- Selangor’s Assembly reported 39 operating centres with 31 operators and 13 under construction in a 14 November 2025 answer. An indexed separate state answer says maximum power demand from Selangor DC users was estimated at 1,000 MW as of September 2024 and would be taken up over five to ten years based on user declarations; that is potential demand, not measured operational load. The Assembly pages could not be opened, so these values are snippet-only. [Selangor Assembly, November 2025][r33] [Selangor Assembly, September 2024 estimate][r34]
- In August 2026, Selangor reported expected future data-centre water demand of about 632 MLD across 39 projects approved during 2021–2025. This is a state forecast and uses a different scope from the federal 396.65 MLD three-state 2025–2030 projection; the figures should not be added or treated as direct comparisons. [Bernama report of Selangor’s statement][r23] [Parliament written answer][r20]
- Johor policy became more restrictive during 2026. In March, Bernama reported that Johor would approve only Tier 3 and Tier 4 facilities using air cooling and would stop Tier 1 and Tier 2 approvals; in October, the state’s public formulation emphasised minimal water and green technology. These are reported policy statements, not formal instruments located in this review; the October report was available as a search excerpt only. [Bernama, 10 March 2026](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2532596) [Bernama, 13 October 2026][r31]

Analyst's read: national pipeline figures, utility maximum demand, market-research IT capacity, and water forecasts describe different stages and measurement bases. They show rapid planned growth but cannot be summed into one national “operational footprint” number without a reconciled dataset. [SDP IAR 2025][r7] [TNB briefing][r8] [Parliament written answer][r20]
