# Struggling local businesses in new townships

Evidence pack on struggling shops and small businesses in new townships, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 49 claims: 25 confirmed, 11 corrected, 9 removed and 4 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings; scores are the worker's, before our own ranking.

## One-line problem

- **Analyst's read:** Shop supply may outpace demand in a new township, while home businesses may struggle to reach customers. The public sources reviewed here do not show shop-unit vacancy, lease duration or tenant survival for SDP townships, so local severity remains unproven. NAPIC reports unsold developer inventory and occupancy in larger commercial buildings; DOSM reports enterprise-level statistics. ([NAPIC H1 2025][r1]; [DOSM Business Demography 2025][r2]; [SDP Integrated Annual Report 2025][r3])

## How big and how bad

- NAPIC counted **5,699 completed, CCC-issued shops worth RM4.95 billion** as unsold overhang in H1 2025. The unit count fell **3.4% year-on-year** and **1.4%** from H2 2024. More than **3,000 units (54.0%)** had been on the market for **5–10 years**. This is unsold developer inventory, not rental vacancy. NAPIC's H1 2025 report says the statistical definition took effect on **1 January 2003** and covers units launched for sale on or after **1 January 1997** that remain unsold for more than **nine months** after launch. NAPIC's 2023 explainer separately lists an overhang threshold of **more than five unsold units**. ([NAPIC H1 2025 report][r1]; [NAPIC 2023 explainer][r4])
- DOSM's release dated **29 September 2026** reports enterprise deaths of **28,924 (3.9%) in 2024** and **39,418 (5.2%) in 2025**. For 2025, Selangor's death rate was **5.6%**, Services represented **79.8%** of deaths, and the five-year survival rate for enterprises established in 2020 and surviving to 2025 was **72.5%**. These are enterprise statistics, not shop-unit closures or an SME-only series. ([DOSM, Business Demography 2025][r2])
- Malay Mail's **26 January 2026** opinion article attributes Q3 2025 NAPIC stock figures of **716 “Shop”** and **7,971 “Shop Office”** units to Selangor. These are existing-stock counts, not vacancy counts. The NAPIC Q3 snapshot and the opened dashboard view did not independently confirm those rows. ([Malay Mail][r5]; [NAPIC Q3 2025 snapshot][r6]; [NAPIC property-stock dashboard][r7])
- **Analyst's read:** Owners, tenants, workers, home-based sellers and residents could be affected if local shops are inactive. The sources reviewed do not provide a public series for shop closures or vacancy duration in SDP townships.

## Trend and triggers

- NAPIC's unsold completed shop count fell in H1 2025, while DOSM's national enterprise deaths rose in 2025. These are different measures and periods; they do not establish a local relationship. No H1 2026 shop-overhang figure is used here. ([NAPIC H1 2025][r1]; [DOSM][r2])
- NAPIC reported H1 2026 occupancy of **77.9% nationally** and **79.2% in Selangor** for shopping complexes that include shopping centres, arcades and hypermarkets. This is not an ordinary neighbourhood-shoplot vacancy measure. ([NAPIC H1 2026 Commercial Building Occupancy and Space Availability Report][r8])
- The MySST Background page, last updated **19 July 2026**, lists rental and leasing services at **6% from 1 January 2026**. The separate 2025 FAQ still says **8% from 1 July 2025**. Its **RM500,000** threshold is for service-provider registration, and its small-business tenant example is not a general tenant exemption rule. Treat the FAQ rate as inconsistent with the later Background page. LHDN's timeline, updated **30 August 2026**, exempts taxpayers with annual turnover below **RM3 million**; its “up to RM5 million” cohort date is **1 January 2026**, so the effective cohort is RM3 million to RM5 million. No reviewed source links these tax changes to shop closures. ([MySST Background](https://mysst.customs.gov.my/background/); [MySST FAQ][r9]; [LHDN e-Invoice timeline][r10])
- **Analyst's read:** No seasonal or event trigger surfaced in the sources reviewed. This was not an exhaustive search.

## Who suffers and who owns the fix

- **Analyst's read:** Inactive shops could reduce nearby services and trading opportunities. Residents, workers, tenants and small business owners are plausible affected groups, but the reviewed sources do not measure local impacts.
- Local authorities can regulate and license specified trades and activities under Act 171 and local by-laws. The exact permit depends on the activity and local rules. ([Local Government Act 1976, Act 171][r11])
- Act 757 assigns joint management bodies duties to maintain and manage buildings or land and common property under section 21. Management corporations have the corresponding duty under section 59. Those cited sections do not assign tenant recruitment or business-survival duties. ([Strata Management Act 2013, Act 757][r12])
- **Analyst's read:** SDP's direct levers depend on whether it owns or manages the commercial space or programme. Verify each pilot site's ownership and decision rights; do not assume SDP controls every shoplot.

## SDP's exposure and lever

- SDP's Integrated Annual Report 2025 reports **27 active townships** across the Klang Valley, Negeri Sembilan and Johor. Examples include City of Elmina, Bandar Bukit Raja, Serenia City, Nilai Impian and Bandar Ainsdale. The report does not provide a township-level shop-vacancy rate. ([SDP Integrated Annual Report 2025][r3])
- In a release dated **11 January 2025**, SDP said Elmina Lakeside Mall had been operational since **August 2024**, had **99% occupancy**, drew more than **180,000 opening-week visitors**, and averaged **640,000 monthly visitors**. These are company-reported mall figures; they do not show effects on nearby shoplots. ([SDP Elmina Lakeside Mall release][r13])
- SDP's **2025** Economic Empowerment Programme Phase 2 graduated **39 entrepreneurs**. SDP reported **242% average income growth**, **79%** with higher monthly earnings averaging **RM7,643**, a **110%** average increase in business knowledge and **87%** expanding their digital presence. SDP's release includes a home-based food entrepreneur from Bandar Bukit Raja; these results do not measure shop retention. ([SDP, “Advances Inclusive Growth Through Targeted CSR Programmes,” 19 December 2025][r14])
- **Analyst's read:** The best documented fit is SDP's Community Experience pillar and its entrepreneur-support work. SDP's Sustainability Strategy and Roadmap 2030 names Community Experience as one of four pillars. A Planet benefit remains a hypothesis until a pilot shows better use of existing space. ([SDP Sustainability Strategy and Roadmap 2030][r15]; [SDP EEP release][r14])

## What already exists

- SDP's EEP builds micro-entrepreneur capacity. Gamuda Land's Business Get Set Go offers capital grants and rental subsidies; a **20 February 2025** article said it had received **more than 100 applications** and had a **RM1.8 million** grant pool. ([SDP EEP release][r14]; [Gamuda Land, 20 February 2025][r16])
- EcoWorld's **14 September 2024** duduk Fun Run and Bazaar at Eco Majestic included **over 40 vendors**, including **17 local brands**. This is an event example, not evidence of an ongoing tenant-retention programme. ([EcoWorld][r17])
- KPKT says MyKiosk provided **3,189 kiosks in 2023** across **114 local authorities** and added **4,054 kiosks in 2024** across **100 local authorities**. KPKT reported July 2025 fill rates of **91.75%** for MyKiosk 1.0 and **86.78%** for MyKiosk 2.0. In February 2026, KPKT said local authorities must conduct demand studies before installation, report performance quarterly and consider moving underused kiosks. Its February 2026 release also described **13 MyKiosk@Kampus units** at Universiti Utara Malaysia. These are kiosk results, not shoplot occupancy rates. ([KPKT, 1 February 2026][r18]; [KPKT, 11 February 2026][r19])
- Maybank advertises **over 30,000 merchants** on Sama-Sama Lokal and **0% commission**. This supports online discovery and ordering; it does not establish physical occupancy. ([Maybank Sama-Sama Lokal][r20])
- A page branded as Lotus's Vendor System, Orange Box, advertises short-term and long-term space, pop-ups and shop sharing. The current page rendered placeholder-like listing names, so it confirms the offer categories but not live inventory, uptake or occupancy impact. ([Lotus's Orange Box](https://carrefour.orangebox.com.my/))
- England's High Street Rental Auctions guidance lets local authorities start an auction process when vacancy and local-benefit conditions are met, including continuous vacancy for **12 months** or at least **366 days within 24 months**. This applies to England, not Malaysia. ([UK MHCLG, updated 5 June 2025][r21])
- The reviewed sources show training, grants, events, kiosks, online discovery and space-sharing offers. None reviewed reports SDP unit-level vacancy or lease-tenure data, or an evaluation of tenant retention. First establish which units are inactive, why and for how long.

## Hackathon and startup prior

- **None found** in a bounded search for Malaysian hackathon projects, winners or startups specifically focused on township shop vacancy, short tenancy or micro-retailer customer access. Searches included English queries about shoplot-vacancy hackathons, vacancy-focused startups and developer retail-support programmes. This is a search result, not proof that no such project exists.
- SDP's own 2026 hackathon is still in progress as of **16 October 2026**. Its announcement says the programme runs from September to December, with a grand finale on **14 November 2026** and winner announcements at an Innovation Day in December. It is not yet a prior winner. ([SDP University Hackathon announcement][r22])
- Related commercial activity is already present through developer grants and rent support, public kiosks, events, online marketplaces and space-sharing offers. The intervention field is broader than the hackathon search suggests.

## Real data for a prototype

- **NAPIC status and occupancy:** H1 2025 overhang and H1 2026 commercial-building occupancy PDFs opened. They cover unsold developer stock, offices and shopping complexes, not rented neighbourhood shop vacancy. The property-stock dashboard loaded, but its visible page did not confirm the Q3 2025 Selangor rows. The reviewed pages exposed no API or reuse licence. NAPIC's PDF copyright notice reserves reproduction absent prior written permission. ([H1 2025 status report][r1]; [property-stock dashboard][r7]; [H1 2026 occupancy report][r8])
- **DOSM Business Demography 2025:** the release provides national, sector and state enterprise statistics. DOSM's terms authorize free reuse and adaptation subject to conditions, including attribution and no implication that DOSM endorses the analysis. It has no township shop detail. ([Release][r2]; [DOSM Terms of Use][r23])
- **OpenStreetMap and Overpass:** mapped streets and shop points can help build a fieldwork map, but coverage varies and points do not show occupancy. OSM data uses ODbL attribution rules. The Overpass manual was reviewed; no query was run in this check. ([OSM attribution guideline][r24]; [Overpass API manual][r25])
- No public SDP township source for unit-level vacancy, rent, lease duration or closure was found in this review. MPSepang's No. 4/2025 premises-vacancy page appeared in official search results, but the page failed to fetch; treat it as **snippet-only** and not as private-shoplot data. Seek anonymised SDP lease and footfall data, council licence counts and field verification. ([MPSepang notice][r26])

## Pilot sketch

- **Proposal:** Consider a Bandar Bukit Raja commercial row linked to SDP's EEP. Verify ownership and secure access from SDP, owners and the local authority before choosing the site.
- Baseline: classify units as occupied, closed-but-leased, for rent or sale, under renovation, or unknown. With consent, record rent bands, vacancy duration, business type, operating days, footfall and home-business participation. Repeat after activation and track continuity separately from event attendance.
- Compare with a similar row if feasible. Measure repeat visits and business continuity, not event attendance alone.
- Cost drivers to scope include fieldwork, opt-in footfall counts, data handling, local permits, insurance, signs and rent or fit-out support. No public SDP pilot cost estimate was found.

## Kill risks

- **Analyst's read:** National unsold inventory and enterprise deaths do not prove local vacancy or show that malls or e-commerce caused it. A low local baseline would weaken the case.
- Owner consent, strata rules and local licensing may limit a pilot. Confirm the approval path with the relevant owners and local authority before activation.
- Protect rent, revenue and customer data through consent and aggregate reporting.
- Events may raise footfall without increasing sales or business continuity. A directory alone may not show measurable impact.

## Scores

- **Analyst's read — Pain: 4/5** — national overhang and enterprise-death indicators are substantial, but local vacancy is unmeasured. ([NAPIC][r1]; [DOSM][r2])
- **Analyst's read — SDP lever: 4/5** — SDP reports 27 active townships and an entrepreneur programme; site ownership limits reach. ([SDP IAR][r3]; [SDP EEP release][r14])
- **Analyst's read — Evidence available: 3/5** — national and state series are useful, but street-level tenancy data was not found in the reviewed sources.
- **Analyst's read — Market density: 4/5** — grants, kiosks, marketplaces, events and pop-ups already operate. ([Gamuda Land][r16]; [KPKT][r19]; [Maybank][r20])
- **Analyst's read — Hackathon prior: 1/5** — no focused winner surfaced in the bounded English-language search.
