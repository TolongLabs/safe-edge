# Township drowning incidents, Selangor and Kuala Lumpur, 2023 to 2026

Deep-dive evidence on drowning and near-drowning in township water bodies in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur, 2023 to 2026, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 45 claims: 31 confirmed, 9 corrected, 0 removed and 5 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings.

## Summary

- This source review found **21 incident clusters** in scope: **18** reports with at least one confirmed drowning death, **1** suspected drowning death with cause pending, and **2** no-death near-drowning events. These are counts of the rows below, not an official census; every row links to its reporting source.
- Water bodies included monsoon and open drains, drain-connected rivers, retention ponds and lakes, an artificial township lake, an ex-mining lake, and a river-excavation site with standing water. The Shah Alam site was part of the Selangor Maritime Gateway project, not a flood-mitigation worksite ([drain and river][r1], [retention pond](https://bernama.com/bm/news.php?id=2274175), [Tasik Biru][r2], [artificial lake][r3], [official Shah Alam clarification][r4]).
- Children and teenagers appear in drain, fishing, and lake incidents; adults also died or nearly drowned at township lakes and rivers ([Ukay Perdana][r5], [Kajang][r6], [Pandan][r7], [Bandar Tun Hussein Onn][r8]).
- Reported activities include retrieving a ball, fishing, swimming, walking home, falling or being swept into water, and a vehicle entering a lake. Several reports give an emergency-call or recovery time rather than the exact time the person entered the water ([Kota Perdana][r1], [Mahkota Cheras][r9], [Tasik Biru vehicle rescue][r10]).
- Heavy rain or a sudden water surge is explicitly reported in the Ukay Perdana, Kota Perdana, Bandar Puncak Alam, and Bandar Mahkota Cheras incidents. Rain is not stated in most other reports ([Ukay Perdana][r11], [Kota Perdana][r1], [Bandar Puncak Alam][r12], [Bandar Mahkota Cheras — Bernama search-result text only][r13]). The Mahkota Cheras weather detail comes from that search-result text only.

## Patterns

- Analyst's read: Drain incidents are a recurring hazard pattern in this review. Reports describe children being swept from drains into rivers or ponds, sometimes several kilometres from the entry point ([Ukay Perdana][r11], [Kajang][r6], [Puncak Alam][r42], [Mahkota Cheras][r9]).
- Analyst's read: Young victims are prominent in the drain and recreation reports. Adults also appear in lake and river incidents, so the risk in township water bodies is not limited to children ([Kota Perdana][r1], [Pandan][r7], [Kota Kemuning][r21], [Kepong][r34]).
- Analyst's read: Reported activities range from play and ball retrieval to fishing and swimming. A few reports describe accidental falls or a vehicle entering a lake; reports do not consistently establish what happened immediately before entry into the water ([Kota Perdana][r1], [Sinar Alam][r25], [Bandar Botanik][r3]).
- Analyst's read: Afternoon and evening response times recur, but reports often provide the call or recovery time instead of the incident time. Some cases occurred or were discovered in the morning or around midnight, so these reports do not support a reliable time-of-day risk estimate ([Taman Aman](https://bernama.com/bm/news.php?id=2274175), [Kota Kemuning][r21], [Tasik Biru rescue][r10]).
- Analyst's read: Rain was not reported consistently enough to test a weather relationship. Heavy rain or a surge is specifically described in four reports, while most incident reports do not state weather ([Ukay Perdana][r11], [Kota Perdana][r1], [Puncak Alam][r12], [Mahkota Cheras][r13]).
- Analyst's read: Tasik Biru Kundang is the clearest repeat water-body site in the included reports: fatal incidents were reported there in 2023 and 2024, followed by a vehicle rescue in 2026. That is a repeat-site signal, not an estimate of incidence or risk. Kosmo's 2026 retrospective also cites a 2018 drowning and reports a fire chief calling for shoreline fencing; that is historical context, not an additional incident in this review ([2023][r2], [2024][r24], [2026][r10]).
- Analyst's read: No school-holiday concentration is established by this media review. The full incident set was not mapped consistently against the Ministry of Education's group-B calendars for each year ([2023/24 calendar][r43], [2024/25 calendar][r44], [2025/26 calendar][r45], [2026 calendar][r46]).

## Official statistics found

- The Ministry of Housing and Local Government's MyKPKT selected-statistics report, using Fire and Rescue Department (JBPM) data, records **293 national drowning-response cases, 282 deaths, 18 injured, and 88 survivors in 2023**; and **279 cases, 277 deaths, 4 injured, and 81 survivors in 2024**. Its state breakdown gives Selangor **28 cases, 30 deaths, 0 injured, and 12 survivors in 2023**, and **29 cases, 29 deaths, 0 injured, and 5 survivors in 2024**. Kuala Lumpur is shown as **3 cases and 3 deaths in each year**, with no injured or survivors recorded. Its water-body table lists **171 river/drainage cases in each year** (164 deaths in 2023; 180 in 2024), and **25 lake/mining-lake cases in 2023** (27 deaths) and **26 in 2024** (25 deaths). The report's scope is incidents attended by JBPM, not all drowning events or only township water bodies. ([KPKT MyKPKT selected statistics, pp. 82 and 84][r47])
- A **16 Oct 2026** report by _The Star_, citing the JBPM Director-General, says that from **Jan 2023 through Aug 2026**, Malaysia recorded **1,020 drowning incidents and 1,001 deaths**; **371 deaths were among children and teenagers** (49 aged 0–6 and 322 aged 7–19). It reports **889 male and 112 female deaths**. Reported water-body categories were rivers and irrigation canals (616), beaches (249), lakes and mining ponds (82), waterfalls and rapids (57), and culverts (16). These are nationwide figures across water-body types, not township-specific counts. ([The Star, JBPM figures][r48])
- Not found: a public official Selangor-only or Kuala Lumpur-only drowning table for **2025 or 2026** in the official sources reviewed. Searches covered KPKT/JBPM and DOSM material; the KPKT state breakdown located ends at **2024**. ([KPKT report][r47])

## Gaps

- Bandar Botanik lead: verified **one** reported drowning of a **15-year-old boy** in an unnamed artificial lake on **1 Oct 2026**. Not found: reports of several teenagers drowning there, a named lake, or a separate river or retention-pond incident. English and Malay searches included “Bandar Botanic drowned teenagers retention pond river”, “Bandar Botanik lemas remaja tasik”, and related date and location combinations. ([Bernama English report][r3], [Bernama radio report][r41])
- A woman in her 70s was reported found in a rainwater retention pond in Bukit Bandaraya, Shah Alam, on **7 Jan 2026**. The Astro Awani and NST pages could not be opened; their search-result snippets say the cause was pending laboratory results. This lead is **snippet-only** and is not counted as a verified drowning. ([NST][r49], [Astro Awani][r50])
- A male body was found in Tasik Biru, Kuang, in **March 2026**, but the report reviewed did not establish drowning as the cause. Not counted. ([Bernama radio](https://bernama.com/radio/news.php?id=2536386))
- Not found: consistent incident-time, rainfall, township-boundary, and exact water-body-name fields across reports. For many incidents, news reports give only the emergency-call or body-recovery time; weather is often absent. The Bandar Botanik lake's name also remains unreported in the sources reviewed.
- This is a media-based incident log, not a complete case register. Some reports conflict on age, some use a broad place name rather than a named township, and some deaths are still under investigation. Exact coordinates and developer attribution were unavailable for many sites.
