# Children drowning in township waters

Evidence pack on children drowning in township drains, ponds and lakes, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 119 claims: 65 confirmed, 49 corrected, 1 removed and 4 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings; scores are the worker's, before our own ranking.

## One-line problem

- Bernama reported that two brothers, aged 12 and 14, were swept from a large drain in Kajang while retrieving a ball. The 14-year-old was found in a retention pond; the other boy was found in Sungai Merbau, two kilometres from where they were swept away. This is an observed drain-to-water incident, but the article does not map the hydraulic connection or identify the asset custodian ([Bernama, 6 Dec 2025](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2499672)).
- Analyst's read: Public drowning tables are aggregated by state or incident type. They do not provide a public record linking each event to its exact site, drainage path, and maintainer. An accessible or unguarded drain is not established by the Kajang report.
- For drains and watercourses under a local authority's control in Peninsular Malaysia, Act 133 assigns maintenance, repair, clearing, and cleansing duties to the authority. JPS says the state government decides whether JPS or the local authority maintains an OSD or detention pond. Check custody and handover records for the particular asset ([Act 133, ss. 53–54][r1]; [JPS FAQ](https://wplb.water.gov.my/en/faq)).

## How big and how bad

- JBPM reported 371 child and teen drowning deaths nationwide from 2023 through August 2026. This total comprises 49 deaths among children aged 0–6 and 322 among those aged 7–19 ([The Star, 16 Oct 2026][r2]).
- JBPM recorded 1,020 drowning incidents across all ages from 2023 through August 2026, resulting in 1,001 deaths. Reported incident locations were rivers and irrigation canals (616), beaches (249), lakes and mining ponds (82), waterfalls and rapids (57), and culverts (16). These are incidents, not child-only counts ([The Star, 16 Oct 2026][r2]).
- KPKT's December 2025 time-series report records 29 Selangor drowning cases in 2024. Its table is aggregated by state and outcome; it gives no event coordinates or age breakdown ([KPKT, Dec 2025][r3]). A six-year-old boy was found drowned in Petaling Jaya's Taman Aman flood-retention pond on 27 February 2024 ([Malay Mail/Bernama][r4]). In Kajang, the 12- and 14-year-old brothers were swept from a drain; one was found in a retention pond and the other in a river ([Bernama, 6 Dec 2025](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2499672)).
- The full NST page for “1 child or teen drowns every 3 days” could not be opened. NST's accessible summary associates the headline with 619 recorded incidents since 2021, not 619 deaths. Treat the rate as snippet-only and do not report 619 as a fatality count ([NST article][r5]; [NST summary, 27 Jun 2026](https://www.nst.com.my/newssummary/1459588)).
- Older context: a deputy minister said 1,340 people died from drowning in Malaysia during 2018–2022. The 268-per-year average is calculated as 1,340 divided by five ([The Star, 12 Jun 2023][r6]).

## Trend and triggers

- All-age incidents fell from 293 in 2023 to 279 in 2024 and 270 in 2025. The 178 incidents recorded from January through August 2026 are partial-year data. Analyst's read: these all-age totals do not establish a rising or falling child-specific trend ([The Star, 16 Oct 2026][r2]).
- Selangor reported two fatal drain incidents in 2026: a 10-year-old boy in Bandar Puncak Alam, whose body was found in Sungai Buloh after he fell during heavy rain, and a nine-year-old girl in Bandar Mahkota Cheras, whose body was found in Sungai Langat after she fell into a drain. The state said it would review high-risk drains ([NST, 30 Apr 2026][r7]; [Sinar Harian, 4 May 2026][r8]; [RTM, 4 May 2026][r9]).
- The Puncak Alam case occurred during heavy rain; in the separate Kajang incident, boys were swept by strong currents. SDP's 2025 Integrated Annual Report says it conducts routine drainage inspections and desilting and maintains flood-related infrastructure in handed-over areas ahead of and during monsoon seasons. It also describes risk assessments, flood monitoring and emergency protocols, wetlands, natural waterways, and retention areas ([NST, 30 Apr 2026][r7]; [Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2499672); [SDP IAR 2025, published 20 Apr 2026][r10]). Analyst's read: these records make rain and flow useful pilot conditions to measure; they do not establish that SDP's inspections are drowning-specific.
- Analyst's read: A targeted government and news search found no drowning-specific Malaysian law introduced in 2025–2026. This is a search result, not a complete legal opinion. The design reference checked here is JPS's MSMA second edition (2012), alongside Act 133 for local-authority-controlled drains ([JPS MSMA][r11]; [Act 133][r1]).

## Who suffers and who owns the fix

- Analyst's read: Assess child-accessible water edges near homes, schools, parks, and pedestrian routes. The cited cases establish incidents at a drain and a pond, not relative risk across those settings. Cases include ball retrieval in Kajang and a 16-year-old student reportedly falling into a drain near a college entrance in Bangi; the Bangi victim was swept to a nearby retention area ([Bernama](https://bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2499672); [Media Selangor/Bernama, 25 Jun 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/06/381326)).
- Act 133 sections 53–54 cover maintenance and clearing of surface-water and storm-water drains, culverts, gutters, and watercourses under local-authority control. The Act applies in Peninsular Malaysia, subject to its state-extension provisions; it does not, by itself, show that every private pond is council-maintained ([Act 133][r1]).
- JPS says the state government assigns OSD and detention-pond maintenance to either JPS or a local authority. Under the Strata Management Act 2013, JMB and management-corporation duties include maintaining common property. Whether a particular pond or drain is common property must be checked against the strata plan and asset records ([JPS FAQ](https://wplb.water.gov.my/en/faq); [Strata Management Act 2013, ss. 21 and 59][r12]).
- Analyst's read: Resident supervision and reporting can support prevention, but they do not establish or replace the legal duty for a particular asset. Identify the owner, controlling authority, and any handover terms before assigning responsibility.

[r12]: https://www.ppj.gov.my/storage/7465/Act-757--Strata-Management-Act-2013.pdf

## SDP's exposure and lever

- SDP's 2025 Sustainability Report lists Sakura Lake and the Forest and Community Parks in Elmina West, the Wetland Townpark in Bandar Bukit Raja 2, and the Wetland Nature Park in Bandar Bukit Raja 3. These are relevant water and green-space assets. Analyst's read: the report does not identify any of them as a drowning hotspot ([SDP Sustainability Report 2025][r13]).
- SDP's IAR describes flood-risk assessments, flood monitoring and emergency-response protocols in high-risk townships, drain cleaning and desilting, maintenance of flood infrastructure in handed-over areas, and nature-based stormwater measures. It says inspections and desilting occur ahead of and during monsoon seasons ([SDP IAR 2025][r10]).
- Analyst's read: No drowning-specific programme appeared in the two SDP reports reviewed. SDP can consider water safety in design and upkeep; confirm who controls each asset after handover before proposing a pilot.

## What already exists

- Malaysia's MSMA second edition recommends detention-pond side slopes flatter than 1V:6H. It says slopes steeper than 1V:4H may require a fence or rail, and that rails, fences, anti-vortex devices, and grates should be provided where necessary. It also recommends signage at stormwater facilities to educate the public. These are design guidelines and conditional safety measures, not a blanket fence rule ([JPS MSMA, 2012][r14]). JBPM's Jom Belajar Renang programme aims to reach 50,000 students between 2026 and 2030; 19,616 had participated in 2026 as of The Star's 16 October report ([The Star, 16 Oct 2026][r2]).
- Malaysian research prototypes also exist. A 2023 UTHM paper describes an early-stage wearable bracelet for swimmer health monitoring and tracking. A 2025 paper by Universiti Teknikal Malaysia Melaka authors describes an IoT child-water-safety prototype with pulse, water-level, and GPS sensors and guardian alerts; its testing used simulated near-drowning conditions. Neither paper establishes a deployed township product ([UTHM, 3 May 2023][r15]; [IJRISS, published 1 Nov 2025][r16]).
- Lynxight and AngelEye describe camera and AI systems for lifeguarded or remotely monitored swimming pools. ISO 20380:2017 covers computer-vision systems for detecting drowning accidents in public swimming pools; ISO says the standard was reviewed and confirmed in 2023. It does not set a standard for open canals or stormwater ponds ([Lynxight](https://www.lynxight.com/); [AngelEye](https://angeleye.tech/us/us-technology/); [ISO 20380][r17]).
- Open-water technology exists outside the pool market. SAFE SWIM's vendor site lists deployments at Tainan Golden Coast beach and Taoyuan Yunei Creek in Taiwan. The UK Defence and Security Accelerator says Zelim's AI person-in-water detection is used on commercial ships, offshore energy infrastructure, and ports. Analyst's read: these are adjacent examples, not evidence of a township-drain deployment ([SAFE SWIM][r18]; [UK DASA, 25 Oct 2024][r19]).
- WHO's 2024 guidance includes barriers to prevent child access to water, safe places away from water for preschool children, swimming and water-safety skills for school-aged children, rescue and resuscitation training, public awareness, safe boating rules, and flood-risk management ([WHO, 13 Dec 2024][r20]).
- Analyst's read: The opportunity is site-level evidence about hazards, asset custody, and whether barriers or warnings work during rain and at night. Existing pool and open-water systems do not prove that a camera or sensor is the best township intervention.

## Real data for a prototype

- MyKPKT drowning statistics 2020–2024: public PDF confirmed. It provides state and incident-type tables, including 29 Selangor cases in 2024. The tables reviewed contain no event coordinates or age field; the reuse licence is not stated ([KPKT PDF, published Dec 2025][r3]).
- JPS Public Infobanjir: its page says it gathers real-time rainfall and water levels from nearly 200 hydrology stations nationwide. The trend page exposes state, district, and station filters and CSV/PDF exports. I opened the pages and filters but did not test a target station or export; reuse terms are unclear ([overview][r24]; [trend page][r25]).
- data.gov.my / MetMalaysia weather API: the docs describe daily updated seven-day general forecasts and live warnings, with no API token required. This check did not test a live response or establish reuse terms. Analyst's read: location forecasts are not asset-level pond alerts ([weather API][r26]; [quickstart](https://developer.data.gov.my/quickstart)).
- Analyst's read: Searches did not find a public geocoded child-drowning or near-miss feed in data.gov.my, DOSM, or MyKPKT. A site baseline would need SDP asset GIS, handover records, maintenance tickets, complaints, and incident logs, subject to access approval.

## Pilot sketch

- Analyst's read: Candidate site is one Elmina residential route beside a drain or water body, after SDP confirms asset custody. The report documents local water assets, not a known hotspot. Frame the pilot as a safety and evidence audit, not a sensor deployment by default ([SDP Sustainability Report 2025][r13]).
- Analyst's read: Map child access, drain-to-water links, outlets, slopes, covers, barriers, signs, lighting, rain and flow exposure, and emergency access. Collect incident and near-miss logs, complaints, repair times, and after-school or weekend footfall. Track hazards closed, warning comprehension, and repair time; deaths alone are too rare for a short-pilot outcome.
- Analyst's read: Cost drivers include survey and GIS work, engineering and resident time, repairs to covers or barriers, signs, possible monitoring, permissions, upkeep, insurance, and evaluation. Do not state an RM estimate without site scope and quotes.

## Kill risks

- Analyst's read: A pilot may stall if SDP, the local authority, JPS, a JMB or MC, and landowners disagree about access or custody. Act 133 covers local-authority-controlled drains; JPS says state government assigns OSD and detention-pond maintenance between JPS and local authorities ([Act 133][r1]; [JPS FAQ](https://wplb.water.gov.my/en/faq)).
- Analyst's read: Avoid identifiable child video and publication of victim locations. Flood protection may outrank drowning in budgets. A sensor-only pitch may compare poorly with barriers, maintenance, swimming skills, or rescue measures.
- Analyst's read: SDP must confirm a site, asset owner, baseline, and sponsor before the national drowning figures establish a commercial case for this pilot.
