Evidence pack on amenity lag in SDP townships, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). ## Summary

- **Analyst’s read:** The sources show several phases where homes were marketed or launched before all public schools and mass transit were operating; they do not establish that every buyer moved in before services, or give comparable first-occupation dates for most townships. [1][2][13][20][31]
- In City of Elmina, SK Denai Alam began operating in February 2025 and classes at SJK(C) Regent Elmina began in January 2026. The SJK(C) school was not the Tamil school relocated from Gemencheh; these are different schools. SDP’s 2024 release estimated SK Denai Alam ready by 2026 and SMK Denai Alam by 2028. [4][5][8][51][53]
- In Bandar Bukit Raja, SK Bukit Raja was under construction in 2025 with completion expected in 2027; the secondary school was still in land procurement. LRT3 service began on 29 June 2026. The Bukit Raja station was a future construction item with no opening date confirmed. [14][15][19][52]
- Reports document crowded classes in Nilai, Puncak Alam and Shah Alam, and a reported secondary-school gap in Bukit Raja. Reported school-run travel times or distances specific to SDP-township families are scarce; one Elmina resident’s opinion piece describes peak traffic taking up to an hour to travel roughly 1 km to an exit. [16][34][35][36][41][42]
- **Analyst’s read:** PLANMalaysia guidance sets facility catchment, land and access benchmarks. It does not state a general deadline requiring public schools or clinics to open by first home occupation. [44]

## Overcrowding and travel burden

- **Shah Alam and Setia Alam:** KPM told Parliament in February 2025 that the standard class capacity was 30 pupils, with consideration up to 35 based on relevant factors. KPM also said RM100 million was allocated for IBS cabin classrooms under the 2025 plan and named six Shah Alam schools, including SK Setia Alam, for approval. [34] In January 2026, an MP told Parliament that some Form 1 classes in Shah Alam had 50–60 pupils; the Hansard statement did not name schools or provide an official class count. [35]
- **Puncak Alam and nearby schools:** A Selangor Assembly answer reported double shifts and over-capacity at Puncak Alam schools. It listed an approved SK Puncak Alam 3 expansion with 12 classrooms, 2 preschool rooms and 6 PPKI rooms, at RM27 million. It also listed 14 modular cabins across SK Desa Aman, SMK Saujana Utama and SK Seri Pristina, at RM1.96 million. Proposed SK Alam Suria and SMK Eco Grandeur projects were still awaiting RMK13 approval in that answer. [36]
- **Puncak Alam health capacity:** The question recorded in a 2023 Selangor Assembly answer described Klinik Desa Puncak Alam as operating from a terrace house and unable to meet demand. The state response proposed a Type 2 KK at Bandar Seri Coalfields under RMK12 RP5, with stated capacity of 800 patients a day. This is a proposal, not evidence of a completed clinic. [37] Another state answer listed planned land reserves in Puncak Alam BP6: six primary-school sites and five clinic sites. These are reserves, not completed facilities. [38]
- **Cyberjaya comparator:** In a 2024 parliamentary answer, KPM described short-term measures for SK Cyberjaya, including redistributing Year 1 pupils to nearby schools and approving 12 additional cabin classrooms. The answer said SK Cyberjaya 2 (36 classrooms) was approved and in procurement, with construction then expected to finish in 2028. These are not SDP townships. [40]
- **Nilai:** The Negeri Sembilan education director identified Nilai Impian, Taman Semarak and Taman Desa Cempaka as school-demand hotspots in Jan 2026, with classes reported at 42–43 pupils. The director said eligible children would receive placement, but might not get their preferred school. A new Desa Casuarina school was reported as approved and expected in 2027. [42]
- **Bukit Raja:** In a Feb 2025 parliamentary statement, an MP said Bandar Bukit Raja had about 30,000 residents, no secondary school, and pupils travelled to SMK Pulau Indah or SMK Bukit Tinggi. These are the MP’s statements; the Hansard gives no measured route distance or travel time. [16]
- **Reported school-run congestion:** In an April 2025 opinion piece, Denai Alam resident Ashraf Abdullah wrote that a roughly 1 km drive from home to a township exit could take up to an hour at peak time. This is a resident’s account, not a traffic survey. [41] The Star reported congestion, blocked roads and queue-cutting complaints around seven schools off Persiaran Raja Muda Musa in Klang in July 2024; the report is a Klang comparator and does not establish that all seven serve Bukit Raja residents. [43]

## Resident voice

- Denai Alam resident Ashraf Abdullah’s April 2025 MalaysiaGazette opinion, “The City of Elmina’s Broken Promise,” criticises external cut-through traffic and describes the peak-hour exit delay noted above. It is an opinion piece and personal account. [41]
- In 2022, the PTA chair for SJK(T) Ladang Ampar Tenang, near the Serenia development, told Malaysiakini that heavy rain and flooding linked to construction made school access difficult for pupils and teachers. The chair said the PTA sought an alternative route about 150 m from the main road and that SDP had orally agreed but had not acted by July 2022. These are attributed PTA claims; the article does not independently establish cause or agreement. [22]
- Klang school communities told The Star in 2024 about blocked access roads, queue cutting and haphazard parking during drop-off and pick-up around seven schools. [43]
- **Analyst’s read:** In the news and official sources searched, I did not find a dated resident report specific to these SDP townships alleging refusal of school intake, or a comparable set of complaints about public clinic access or a township bus service. [16][22][41][43]

## Developer obligations and practice

- PLANMalaysia’s 2022 _Garis Panduan Perancangan Kemudahan Masyarakat_ gives primary-school catchment and capacity modules from 1,250 to 7,500 people, with 6–36 classroom modules, typical sites of 6–8 acres, and access guidance of 800 m or 10 minutes. Its secondary-school table covers catchments from 2,500 to 12,500 people, 12–60 classroom modules, typical sites of 8–12 acres, and access guidance of 1.7 km or 20 minutes. [44]
- For public clinics, the guideline sets clinic types against population catchments and site standards; listed sites range from 1.3 acres for Type 3 to 3 acres for Type 1. It also discusses alternate delivery in existing buildings or vertical/strata settings with relevant agency and local-authority approval. [44]
- The guideline describes planning benchmarks and delivery mechanisms. It identifies KPM and public agencies in school planning and construction, developer involvement in land provision, and several ways to provide clinic space. **Analyst’s read:** the guideline does not state a universal deadline requiring a government school or KK to open before first home occupation; project approval conditions and agency delivery still matter. [44]
- **Early school example:** EcoWorld said it fully subsidised construction of SJK(C) Ladang Grisek at Eco Tropics, with estimated cost RM7.5 million and 30 classrooms. The company reported phase one completion in Jan 2020, 405 pupils and 23 teachers resuming on 1 Mar 2020, and the school’s official opening on 16 Aug 2020. The school was relocated from Ladang Grisek, so this is an early developer-funded relocation/rebuild example, not a newly added school in an entirely unserved area. [45]
- **Shuttle example and cost:** EcoWorld’s 25 Feb 2018 release said the Eco Botanic shuttle was expected to start in March. It said EcoWorld owned and operated the service, which would run seven days a week, 365 days a year, and be free for residents; members of the public could use it with a rider pass and an Eco Boulevard purchase receipt. Proposed stops included nearby supermarkets/hypermarkets, EduCity and the Gelang Patah bus interchange. The release did not disclose operating cost. [46]
- SDP’s 2017 Pagoh release describes shuttle services associated with the education hub, but gives no service start date, fare or operator cost. [31] **Analyst’s read:** the FY2025 SDP Sustainability Report pages reviewed do not establish a standing township-resident bus service. [47]

## What SDP says

- SDP’s 2018 Elmina launch material discussed proposed rail extensions from Sungai Buloh and Subang Jaya. Those were proposals, not an operating in-town rail service. The cited Selangor public-transport master-plan answer is from 2016, not 2020, and its page could not be opened during this check. [11][12]
- SDP said in 2018 that Bandar Bukit Raja would welcome an “upcoming General Hospital”; its release gave no delivery date. The separately documented Hospital Kapar project is at a Bandar Bukit Raja address, was 90.56% complete as at 20 Aug 2026, and had phased operations planned from June 2027. The claimed 50-acre Phase 2 reserve is unsupported by the cited Selangor answer and has been removed. [48][54]
- SDP’s 2018 Serenia launch described Salak Tinggi ERL and nearby schools and hospitals as access/amenities. The release did not give dates for an in-town public school, clinic or bus service. [20]
- SDP’s FY2025 Sustainability Report says it supported 7,894 students with school supplies and programmes, spent RM257,800 upgrading five schools with smart technology for 5,000 users, and provided free health checks for 200 Bandar Bukit Raja residents. These are CSR activities, not a public school or KK delivery schedule. [47]
- SDP’s 2024 integrated report says it supported Poliklinik Primer Sejahtera Pagoh with Yayasan Sime Darby, providing healthcare services to **over 600** residents and students. The report does not identify it as a government Klinik Kesihatan or publish an opening date. [49]
- **Analyst’s read:** in the FY2025 annual and sustainability report sections searched, I did not find a dated, township-by-township public-school, public-KK, resident-shuttle or rail-opening schedule. [47][50]

## Gaps

- First home handover dates remain unverified for all six townships; several rows therefore use the earliest located launch, while BUP’s June 2018 handover remains a target rather than a confirmed event. [1][13][20][23][28][31]
- No dated public Klinik Kesihatan opening was verified for Elmina, Serenia, Ainsdale or Bandar Universiti Pagoh. For Bandar Bukit Raja, Hospital Kapar is a separate public hospital project with phased operations planned from June 2027; this is not a KK. Nilai’s 2018 report gives a target date for a new clinic but not a confirmed opening, and does not place it within Nilai Impian. [9][26][29][32][54]
- Bus route start dates were not found for T772, T729 or MU004; no route start date was found for the other townships. Current route evidence does not show how many residents can reach a stop safely or within the guideline’s access benchmarks. [10][18][33][44]
- Exact opening dates were not found for the proposed Ainsdale terminal, a rail stop inside Elmina or Serenia, or rail access at Nilai and Pagoh. The dates for nearby stations are also incomplete in the SDP sources reviewed. [11][20][27][28][30][31]
- **Analyst’s read:** searches of SDP township pages, launch releases and FY2024–25 reports; KPM/Parliament and Selangor Assembly answers; JKN clinic directories; PLANMalaysia guidance; local authority/transport route pages; and English/Malay news did not locate a consistent dataset linking home occupation dates, school enrolment/capacity, clinic openings, bus frequency and door-to-door travel times. Exact school-run distance/time evidence for families in the six townships remains sparse.
