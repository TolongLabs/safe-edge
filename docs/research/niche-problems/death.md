# Death in the township

Evidence pack on how townships handle death: cemetery land, wakes and bereaved residents, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 55 claims: 22 confirmed, 28 corrected, 1 removed and 4 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings; scores are the worker's, before our own ranking.

## One-line problem

- Analyst's read: Death-related arrangements may require families, neighbours, faith groups, strata managers, funeral providers and public agencies to coordinate. The sources show cemetery-capacity planning and permits for some funeral activity, but they do not measure township-level disruption or handoffs.

## How big and how bad

- Malaysia recorded 199,807 deaths in 2025, up 815 (0.4%) from 198,992 in 2024. The crude death rate stayed at 5.8 per 1,000 population. DOSM released the figures on 15 October 2026. [1] [24]
- DOSM's 11 July 2025 projections say Malaysia became an “Ageing Society” in 2021, when the population aged 65 and over exceeded 7%. DOSM projects the “Aged Society” threshold of more than 14% in 2048. The cited PDF is titled _Population Projections, Malaysia, 2020–2060_. [2]
- A Malay Mail report published on 9 July 2026 quoted June 2023 figures for Kuala Lumpur's Islamic burial grounds: over 70% occupied, with 34,496 plots (about 29%) remaining. The report said those plots were expected to meet needs until around 2032. This is a dated citywide snapshot, not a current plot count. [3]
- Selangor's 2024 cemetery study uses JAIS cemetery figures from 2023. Its table lists Bandar Baru Salak Tinggi cemetery in Sepang with 5,367 plots and an estimated full year of 2027. The inventory also lists sites that were not yet operating; it does not prove a present shortage at every township. [4]
- The Star reported on 7 August 2026 that three cemeteries serving Seri Serdang had an estimated combined capacity of about 2,500 burial plots. The article does not say these are plots remaining. [5]
- Analyst's read: I did not find current non-Muslim capacity totals or township-level counts of wake disruption, parking conflicts, noise or bereavement-access problems in the sources reviewed. The sources do document cemetery-capacity planning, but not local incident frequency.

## Trend and triggers

- Deaths rose 0.4% in 2025. Analyst's read: that is a modest one-year increase; DOSM's ageing projections point to a longer-term planning issue rather than proving a sudden 2025–26 demand spike. [1] [2]
- On 7 August 2026, Selangor said it was identifying government land for future Muslim burial grounds, retaining its policy for large developments to allocate land, and improving the use and management of existing sites. The state said a two-tier burial system might be introduced at new cemeteries, subject to local proposals and state fatwa standards. [5]
- On 8 October 2026, the Dewan Rakyat was told that PLANMalaysia was still finalising national planning guidance for non-Muslim cemeteries, crematoria and columbaria. The draft is intended as a reference for state and local authorities, developers and operators; it was not yet a settled national standard. [6]
- Analyst's read: The targeted searches did not identify an enacted cemetery-specific law change in 2025–26. The sources reviewed instead document capacity forecasts, land planning and permits for funeral activity on roads. [5] [7] [11]

## Who suffers and who owns the fix

- Analyst's read: Bereaved households may face coordination, cost and access burdens; neighbours, building staff and funeral workers may face parking, tent, traffic, noise or unclear-rule issues. The sources reviewed do not provide local incident counts.
- The Local Government Act 1976 gives local authorities a role in burial grounds and crematoria. Section 94 allows them to provide suitable grounds and requires proper maintenance of grounds they provide; it also allows licences for other burial places. Section 95 penalises burial or cremation at places that are neither provided nor licensed, or that have been closed. [7]
- Muslim cemetery planning and management also involve state Islamic authorities and local mosque or kariah committees. PLANMalaysia's guide assigns monitoring and administration to the state Islamic department; Selangor's 2026 statement describes local-authority and kariah roles. [4] [5] [8]
- PLANMalaysia's 2023 planning guide sets out a two-route mechanism. Its summary pairs the on-site cemetery route with developments of 100 hectares (250 acres) and at least 10,000 residents; for developments below 100 hectares and at or below 10,000 residents, it lists a contribution to the Cemetery Trust Fund. The guide says implementation must align with the local authority's development plan, so it is planning guidance rather than a universal statutory land quota by itself. [8]
- The guide sizes cemetery needs using projected population, death rate, grave-lot area and reuse period. Its example is 0.3 acre per 1,000 residents, using a 5-per-1,000 death rate, an 8.36-square-metre average lot including support space and landscaping, and a 30-year reuse period. It does not set one fixed percentage of total township land. [8]
- The 2017 Selangor State Assembly answer's search excerpt says an older guideline required 8 acres of cemetery land for a development of 250 acres or more. Eight acres divided by exactly 250 acres is 3.2%; that percentage is a calculation, not a figure quoted by the answer. Treat the rule as historical, not as today's general requirement. [9]
- MBIP's Strata Management page search excerpt describes JMB and MC duties as managing and maintaining buildings and common property under sections 21 and 59 of the Strata Management Act 2013. The page did not load in the browser. Analyst's read: this is a separate role from the Local Government Act's public burial-ground function. [7] [10]
- DBKL's road-closure permit page lists small events such as weddings and deaths among activities that may require a temporary road-closure permit. [11]

## SDP's exposure and lever

- Sime Darby Property's 2025 Integrated Annual Report lists City of Elmina, Serenia City, Bandar Bukit Raja and Nilai Impian among its townships. It also identifies ageing demographics as a megatrend. The board approved the report on 13 April 2026. [12]
- Analyst's read: These townships are possible places to assess local exposure; the report contains no SDP funeral-incident data.
- Analyst's read: SDP's design-stage levers may include masterplanning, land reservation, access, shared-space design and resident engagement. The Local Government Act gives local authorities the burial-ground functions described above; state religious bodies and kariah committees also have roles, while JMBs and MCs manage strata common property. [5] [7] [8] [10]
- Sime Darby Property's Sustainability Strategy and Roadmap 2030 includes Community Experience and Climate Resilience. [12]
- Analyst's read: A text search of the opened Integrated Annual Report 2025 found no match for “cemetery” or “grief”. The separate Sustainability Report 2025 PDF was too large for the browser to inspect, so this does not establish that no local programme exists. [12] [13]

## What already exists

- The sources reviewed show local-authority burial-ground powers and road permits, Selangor's Muslim cemetery inventory, and specific support services. Allay offers post-loss guidance on administration, finances and grief; FHL Hospice says it provides bereavement support to grieving families; Xiao En says it has offered professional counselling since 2012. [4] [7] [11] [14] [15] [28]
- PLANMalaysia's Muslim cemetery guide uses population and death-rate-based planning. It also addresses cemetery access, adequate parking, off-street parking and traffic circulation. Separately, the national guidance for non-Muslim cemeteries, crematoria and columbaria was still being finalised on 8 October 2026. [6] [8]
- Singapore's MyLegacy funeral guidance covers venue-specific wake arrangements and permits; its indexed page says wakes lasting more than seven days require NEA permission and road closures require an SPF permit. NEA lists burial, cremation and ash-management arrangements. [16] [17]
- Singapore's AfterLifeSG is a public digital memorial service for shared photos, biographies, resting-place information and memories. It grew from a GovTech hackathon prototype; it is not a cemetery-capacity or wake-logistics system. [27]
- Analyst's read: The 2024 Selangor inventory gives cemetery status and capacity projections, but the sources reviewed do not show a township-level dataset joining capacity with temporary wake activity, resident experience and support referrals. PLANMalaysia's guide already covers cemetery-site parking and traffic design; the remaining gap is the cross-system view, not a lack of cemetery access guidance.

## Hackathon and startup prior

- Sime Darby Property's 2026 hackathon timeline lists registration through 21 October, proposals due 23 October, judging on 24–28 October and an award ceremony on 3 December. As of 16 October, the proposal deadline and results were still ahead. [18]
- UiTM's 2022 competition volume includes a paper on “Mobile Application for Funeral Reporting System” / “E-Easy Funeral”. It describes reporting a death to a committee and coordinating arrangements with families, communities and mosque committees. The entry is not evidence of a prize win or township focus. The competition was in 2022; the PDF carries a 2023 copyright notice. [19]
- Bereev's product page describes a death-preparation web app for sharing funeral, legal, financial and personal plans. It is an adjacent Malaysian service, not a township-capacity or wake-logistics product. Allay is an adjacent post-loss service. [14] [26]
- Singapore GovTech reports that AfterLifeSG began as a prototype at its March 2025 hackathon, won Judges' Choice, and launched to the public in July 2026. It is a digital memorial service, not a township deathcare operations project. [27]
- Analyst's read: The three additional searches found no township-level deathcare winner or startup. This is a search result, not proof that none exists.

## Real data for a prototype

- DOSM annual deaths: https://open.dosm.gov.my/data-catalogue/deaths — data through 2025, CC BY 4.0. The catalogue page opened; I did not parse the raw download. [20]
- DOSM deaths by district and sex: https://open.dosm.gov.my/data-catalogue/deaths_district_sex — data through 2024, by usual residence rather than place of death, CC BY 4.0. The catalogue page opened; I did not query its API. [21]
- Selangor's 2024 study is a public PDF with a Muslim-cemetery inventory and capacity/fill projections, including JAIS figures from 2023. No reuse licence statement surfaced in the document text reviewed; revalidate figures with JAIS or the local authority before relying on current capacity. [4]
- OpenStreetMap is licensed under ODbL. The OSM Wiki documents tags for cemeteries, crematoria, funeral halls and funeral directors. The Overpass manual describes public query instances, including overpass-api.de; no target query was run, and this browser check did not confirm that the API endpoint works for a prototype. [22] [23] [25]
- Analyst's read: I did not find public SDP, council or JMB feeds for aggregate wakes, road permissions, complaints or support referrals. Privacy-safe access to these records would be valuable for a pilot.

## Pilot sketch

- Analyst's read: Start with a feasibility audit in Serenia City/Sepang, subject to JAIS and council confirmation of burial capacity and service catchment. The 2027 Bandar Baru Salak Tinggi estimate is based on the study's 2023 JAIS figures; recheck it before choosing a site. [4] [12]
- Analyst's read: Baseline the prior year's aggregate council, JMB, security, cemetery and operator records for funeral road use, permits, access and parking conflicts, complaints, travel time, capacity and support referrals. Use opt-in consent and a sensitive protocol for bereaved-resident interviews.
- Analyst's read: Likely cost drivers include fieldwork, translation, travel, secure data handling, agreements and coordination. I found no defensible public RM estimate.

## Kill risks

- Analyst's read: Local authorities and state bodies have roles in burial approvals and capacity; SDP cannot promise plots or standardise faith practice. [5] [7] [8]
- Analyst's read: Bereavement outreach may create privacy or emotional harm. Road use depends on tenure, local rules and custom; findings may not transfer between sites.
- Analyst's read: A death-registration or generic funeral app may repeat the team's prior GovTech work and miss the township brief. SDP may see weak value without evidence of community-experience or planning benefits.

## Scores

- Analyst's read: Pain — 4/5. Cemetery pressure is documented, while funeral disruption and bereaved-family access are not measured well. [3] [4] [5]
- Analyst's read: SDP lever — 3/5. SDP may shape planning and spaces, but local authorities, religious bodies and public cemeteries hold other key roles. [5] [7] [8]
- Analyst's read: Evidence available — 3/5. Official death totals and a Selangor inventory exist; township-level operating data is missing from the sources reviewed. [1] [4] [20]
- Analyst's read: Market density — 2/5. Adjacent post-loss, planning and memorial services exist; a township-operations focus did not surface in the searches. [14] [26] [27] [28]
- Analyst's read: Hackathon prior — 2/5. A 2022 Malaysian funeral-reporting entry and a 2025 Singapore memorial-service hackathon project exist, but no township-focused winner surfaced in the searches. [19] [27]
