Evidence pack on living inside a township that is still being built, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 47 claims: 15 confirmed, 18 corrected, 1 removed and 13 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings; scores are the worker's, before our own ranking.

## One-line problem

- **Analyst's read:** Where active work sits beside occupied neighbourhoods, a pilot could test whether a shared log of site activity, measured conditions, complaints, and corrective action helps people respond to mosquito breeding, dust, noise, traffic, and worker heat. The cited evidence documents separate hazards, measurements, and complaints. It does not establish that construction caused particular illnesses or damage.

## How big and how bad

- **Analyst's read:** The sources do not support a comparable ranking across dengue, heat, dust, noise, and road impacts. Dengue has national counts and site entomology studies. The heat case count has no exposure denominator. Dust, noise, and road examples are not comparable health-outcome rates.
- Malaysia recorded 65,979 dengue cases by epidemiological week 35 of 2026, versus 39,616 by the same week in 2025. Dengue deaths were 62 versus 32 ([1]). Selangor's ME21/2026 report gives cumulative ME01–ME21 totals of 13,339 cases, up 22.2% from 10,919, and 13 deaths versus 2 ([2]).
- **Breeding evidence is not disease attribution.** A 2023 study sampled 3,714 water-filled containers at four selected residential construction projects over 12 weeks, from 1 October to 31 December 2021. It found immature mosquitoes in 126 containers (3.4%) ([3]). A separate study surveyed 19 Kuala Lumpur construction sites from January to December 2018. It found 76 positive containers among 742 checked (average container index 10.24%); eight sites exceeded the study's 10% threshold. The paper appeared in 2022 ([4]).
- A 2014 CIDB inspection found 236 of 304 sites (77.6%) breeding or at risk of breeding Aedes; the report did not say all 236 had confirmed breeding ([5]). In a 23 May 2024 report, RTM said CIDB had carried out 1,528 enforcement operations nationwide from January to April. In a Kuala Lumpur operation, the Health Ministry identified eight suspected Aedes breeding locations, six inside buildings and two outside, during 169 visits. RTM did not give a denominator for those eight locations ([6]).
- Malaysia's ambient PM10 standards are 100 µg/m³ over 24 hours and 40 µg/m³ annually ([7]). WHO's 2021 guideline values are 45 µg/m³ over 24 hours and 15 µg/m³ annually ([8]). These are ambient air values. Treating them as construction-fence thresholds would be a pilot choice, not a direct statement of a construction-site limit.
- In an article published 6 April 2026, Media Selangor reported that JAS investigated a complaint made on 2 April about a concrete batching plant at a high-rise residential construction site in Bandar Baru Bangi. JAS said cement wash water reached public drains and the bag filter was unsatisfactory. It ordered suspension of the inlet and wash-water pipes and directed the operator to maintain wash ponds and replace the filters ([9]).
- The Straits Times reported on 10 March 2026 that Johor residents described persistent construction dust and noise near data-centre developments ([10]). The article reports residents' accounts; it does not provide exposure measurements.
- DOE's 2019, revision 3 construction-noise guideline lists residential and sensitive-area L10 limits of 75 dBA by day, 70 dBA in the evening, and 70 dBA at night. The corresponding Lmax values are 90, 85, and 85 dBA ([11]).
- Selangor's 2023 written response says local authorities patrol development routes, can penalise dirt or soil dropped in public places, require vehicle wash-throughs, and use earthwork deposits, route permits, and SISPAA complaints to manage road damage and dirt ([12]).
- DOSH recorded eight occupational heat-stress cases from 2017 to 2025. This is a case count, not a measure of worker exposure prevalence ([13]). DOSH's 2016 workplace guide uses WBGT to estimate heat stress. A 2026 DOSH interview also describes assessments that consider workload, exposure duration, worker health, ventilation, and PPE ([14], [13]).

## Trend and triggers

- Malaysia's dengue cases fell to 55,399 in 2025, down 54.7% from 122,423 in 2024, then rose by epidemiological week 35 of 2026 ([40], [1]).
- As of 16 October 2026, MetMalaysia's latest ENSO update said El Niño was strong and forecast to persist until May 2027. It expected very strong intensity toward the end of 2026, with hot, dry conditions from January to May 2027 ([15]). Separately, Bernama reported on 13 October that MetMalaysia's director-general projected temperatures could reach 41°C or higher in March–May 2027. This is a forecast, not an observed temperature ([16]).
- The Occupational Safety and Health (Amendment) Act 2022 took effect on 1 June 2024. The Construction Work (Design and Management) Regulations 2024 also took effect on 1 June 2024 ([17], [18]). The Environmental Quality (Amendment) Act 2024 took effect on 7 July 2024, but the cited DOE release does not describe this as a noise-specific amendment ([19]).

## Who suffers and who owns the fix

- **Analyst's read:** Residents, schools, shops, and workers near active phases may face different combinations of dust, noise, traffic, mosquitoes, and heat. JMBs can pass on complaints, but the report's sources do not establish their authority over construction sites.
- The Destruction of Disease-Bearing Insects Act 1975 gives health officers and inspectors authority to enter premises and order measures where disease-bearing insects may breed. It also prohibits water-holding holes or depressions from remaining for more than seven days, subject to the Act's conditions. MOH's construction-site guidance assigns developers and contractors daily Aedes source-reduction work ([20], [22]).
- Under section 18B of the Occupational Safety and Health Act, employers, self-employed people, and principals must assess safety and health risks to people affected by their work. DOSH's enforcement page says it enforces the Act ([21], [41]).
- DOE/JAS handles environmental pollution enforcement and EIA oversight. Selangor's written answer describes PBT roles in development-route patrols, road cleanliness, permits, and wash-through requirements ([42], [43], [12]).

## SDP's exposure and lever

- SDP's 2025 Integrated Annual Report describes 27 active townships across the Klang Valley, Negeri Sembilan, and Johor ([23]).
- SDP's 2022 Sustainability Report says it carried out EIA and environmental monitoring for water, air, and noise. It also records community reports of machinery noise and construction-period dust ([24]).
- SDP's 2025 Sustainability Report says construction accounted for 93% of the Group's waste generated in 2025 ([25]).
- SDP's Sustainability Strategy and Roadmap 2030 has four pillars: Energy and Carbon, Urban Biodiversity, Climate Resilience, and Community Experience ([39]). In 2025, SDP reported piloting SafePass AI for multilingual safety documentation and Guardian AI to identify unsafe acts and behaviours on construction sites ([25]). SDP also reports using Procore for construction quality, inspections, and project communication ([23]).
- **Analyst's read:** These are site-management and safety workflows. The reviewed reports do not describe a public community-exposure programme or a site-level dengue, dust, or heat baseline.

## What already exists

- Malaysia has MOH construction-site Aedes guidance, the public iDengue portal, DOE ambient-air and construction-noise criteria, DOSH WBGT guidance, and Selangor local-authority wash-through requirements ([22], [28], [7], [11], [14], [12]). The iDengue portal says it updates daily and includes outbreak and hotspot maps, state and district case status, and monthly totals ([28]).
- WHO's 2021 air-quality guidance and NIOSH's 2020 construction heat bulletin provide international reference material ([8], [26]). Oizom's March 2024 case study describes real-time air-quality monitoring at a London construction site ([27]).
- A Malaysian supplier, I-Tracking (M) Sdn Bhd, advertises a construction-monitoring system that combines live video, environmental sensors, site data, air-quality and noise monitoring, cloud access, alerts, and reporting. This is a vendor description, not independent evidence of a particular deployment ([29]).
- **Analyst's read:** The prototype's useful distinction would need to be a joined record of site activity, environmental readings, complaints, and remediation. Monitoring hardware and construction-site dashboards already exist. The reviewed sources do not show a universal national construction-fence PM10 limit.

## Hackathon and startup prior

- **Analyst's read:** The MYHackathon official winners page lists nine 2022 solutions. A title and description check did not surface an exact construction-edge mosquito, dust, noise, or heat project. This search is not a complete inventory of every Malaysian competition ([34]).
- MOSTI says five local solutions won MYHackathon 2024 Cohort 2, announced on 11 December 2024. The article was posted on 6 January 2025, and it does not name the solutions, so it cannot establish whether any overlaps with this idea ([35]).
- Malaysia took part in the 2024 ADVA/Junior Achievement Dengue Slayers Challenge, which covered outreach, surveillance and epidemiology, and vector control. Its Asia-Pacific final was held in Kuala Lumpur on 7 June 2024. The ADVA article was posted on 6 January 2025 ([36]).
- Adjacent local prior art includes UTM student startup Dispar Technologies' Sentinel, which embeds RFID in safety helmets for worker movement monitoring. UTM reported a two-to-three-month pilot at a construction firm in Skudai in June 2025 ([37]). Hayat Technologies describes AI.rbo as an AI-powered dengue forecasting system; its August 2023 release covered a study to assess implementation in Laos and said the system had been piloted or used through grants or commercial arrangements in Malaysia and other countries ([38]).
- The I-Tracking product above is also relevant local prior art: its advertised construction dashboard already combines site monitoring with air-quality and noise sensors ([29]).

## Real data for a prototype

- **Dengue:** JKN Selangor publishes weekly district reports, and iDengue describes public daily-updated case and hotspot information ([2], [28]). No reusable API or licence was identified in this review; that is an analyst search result, not proof that none exists. Data request 187, submitted 16 September 2026, asks for Selangor weekly dengue counts from 2015 to 2025 and is listed as Under Review ([32]).
- **Air:** data.gov.my's Monthly Air Pollution catalogue describes station averages for PM10, PM2.5, and other pollutants across Malaysia. Its data run through 31 December 2024; the catalogue was last updated on 30 September 2026 and lists CC BY 4.0. The catalogue page loaded, but the CSV was not parsed ([30]).
- **Air and weather feeds:** DOE's APIMS page is a public interactive portal, but this review could not extract its live readings through the text view ([31]). The data.gov.my weather documentation lists public forecast and warning endpoints, including state and district filters; the documentation was checked, but a live API response was not confirmed ([33]). Neither source provides worker WBGT or SDP fence-line measurements in the material reviewed.
- **Analyst's read:** The review found no public site-level dataset for SDP boundary PM10, construction noise, truck flow, or worker WBGT. Confirm this with SDP and the relevant agencies before treating it as a hard constraint.

## Pilot sketch

- **Analyst's proposed design:** At an occupied housing edge beside an active City of Elmina phase, compare 12 weeks of site-boundary observations with a nearby non-construction reference. Measure container index, boundary PM10 and noise, truck counts and road condition, and WBGT by work zone and task. The design is a proposal, not an existing SDP programme ([23], [3]).
- Cost drivers would include calibrated meters, field staff, site access and safety induction, traffic observation, and consent. No local unit-cost estimate was found in the cited material.

## Kill risks

- **Analyst's read:** A short pilot may show conditions and response times but not prove that construction caused illness or road damage. Site access, worker privacy, sensor calibration, and divided enforcement responsibilities could limit the evidence. Judges may see a generic sensor exercise unless the team demonstrates a clear link from readings to actions.

## Scores

- **Analyst's read — Pain: 4/5.** Dengue counts, SDP's community reports, and reported Johor complaints point to meaningful concerns, but outcomes are not comparable ([1], [24], [10]).
- **Analyst's read — SDP lever: 5/5.** SDP controls its development phases and reports contractor, inspection, and safety workflows ([23], [25]).
- **Analyst's read — Evidence available: 3/5.** Dengue and ambient-air material are available, while public site and worker exposure records were not identified ([4], [30]).
- **Analyst's read — Market density: 4/5.** Local vendors already advertise construction dashboards and air/noise monitoring, alongside dengue forecasting and worker wearables ([29], [38], [37]).
- **Analyst's read — Hackathon prior: 2/5.** The searches found adjacent dengue and worker-safety projects but no exact construction-edge match; the search was not exhaustive ([34], [36], [37]).
