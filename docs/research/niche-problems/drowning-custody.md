# Who owns township water edges, and how to pilot there

Deep-dive evidence on who owns and maintains township drains and ponds, and how to get a pilot approved, researched and then fact-checked by two separate Codex runs on 16 October 2026 for [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md). The checker tested 39 claims: 12 confirmed, 15 corrected, 2 removed and 10 it could only see in search snippets. Lines marked "Analyst's read" are the worker's inference, not findings.

## Summary

- **Control depends on the asset and its formal status.** Act 133 places local-authority drain and watercourse maintenance duties only on features under that authority’s control. Selangor answers also describe developers retaining responsibility for development infrastructure before formal handover; those DUN pages were available only as search snippets in this check. [Act 133, ss. 53–54][r1]; [Selangor 2025 handover answer, snippet-only][r2]
- **JPS does not automatically maintain every pond.** JPS guidance says responsibility for on-site detention or detention ponds depends on the State’s decision. Selangor’s 2025 answer describes a division for certain unclaimed drains, but the page could not be opened directly in this check. [JPS FAQ, snippet-only](https://wplb.water.gov.my/en/faq); [Selangor drain answer, snippet-only][r3]
- **SDP reports company-wide flood and drainage work, but the reviewed sources do not identify the current operator of each named lake.** SDP’s 2025 Integrated Annual Report describes drain and waterway cleaning, monitoring, emergency response, retention measures, and work in handed-over areas. [SDP Integrated Annual Report 2025][r4]
- **Public data can support a prototype, with limits.** Public InfoBanjir pages expose station selectors, but this check did not verify live readings or freshness. data.gov.my has district-level school counts, not school coordinates. MBDK publishes a school-list PDF and a cleaning schedule. No downloadable SDP pond-and-drain GIS was located in the sources reviewed. [Public InfoBanjir rainfall][r5]; [schools dataset][r6]; [MBDK open data][r7]
- **Analyst's read:** before a live pilot, get the land/asset record, current custodian, and relevant authority’s written agreement. No published standard permit timeline for a temporary sensor or barrier was located in the materials reviewed; that is a bounded search result, not proof that no timeline exists.

Act 133 section 42 requires prior written local-authority permission to deposit building materials or make a hole in a street or back lane. Section 46 bars covering or obstructing an open drain or aqueduct along a street without prior written permission. These sections support checking permits before installing equipment that involves those actions. [Act 133, ss. 42, 46][r1]

**Who pays:** the 2024 Selangor answer says a council may collect assessment after CCC while the developer still maintains infrastructure pending handover. The 2025 answer’s deposit and handover details are snippet-only. After transfer, the responsible agency’s budget or maintenance contract applies; the reported RM5 million allocation is a specific 2026 measure, not a permanent funding rule. In strata schemes, charges and sinking-fund contributions finance common-property maintenance. [Selangor 2024 answer][r9]; [Selangor 2025 answers, snippet-only][r3]; [Strata Management Act 2013][r12]

A separate 2024 Selangor answer about Kampung Sungai Pinang says the pond had not been gazetted because ownership remained with MBDK, and mentions As-Built, pre-calculation, and Form 12B records. The page was available only as a search snippet in this check. Treat this as a site-specific example, not a general ownership rule. [Selangor 2024 answer, snippet-only][r13]

**Analyst's read:** request the title or reserve plan, handover/acceptance record, current maintenance contract, and any defect-liability record for the exact lot or feature. A contractor’s cleaning work does not by itself establish title or statutory control. A 2026 MPHS snippet describes an internal-audit visit monitoring KDEB public-cleaning work in Rasa; it is evidence of a cleaning activity, not pond ownership or operation. **Snippet-only.** [MPHS, 7 September 2026][r14]

**Defect-liability periods vary in the Selangor examples reviewed.** A 2020 answer about a road under a developer describes a one-year defect period after CCC. A 2019 answer concerning a specific council refers to a six-month defect period before handover. The 2019 page was available only as a search snippet. Neither establishes a universal pond or drain period; check the site’s contract and handover record. [Selangor 2020 road answer][r15]; [Selangor 2019 answer, snippet-only][r16]

## Liability cases and inquiries

- The original research pack’s searches did not locate a final reported Malaysian judgment deciding liability for a child drowning in a township drain or retention pond. This is a bounded search result, not evidence that no such judgment exists.
- **Bandar Hillpark retention-pond flood case:** MKH’s interim report says 47 plaintiffs (Mohd Zikri and 46 others) sued MKH and its wholly owned subsidiary Hillpark Resources Berhad over flood incidents on 29 December 2024 and 23 April 2025. The report says both defendants filed defences on 16 January 2026. On 12 March 2026, the High Court struck out the claim against MKH only and ordered RM1,500 in costs to MKH. The report identifies Hillpark Resources as the pond’s developer; it does not report a finding that the subsidiary was liable. The report covers the period to 31 March 2026 and was authorized for issue on 22 May 2026. This was a flood-damage case, not a drowning case. [MKH interim report][r17]
- **Selangor child incidents and review:** RTM reported on 4 May 2026 that the State announced a comprehensive review of risky drains after two recent child drownings. Media Selangor reported on 10 May that MBPJ planned a phased local drainage audit, starting with flood hotspots and checking old or damaged covers; it also said privately installed covers can make cleaning difficult. The account describes only one child as having fallen into a drain; both bodies were found in rivers. The sources do not support saying both deaths happened in drains or that the incidents occurred in April and May. **Not located in the sources checked:** a published consolidated result of the statewide review. [RTM, 4 May 2026][r18]; [Media Selangor, 10 May 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/05/356449)
- **Open-drain precedent, outside Selangor:** Malay Mail reported on 14 May 2014 that an 11-year-old student died after falling into an uncovered monsoon drain near SK Ampang on 16 April 2014. The school and Ampang Jaya Municipal Council later grated and padlocked the opening and took other safety steps. The article reports corrective action, not a court ruling on civil liability. [Malay Mail, 14 May 2014][r19]
- **Sibu uncovered-drain incident:** a Sibu Municipal Council page reposting a Borneo Post report, posted 5 August 2025 and updated 2 October 2026, says a two-year-old boy fell into an uncovered storm-drain manhole the previous day and died. It says the council replaced missing covers and installed warning lines; the council’s engineering division said the drain was under DID jurisdiction. This is not a court ruling. [Sibu Municipal Council](https://smc.gov.my/web/subpage/news_view/1341)

**Analyst's read:** control, a statutory or contractual duty, notice of a hazard, safeguards, and causation would matter to a liability assessment. A child’s death beside an asset does not by itself identify a liable party. Act 133 section 43 gives a council a power to require an owner to protect or enclose a dangerous place; sections 53–54 address maintenance of drains under the council’s control. [Act 133, ss. 43, 53–54][r1]

## Pilot approvals and timeline

**Approvals to secure before installing anything:**

1. **Confirm the site and asset controller.** Ask SDP for the title or reserve information, approved drainage and landscape plans, as-built drawings, acceptance/handover letters, defect-liability record, and current maintenance contract. Ask the relevant local council and JPS to confirm control of the drain, pond, outfall, and access route. The site address determines which PBT to contact.
2. **Get written site and engineering consent.** The owner/current custodian and relevant authority should approve sensor placement, access, servicing, signage, and barriers. **Analyst's read:** this is a prudent pilot condition. Act 133 specifically requires prior written permission for depositing building materials or making a hole in a street or back lane (s. 42), and for covering or obstructing an open drain or aqueduct along a street (s. 46). Section 43 allows a council to require an owner to protect or enclose a dangerous place. [Act 133, ss. 42–43, 46][r1]
3. **Add the site-specific authority.** **Analyst's read:** involve JPS if it controls the pond or drain or the work may affect hydraulic function; involve the council if it controls the public street, drain, or park; include a JMB or management corporation when the asset is strata common property. Act 171 s. 101(c) grants park-related powers but does not identify the controller of a particular feature. [Act 171, s. 101(c)][r11]; [Strata Management Act 2013][r12]
4. **Keep the pilot non-invasive.** **Analyst's read:** a clamp-on or stand-off level sensor, battery/solar logger, and sign fixed on approved land may avoid cutting into a drain or changing pond hydraulics. Treat changes to an inlet, outlet, barrier, bank, public path, or approved pond shape as a design change and ask the responsible engineer to review them. A first proof could monitor rainfall and water level and route maintenance alerts; add a physical barrier only after the custodian and engineering authority review its effect on flow and access.
5. **Agree data and maintenance responsibilities.** **Analyst's read:** state who owns readings, checks failed sensors, receives alerts, inspects flagged sites, and pays for replacements. Align access with the current maintenance contract. MBDK’s published schedule specifies monthly cleaning for drains and daily cleaning for sumps, scupper drains, and litter traps in its service area. [MBDK cleaning schedule][r29]

**Timeline:** no published standard approval time for a research sensor, warning sign, or barrier was located in the sources reviewed. This is a bounded search result. The cited DLP examples concern handover defects, not pilot permits. **Analyst's read:** ask SDP, the selected PBT, and JPS for the written review steps before setting a calendar. A practical sequence is to confirm the asset and title, obtain owner/custodian consent, obtain engineering and public-space approvals, agree data and maintenance responsibilities, install, commission against a real rain or water-level event, and evaluate alerts and maintenance follow-up. Use actual readings and dated inspection records.

**2026 state action:** RTM reported a State announcement on 4 May for a comprehensive review of risky drains after two recent child drownings. Media Selangor reported on 10 May that MBPJ planned a phased audit, starting with flood hotspots and checking old or damaged covers; it also described privately covered drains as a maintenance obstacle. **Not located in the sources checked by 16 October 2026:** a published consolidated statewide result. [RTM, 4 May 2026][r18]; [Media Selangor, 10 May 2026](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/05/356449)

**Analyst's read:** a pilot may be easier to approve where SDP can show an asset record and maintenance owner, and where the pilot observes water without changing the approved drainage path. A useful output is a custody map linking each monitored edge to its owner, inspection routine, responsible agency, and escalation contact.
