## People track

### Safety and security

- Malaysia's crime index rose 11.1% in 2024 to 58,255 cases, driven by property crime, up 12.4% to 47,188 ([DOSM Crime Statistics 2025][dosm-crime]). This DOSM release covers 2024 data.
- House break-ins fell 1.8% in 2024; 77.6% happened at night ([DOSM][dosm-crime]).
- Scams are the growing threat. Police recorded 67,735 online crime cases from January to November 2025, with losses above RM2.7 billion; telecommunications scams led with 28,698 cases ([Malay Mail, December 2025][scams]).
- Selangor's commercial crime losses passed RM322 million in the first four months of 2025, mostly online scams ([Malay Mail, excerpt][selangor-scams]).

### Community wellbeing

- About one million people over 15, or 4.6%, had depression in NHMS 2023, double the 2019 number ([CodeBlue][nhms-dep]).
- Young people are hit hardest: 7.9% of those aged 16 to 19 and 7.6% of those aged 20 to 29. Nearly half of people with depression had thoughts of self-harm or suicide ([CodeBlue][nhms-dep]).
- No 2024 or later loneliness prevalence figure for Malaysia was found.

[nhms-dep]: https://codeblue.galencentre.org/2024/06/nhms-2023-a-million-people-aged-above-15-in-malaysia-suffer-from-depression/

### Ageing

- Malaysia became an ageing nation in 2021, with 7% aged 65 and over, and is projected to be an aged nation, 14%, by 2048 ([Malay Mail, August 2025][aged]).
- In NHMS 2025, 18.8% of people aged 60 and over live alone, almost three times the 6.3% of 2018. The Health Minister called transport "a major barrier to consistent access to healthcare", and one in three caregivers is under severe strain ([NST, April 2026][alone]).
- Dementia among seniors rose to 9.8% in 2025 from 8.5% in 2018, and only 14.7% met healthy-ageing criteria ([CodeBlue, excerpt][nhms-older]). The full report was still to come, so these may be revised.

### Accessibility and inclusion

- The Department of Social Welfare had 756,681 registered persons with disabilities (OKU) on 31 January 2024; Selangor had the most, 128,401 ([Galen Centre, citing Bernama][oku]).
- About 40% of public low-cost housing (PPR) households rely on a single earner, and nearly one in ten has no breadwinner, mostly homes with a disabled member ([Malay Mail, May 2026, excerpt][ppr]).
- No 2024 or later audit of MS 1184 accessibility compliance in Selangor townships was found.

### Mobility

- An average Klang Valley commuter spends over 500 hours a year in traffic, about 2 hours 15 minutes a day, by a UKM lecturer's 2025 estimate ([TheVibes, excerpt][traffic]).
- In City of Elmina, a resident told Focus Malaysia in April 2025 that "An 1km exit to my house takes an hour" ([Focus Malaysia][elmina-traffic]). Before its 2025 AGM, SDP blamed non-residents using its roads "as shortcuts to GCE and DASH highway" ([SDP AGM answers][sdp-agm] p. 6).
- The 13th Malaysia Plan puts Klang Valley public transport's share of trips at 25.9%, with a 40% target for 2030 ([Bernama, excerpt][13mp-transport]). Ridership grew 18.6% a year from 2021 to 2024 (same source).
- From 16 January 2026, 19 low-ridership bus routes, 16 of them MRT feeders, moved to on-demand vans ([Malay Mail, excerpt][drt]). The Shah Alam LRT line logged 60,000 trips on its first full day in June 2026 ([Malay Mail, excerpt][lrt3]).
- Road safety: an average of 434 children died on Malaysian roads each year from 2014 to 2023, about eight a week ([NST, September 2025][kids]). Motorcyclists are about two thirds of road deaths ([TheVibes, August 2026, excerpt][moto]).

### Digital living and neighbourhood connection

- Internet use reached 98.0% of individuals in 2024; household access was 98.8% in urban areas and 90.3% in rural areas ([DOSM ICT survey 2024][ict]).
- Subang Jaya City Council received about 29,000 resident complaints by November 2024, around 2,000 a month, mostly about street lights and landscaping ([Malay Mail, excerpt][mbsj]).
- A single reporting channel is taken as an idea ([BRIEF, the challenge](/docs/BRIEF.md#the-challenge)), and the government's SISPAA already offers one ([What already exists](/docs/research/landscape/existing-solutions.md)).

### Healthy lifestyles

- NHMS 2023: 54.4% of adults are overweight or obese (32.6% overweight, 21.8% obese), 15.6% have diabetes and 29.2% have hypertension ([NHMS 2023 fact sheet][nhms-fact]). Overweight and obesity rose from 44.5% in 2011 ([CodeBlue][nhms-obese]).
- Physical inactivity was 29.9% in 2023, up from 25.1% in 2019, and higher in urban areas ([PMC study][inactive]).

## Planet track

### Waste and circular economy

- Malaysia generated 15.2 million tonnes of solid waste in 2024 ([Asia News Network, April 2026][ann-waste]). The national recycling rate was 37.9% in 2024, short of the 40% target for 2025 ([SWCorp][swcorp]).
- About 40% of landfilled waste is recyclable; plastics are the largest share at 20.55% ([Asia News Network][ann-waste]).
- **Selangor is changing the rules.** On 29 July 2026 the Cabinet agreed to extend the Solid Waste Act (Act 672) to Selangor, in stages across its 12 local authorities, with better collection and separation at source under the 13th Malaysia Plan ([The Star][act672]). No start date was given.
- **Malls must now recycle.** From June 2026, recycling facilities at shopping malls are mandatory for applying for or renewing a council business licence, phased in ([Malay Mail][mall-recycling]).
- Selangor had 76 active illegal dumpsites in August 2026 and is adding CCTV at hotspots ([NST][dumpsites]).
- **Developers are named.** KPKT's Circular Economy Blueprint for Solid Waste 2025 to 2035 aims to "encourage housing developers to incorporate circular economy facilities in neighbourhoods, enabling residents to easily sort and dispose of recyclables" ([MGTC, citing The Star][blueprint]). It also plans extended producer responsibility.
- Budget 2025 gives households a RM2,500 rebate for food-waste composting machines ([MGTC][blueprint]).

### Water

- Selangor's new water tariff started on 1 September 2025. Homes using up to 20 m³ a month still pay RM0.65/m³; 20 to 35 m³ costs RM1.62/m³ and above 35 m³ RM3.51/m³. Data centres now pay RM5.31/m³ ([Malay Mail][water-tariff]).
- Air Selangor's non-revenue water was 26.76% in 2025 ([Air Selangor Sustainability Report 2025 highlights][airsel-sr]). It manages 31,817 km of pipes and found 257,448 leaks in 2024, and says old pipes make losses hard to cut ([Malay Mail, May 2026][nrw]).
- National non-revenue water in Peninsular Malaysia stayed above 33% from 2015 to 2024. The 31% target for 2025 was missed; the new target is 28.8% by 2030 ([Malay Mail][nrw]).

### Green transport

- Members of the Malaysian Automotive Association sold 30,848 EVs in 2025, up 109% from 14,766 in 2024 ([paultan.org, excerpt][ev-sales]).
- Malaysia had 6,416 public EV chargers on 31 May 2026, 2,143 of them DC. The 10,000 target for 2025 was missed; the new target is 30,000 by 2030 ([paultan.org, excerpt][chargers]).
- Imported EVs lost their duty exemption on 31 December 2025; locally assembled EVs keep it until the end of 2027 ([Malay Mail, excerpt][ev-duty]).

### Biodiversity and urban nature

- Selangor's wildlife department logged 18,444 complaints from 2023 to May 2025; nearly 80% were about monkeys ([Bernama, excerpt][wildlife]).
- Dengue in Selangor fell 61% in 2025, to 23,565 cases from 60,364 ([Malay Mail, January 2026][dengue]). It rose again in 2026: by week 40 Selangor had 31,782 cases, up 72.6% from 18,419 a year earlier, and 31 deaths against 6. ### Climate resilience: floods, heat and haze

- **Floods:** national flood losses were RM636.9 million in 2025, down from RM933.4 million in 2024; Selangor lost RM52.9 million ([RinggitPlus, citing DOSM][floods]). Flash floods on 11 April 2025 hit Petaling, Klang, Sepang, Hulu Langat and Gombak ([NST, excerpt][flash]).
- **SDP's own exposure:** a Swiss Re assessment found flood exposure at Bandar Bukit Raja and Bandar Universiti Pagoh ([Sime Darby Property](/docs/research/landscape/sime-darby-property.md#climate-risk-nature-and-community)).
- **Heat:** MetMalaysia issues a Level 1 heat alert at 35 to 37°C for three or more days. Parts of Selangor were on Level 1 in April 2026 and again in August 2026 ([Malay Mail, excerpt][heat]). A 2024 small-scale test found a ventilated cool roof kept a model house up to 4.8°C cooler indoors ([UTHM][coolroof]).
- **Haze:** Johan Setia in Selangor reached an air-pollutant index of 193 on 18 September 2026 ([Malay Mail, excerpt][haze]). In October, haze closed Selangor schools; they were due to reopen on 15 October ([The Star][haze-reopen]), then schools in eight districts, including Klang and both Petaling districts, closed again on 16 October ([The Star][haze-close]). In Singapore on 15 October, people queued for N95 masks, while air-conditioned haze shelters "appeared to be less popular" ([The Straits Times][sg-shelters]).

## Where the numbers point

Each point is our read.

- **Fast-rising problems:** older people living alone (×3 since 2018), scams (RM2.7 billion in 11 months), SDP's own reported emissions (+24%, partly a wider boundary), and dengue's 2026 rebound.
- **Problems with a fresh rule change:** waste separation in Selangor (Act 672, July 2026), mandatory mall recycling facilities (June 2026), Selangor water prices (September 2025), and building energy labels under the Energy Efficiency Act.
- **Problems SDP can act on directly:** waste facilities in neighbourhoods, which the national blueprint asks developers to build; flood and heat at its named townships; and energy in the malls it runs.

## Unverified and not found

- DOSM's 2024 disability statistics page didn't load, so its count of 805,509 registered OKU is unconfirmed; we use JKM's January 2024 figure.
