## Summary

- UAV images can locate visible sewer inlets: a 2018 study reported average precision (AP) of 0.65 from one view and 0.73 after combining views at 3–3.5 cm/px; a 2024 study reported precision/recall of 91%/96% with YOLOv5 at 1 cm/px. These are inlet-location results, not proof of detecting a missing grate or blocked drain. [1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/10/5/706) [2](https://opencivilengineeringjournal.com/VOLUME/18/ELOCATOR/e18741495308303/FULLTEXT/)
- A 2017 aerial-image drainage-grate study reported an 89.3% detection rate on 20 images containing 289 grates, but its available record does not give precision, recall, altitude, or GSD. Analyst's read: The searches described below did not locate a direct aerial benchmark for missing drain covers. [3](https://trid.trb.org/View/1438602)
- Photogrammetry can measure a visible, textured bank under controlled conditions: one riverbank study reported 2.1 cm/px average GSD and 3 cm RMSE. Vegetation at the waterline still hid the bank in some views. [7](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/6/933/2018/)
- Water-level estimates and floating-object detection are possible in narrow conditions. A 2022 RANSAC study estimates a water plane from UAV photogrammetry using a known-elevation reference; current Pix4D guidance says reflections and waves cannot be used for visual matching. A 2025 floating-object model reported 90% mAP@0.5 at 10–20 m flight heights; its GSD was not reported and it used its own dataset. [10](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9315810/) [11](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/14988429657117) [12](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/6/1938)
- Analyst's read: Repeat-flight comparison needs co-registration and visual review. One study reduced relative alignment error below 2 cm; RTK improves absolute positioning, but RTK alone does not make two orthomosaics pixel-aligned. [18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2022.102772) [22](https://enterprise.dji.com/mavic-3-enterprise/specs)
- Analyst's read: A 100 mm opening is optically resolvable in a close, sharp, near-normal image, but no published study located here validates automated detection of a 100 mm fence gap from a township drone patrol. A 1.2 m barrier may be visible, but accurate height measurement needs a calibrated, checked 3D survey. [23](https://enterprise.dji.com/zenmuse-p1/specs) [7](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/6/933/2018/)
- Analyst's read: Open-vocabulary models are useful for proposing candidate objects, not for certifying edge hazards. Results on aerial-image benchmarks remain weak or unrelated to drain defects, and general vision-language models can describe scenes while mislocalizing or miscounting objects. [29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22164) [31](https://www.openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2025W/V4A/papers/Singh_Few-Shot_Adaptation_of_Grounding_DINO_for_Agricultural_Domain_CVPRW_2025_paper.pdf) [36](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/EarthVision/papers/Zhang_Good_at_Captioning_Bad_at_Counting_Benchmarking_GPT-4V_on_Earth_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)
- Public datasets cover flood scenes, floating debris, vegetation, fences, construction, and lifebuoys. Analyst's read: The English and Malay searches described below did not locate a Malaysian, commercially reusable, labeled drone dataset for township drain-cover loss, fence gaps, fixed ring buoys, or safety signs. [14](https://figshare.com/articles/dataset/__zip/24123666) [16](https://aimh.isti.cnr.it/dataset/mobdrone/) [28](https://zenodo.org/records/13902550) [51](https://journal.ump.edu.my/mekatronika/article/download/12196/3699/52056)

### 2. Repeat-flight change detection

Registration is the first gate. A 2022 study evaluated 120 digital surface models generated through 24 workflows. Co-alignment reduced relative errors below 2 cm; the study also found that RTK can improve absolute horizontal accuracy and that ground-control points in at least one survey improve absolute vertical accuracy. [18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2022.102772) DJI lists Mavic 3 Enterprise RTK GNSS positioning accuracy as 1 cm + 1 ppm horizontally and 1.5 cm + 1 ppm vertically. Those are positioning specifications, not a guarantee that two image products align to that accuracy. [22](https://enterprise.dji.com/mavic-3-enterprise/specs)

Analyst's read: Pixel-level change compares aligned pixel values and can largely ignore spatial context; object-level methods compare segmented objects and their spectral, spatial, or contextual features. Both approaches depend on reliable alignment, and pixel-level differences can be sensitive to camera pose, sun angle, shadow, exposure, wet surfaces, and seasonal colour changes. Three-dimensional methods compare point clouds or surfaces and should use a local level of detection based on registration error and surface roughness, rather than one global threshold. [19](https://eprints.whiterose.ac.uk/112711/1/James%20et%20al%202017%20DEM.pdf) [20](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-4/65/2014/isprsarchives-XL-4-65-2014.html) [48](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0924271613000804)

Analyst's read: For tropical township patrols, expect false flags from changed shadows and illumination, perspective differences, vegetation growth or movement, wet or reflective materials, and water glint or waves. Published work documents illumination and shadow as false-change sources, canopy occlusion as a ground-visibility limit, and reflections/waves as a water-matching problem; test wet-surface and seasonal-growth effects locally. [20](https://isprs-archives.copernicus.org/articles/XL-4/65/2014/isprsarchives-XL-4-65-2014.html) [21](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021JG006586) [11](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/14988429657117)

Analyst's read: Use repeatable routes, similar time-of-day and camera orientation, fixed image scale, and stable reference targets. Register each revisit to the accepted baseline, then compare both objects and surface geometry. Treat a difference as a review flag until a person confirms it in source imagery and at the site. The reported sub-2-cm relative alignment is a feasibility benchmark, not a universal acceptance threshold for Safe Edge. [18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2022.102772)

### 3. 100 mm gap and 1.2 m barrier

The DJI Zenmuse P1 is listed with a 35 mm lens option, a 35.9 mm-wide sensor, and 8,192-pixel image width. DJI’s stated mapping accuracy is 3 cm horizontal and 5 cm vertical at 3 cm/px GSD under its stated mapping conditions; that specification does not validate close-range fence measurements. [23](https://enterprise.dji.com/zenmuse-p1/specs)

Analyst's read: With a perpendicular 40 m camera-to-fence distance and the P1’s 35 mm lens, pinhole geometry gives about 5 mm/px; a 100 mm opening would span about 20 pixels, and a 1.2 m height about 240 pixels in the image plane. These are sampling estimates, not detector or survey accuracy. A tilted view makes the fence plane foreshorten, while motion blur, low contrast, vegetation, wire texture, and occlusion reduce usable detail. [23](https://enterprise.dji.com/zenmuse-p1/specs)

Analyst's read: A 100 mm gap is optically resolvable in a sharp, unobstructed image with a near-normal view. No located paper validates that exact gap size with a routine aerial patrol. For a pilot, a provisional width-error target of at most 20 mm would make the measurement error smaller than the gap; verify it against tape-measured openings and report failures. This is a design target, not a published standard. A 1.2 m barrier can be visually observed at suitable scale, but photogrammetric height needs both top and base visible and a checked 3D model. DJI states 5 cm vertical mapping accuracy at 3 cm/px under its mapping conditions, but that does not establish fence-height accuracy. The 3 cm bank RMSE from one controlled river study is not a fence-height guarantee. [7](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/6/933/2018/) [18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2022.102772) [23](https://enterprise.dji.com/zenmuse-p1/specs)

Under tree canopy, a drone generally measures the canopy surface, not the hidden ground or fence base. A tropical forest study found that SfM photogrammetry cannot be used alone to measure ground elevation reliably, even under low tree cover. Ground-level inspection or a separate sensor and measurement method is needed for hidden gaps. [21](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021JG006586)

### 4. Thin structures

Rails, mesh, chain-link, and narrow posts can occupy only a few pixels, repeat in a regular pattern, blend with background vegetation, or disappear against shadow. These conditions create missed detections, unstable segmentation, and holes or broken surfaces in photogrammetric reconstructions. A UAV power-line reconstruction study identifies missing data in the photogrammetric surface model as a common error; power lines are a nearby example of thin structures. [46](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/12/22/3698) An airport fence-damage study identifies low contrast and limited training data as important problems; its optimized YOLOv5 improved AP by 6.9 percentage points, but the cameras were on a ground robot. [26](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024W/RWS/papers/Friederich_Security_Fence_Inspection_at_Airports_Using_Object_Detection_WACVW_2024_paper.pdf)

The aerial MultiFranceFences dataset removes annotations under tree canopy or where visibility is limited. [28](https://zenodo.org/records/13902550) Analyst's read: This is evidence that labels omit occluded fence sections. The Zenodo record does not report pixel spacing, so it cannot support a small-gap resolution estimate.

Analyst's read: For visible fence faces, capture a high-resolution pass with the camera aimed close to perpendicular to the fence, then a second view from a different direction to reveal occluded sections. Keep a conservative blur/visibility rejection rule. Use ground inspection for mesh openings, wire breaks, obscured bases, and any segment hidden by canopy. Do not fill photogrammetric holes and then treat the filled mesh as observed evidence. [21](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021JG006586) [26](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/WACV2024W/RWS/papers/Friederich_Security_Fence_Inspection_at_Airports_Using_Object_Detection_WACVW_2024_paper.pdf)

Analyst's read: For a first pilot, use a two-scale capture plan rather than one fixed altitude. Fly a mapping pass at the height that gives the required site coverage and overlap, then a closer detail pass sized by the smallest target. With a P1 and 35 mm lens, a 40 m perpendicular stand-off gives a theoretical GSD near 5 mm/px; recalculate for the actual camera and angle. [23](https://enterprise.dji.com/zenmuse-p1/specs)

Analyst's read: For a tree-lined edge, plan at least 85% forward and side overlap. Pix4D gives a general minimum of 75% forward and 60% side, and recommends 85% in both directions for dense vegetation. These are processing guidelines, not safety-performance guarantees. [47](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/203756125)

Analyst's read: For barriers, capture a near-normal image of the fence face, with enough stand-off to preserve focus and enough overlap to keep it visible in multiple frames. For banks, include oblique views from the water side where permitted; the cited river study used a 25 m above-water pass and about 40 m mean stand-off. [7](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/6/933/2018/)

Analyst's read: Reject or downgrade segments with glare, blur, heavy shadow, moving foliage, or canopy occlusion. Repeat the flight after rain only when the drone can safely see the same surfaces. A field walk remains the reference for hidden drains, fence bases, and small emergency equipment. [11](https://support.pix4d.com/hc/en-us/articles/14988429657117) [21](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021JG006586)

Analyst's read: Before live flights in Malaysia, check permissions for the site and planned aerial work. CAAM states that aerial work requires authorization under the Civil Aviation Regulations and that UAS aerial photography, mapping, or LiDAR operations require JUPEM approval. [24](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/) The JUPEM page [25](https://www.jupem.gov.my/v2/en/pages/uas) did not load during this check; CAAM’s page corroborates the approval requirement.

## What we can and cannot claim

**Safe claims for the pitch**

- “UAV imagery can help inventory visible drains, grates, floating debris, paths, and exposed bank changes; published studies show task-specific results, with local validation still required.” [1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/10/5/706) [3](https://trid.trb.org/View/1438602) [7](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/6/933/2018/) [12](https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/25/6/1938)
- “Safe Edge proposes change flags and human-reviewed maintenance actions; the AI does not certify an edge as safe.” This is a product-design statement, not a measured research result.
- “A controlled photogrammetry study measured a visible riverbank at 3 cm RMSE, and a separate study aligned repeat surface models to below 2 cm relative error.” These are reported study results, not a guarantee for Elmina. [7](https://esurf.copernicus.org/articles/6/933/2018/) [18](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jag.2022.102772)

**Claims to avoid**

- “The drone detects every missing drain cover, fence gap, blocked inlet, or missing ring buoy.” Analyst's read: The searches described in this pack did not locate direct validated aerial performance for those township defects; inlet and cover-type studies measure different tasks. [1](https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/10/5/706) [4](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3417/14/7/2769)
- “A 100 mm gap or 1.2 m barrier is measured accurately from any flight.” The pixel calculation is optical sampling, and no cited study validates those dimensions under routine patrol conditions. [23](https://enterprise.dji.com/zenmuse-p1/specs)
- “AI proves an edge is safe or guarantees a repair.” Open-vocabulary and VLM results have false positives and localization limits, and a drone cannot observe through canopy, water, or a closed drain. [21](https://agupubs.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1029/2021JG006586) [29](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.22164) [36](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2024W/EarthVision/papers/Zhang_Good_at_Captioning_Bad_at_Counting_Benchmarking_GPT-4V_on_Earth_CVPRW_2024_paper.pdf)
