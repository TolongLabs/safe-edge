## Summary

- The operative framework remains the Civil Aviation Act 1969 and Civil Aviation Regulations 2016 (CAR 2016), especially regulations 140–144. Regulation 140 covers Class A, B, C and G airspace, an aerodrome traffic zone, and flight above 400 feet; the regulation requires permission from the Director General. CAAM's Standard ATF is the published route for low-risk VLOS or EVLOS operations. [MOT legislation list](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/aviation/acts-and-policy), [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf), [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)
- A camera-equipped inspection drone will normally meet CAAM's definition of a small unmanned surveillance aircraft (SUsA). The published fee is RM250 per SUsA, multiplied by aircraft count and each location area with a radius under 15 nautical miles; one application may cover up to 90 days. Standard ATF documents are due at least 14 working days before flight. [CAAM fee page](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/aviation-professional/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf)
- Elmina Central Park appears to sit inside Subang Airport’s Class C control zone. The approximate distance is derived from a mapped park point, so CAAM and Subang ATS must check the selected water edge’s submitted coordinates before any flight. [Subang AIP entry](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2026-08-06-AIRAC/html/eAIP/WM-AD-2.WMSA-en-MS.html), [park map point](https://mapcarta.com/W866543394)
- BVLOS and drone-in-a-box operations have Malaysian precedents, but the confirmed cases specify their sites, operating areas, altitude/time limits and air-traffic procedures. The AIP records do not say that a warehouse or port approval covers a public park. [Rawang AIP SUP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2025-04-17-AIRAC/html/eSUP/WM-eSUP-25-26-en-MS.html), [Bintulu AIP SUP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2026-01-22-AIRAC/html/eSUP/WM-eSUP-26-10-en-MS.html), [Samalaju AIP SUP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2026-06-11-AIRAC/html/eSUP/WM-eSUP-26-34-en-MS.html)
- **Analyst's read:** A weekly VLOS pilot is a practical first phase, subject to CAAM ATF and local/site approvals. A dock-based BVLOS pilot needs a separate SUP case, an accepted SORA or PDRA route, pilot competence set by the approval, and third-party insurance. CAAM's CAD 6011 Part V does not allow fully autonomous operations where a remote pilot cannot intervene. [CAAM Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf), [CAD 6011 Part V](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/CAD-6011-V-Unmanned-Aircraft-System-SUP-ISS01_REV01.pdf)
- CAAM's 2026 Low Altitude Economy framework is still a proposal in the sources reviewed; comments closed on 2 September 2026. The Edge reported future targets for a UAS traffic-management platform and near-instant approvals for low-risk drones up to 250 g. Those reports do not establish a live shortcut for a camera-equipped township patrol. [CAAM consultation](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/publications/), [The Edge report](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/814623)
- Public-sector sources confirm adjacent drone uses for catchment work and government search and rescue. MBSA search snippets also describe municipal monitoring, but the portal pages could not be opened. These sources do not establish routine preventive water-edge safety patrols by SDP's named councils. LUAS and JPS reported a 2024 data-sharing arrangement covering 280 telemetry stations; this is a water-monitoring baseline, not a drone programme. [Air Selangor Sustainability Report 2024](https://hydrohub.airselangor.com/content/uploads/2025/05/Air-Selangor-Sustainability-Report-2024-Full-compressed.pdf), [MBSA IRIS](https://iris.mbsa.gov.my/homepage.php), [AIC 02/26](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2.%202026-01-22/html/eAIC/WM-eAIC-26-02-en-MS.html), [LUAS-JPS data-sharing MOU](https://www.luas.gov.my/en/news/memorandum-of-understanding-mou-signing-ceremony-for-cooperation-in-the-sharing-of-hydrological-data-and-water-resources-management-information-between-selangor-water-management-authority-luas-and-department-of-irrigation-and-drainage-malaysia-jps)
- If the target flight date is 23 October 2026, a new application filed on 17 October cannot meet CAAM's 14-working-day lead time. A flight sooner would require an existing approval that expressly covers the site, aircraft, date, activity and airspace, along with site, imagery and air-traffic approvals. No Safe Edge approval document was provided for this check. Use a clearly labelled simulation for the hackathon demonstration. [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)

## Findings

### 1. Legal basis and changes through 2026

The current legal basis is the Civil Aviation Act 1969 (Act 3) and CAR 2016, listed by the Ministry of Transport and published as the regulation text. CAR 2016 regulates UAS in Part XVI. [MOT legislation list](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/aviation/acts-and-policy), [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)

- Regulation 140 says a small UA must not fly in Class A, B, C or G airspace, within an aerodrome traffic zone, or above 400 feet above ground without permission from the Director General. Class G is uncontrolled airspace, so it should not be described as controlled airspace. Regulation 141 requires authorisation for aerial work. [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf), [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)
- Regulation 142 permits a small UA only when operated safely and in direct, unaided visual contact. CAR defines small UA by a mass not over 20 kg, including attached equipment at take-off. Regulation 144 requires approval for aircraft above that threshold. [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)
- Regulation 143 requires authorisation for an SUsA to fly over or within 150 m of a designated residential, commercial, industrial or recreational area, and over or within 150 m of an open-air assembly of more than 1,000 people. It also restricts flight within 50 m of a person, vessel, vehicle or structure not under the remote pilot’s control, and within 30 m of any person during take-off or landing. These are authorisation triggers and operating constraints, not a declaration that an approved operation is safe. [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)
- CAR 2016 regulation 4(3) exempts small UA and small unmanned surveillance UA from the ordinary aircraft registration provisions. CAAM’s current guidance says registration and markings may be required for UA over 20 kg if CAAM requires them. I found no current rule making every sub-20 kg drone registerable as a precondition to a standard ATF. [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf), [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/aviation-professional/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)

**What changed, and what did not:**

- CAAM’s Advisory Information 02/2024, dated 26 April 2024, sets revised fees effective 1 January 2025. Its affected UAS registration and identification fee rows carry a footnote deferring them until implementation of the UAS Traffic Management System. It also lists SUP authorisation fees by SORA or PDRA route. Treat the deferred rows as deferred, not as current universal ATF prerequisites. [CAAM Advisory Information 02/2024](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/AI-02_2024_CAAM-ANNOUNCES-REVISION-TO-ITS-FEES-AND-CHARGES.pdf)
- CAAM’s February 2025 Safety Information SI 01/2025 reiterated that an ATF is required for UAS operations, including operations related to training. [CAAM SI 01/2025](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/02/SI-01_2025-ATF-Application-for-UAS-Operations-Including-Operations-Related-to-Training.pdf)
- CAAM’s Circular 01/2025 took effect on 1 November 2025 and revised CAD 6011 Parts I and II. The current documents distinguish remote-pilot training organisation approval and agricultural operations; I found no universal remote-pilot certificate requirement for the standard low-risk VLOS ATF route. The Standard ATF form asks for pilot identity and training details, and requests competency evidence “if available”; a SUP operation has additional competency requirements. [CAAM Circular 01/2025](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/CAC-01_2025-Revised-CADs-Requirements-and-Guidance.pdf), [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf), [CAD 6011 Part V](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/CAD-6011-V-Unmanned-Aircraft-System-SUP-ISS01_REV01.pdf)
- Current CAAM guidance still offers Standard ATF for low-risk VLOS or EVLOS operations, with no spraying or dispensing and below 400 feet AGL unless otherwise approved. It also describes a separate SUP approval route for higher-risk projects. A Certificate of Approval (COA) listed by CAAM is for a Remote Pilot Training Organisation (RPTO), not a general commercial park-inspection operator certificate. [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)

**Analyst's read:** The sources support separate ordinary ATF and project-level SUP routes. CAAM's 2024 advisory lists SORA operational authorisation at RM1,500 and PDRA operational authorisation at RM250; these are listed approval fees, not all-in project costs. The July 2026 report that 250,000 drones had been registered with CAAM does not amend regulation 4(3) or explain which registrations the figure counts. [CAAM Advisory Information 02/2024](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/AI-02_2024_CAAM-ANNOUNCES-REVISION-TO-ITS-FEES-AND-CHARGES.pdf), [Bernama report, 29 July 2026](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php/?id=2587496), [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)

### 2. Requirements for a commercial inspection flight in a township park

For a visual inspection, a camera-equipped drone will normally meet CAAM’s description of an SUsA because it acquires surveillance or other data. The company or person managing the aircraft is the applicant; the current CAAM form asks for an accountable manager and company registration details. [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/aviation-professional/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf)

The Standard ATF submission calls for the accountable manager’s details, pilot identity and training, UAS specification and SIRIM approval, risk assessment, site and time, and a KML/KMZ flight plan with activity, geofence, risk and boundary zones. It also lists landowner permission or an appointment letter, MCMC approval where applicable, and JUPEM approval for photography, video, mapping or similar activities where that approval applies. CAAM says JUPEM coordinates review with security bodies for UAS capable of surveillance or data acquisition, so imagery approval is a gate to resolve before capture. For automated flight assistance, the form asks for mission details and a screenshot. [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf), [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)

**Lead time and CAAM fee:** CAAM’s current guidance requires a complete Standard ATF application at least 14 working days before the planned flight and warns that late or incomplete applications may be rejected. Complex operations, including airport-area cases, may take longer. The SAYS explainer is dated 19 February 2025 and simplifies the fee as applying to drones under 20 kg. CAAM’s official fee page is narrower: Item 97 lists RM250 per SUsA for each proposed location area with a radius of less than 15 nautical miles; a larger area requires another form. Multiple locations may be listed, with the fee scaling by aircraft and location area. One application may cover up to 90 days. This is the fee listed on CAAM’s page as accessed 17 October 2026, not a vendor quote or a fee per patrol. [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [CAAM fee page](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/aviation-professional/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [SAYS explainer, 19 February 2025](https://says.com/my/seismik/cara-mohon-permit-untuk-kendalikan-dron-di-malaysia)

**Consent, police and insurance:**

- The form expressly requires landowner permission or an appointment letter. If SDP owns or manages the chosen edge, obtain written SDP permission; if MBSA owns or manages the park or requires a municipal flight booking, check the IRIS route. Opened on 20 October 2026, MBSA's IRIS drone form books dated flight slots for MBSA departments and third parties and shows no fee or lead time; see [the approval path note](/docs/research/drone-integration/approval-path.md#mbsas-drone-form). [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf), [MBSA IRIS drone request](https://iris.mbsa.gov.my/dron.php)
- CAAM says JUPEM coordinates background/security checks with the relevant government security bodies for UAS capable of surveillance or imagery. I found no blanket public rule requiring a separate police letter for every ordinary VLOS township flight; ask CAAM/JUPEM whether the exact imagery or site triggers a PDRM process. [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)
- The Standard ATF checklist does not list insurance. I found no universal published third-party insurance requirement for the ordinary Standard ATF route. Third-party liability insurance is, however, expressly required for a SUP holder. A vendor can still be required to carry insurance by its client or contract. [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf), [CAD 6011 Part V](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/CAD-6011-V-Unmanned-Aircraft-System-SUP-ISS01_REV01.pdf)

### 3. BVLOS and drone-in-a-box operations

CAAM handles BVLOS as a Special UAS Project. Its CAD 6011 Part V sets out the SUP approval framework, including a Concept of Operations and an operational safety case using SORA or a Pre-Defined Risk Assessment (PDRA). The case must address air and ground risk, mitigations and the relevant air-traffic interaction. CAAM identifies accountable management and safety/flight-operations roles; one person may hold several posts depending on the operation’s nature and scale and the person’s competence and capacity. [CAD 6011 Part V](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/CAD-6011-V-Unmanned-Aircraft-System-SUP-ISS01_REV01.pdf), [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)

The remote pilot must hold the competency defined by the particular SUP approval; the cited CAD does not make RCoC-B plus a mission module a universal condition for every SUP or BVLOS operation. The SUP holder must maintain valid third-party liability insurance. CAD 6011 Part V also prohibits autonomous operations, defined there as operations in which the remote pilot cannot intervene. Pre-programmed or automated flight remains a different case if the remote pilot can intervene and the approval permits it. SORA is a safety-assessment route, not an approval in itself. [CAD 6011 Part V](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/CAD-6011-V-Unmanned-Aircraft-System-SUP-ISS01_REV01.pdf)

The following are the four nested/dock BVLOS cases I could confirm in the published AIP material reviewed:

### 4. Airspace around City of Elmina

The current AIP lists Sultan Abdul Aziz Shah Airport (Subang, WMSA) and its CTR as Class C, extending from the surface to 1,500 feet AMSL. Its western semicircle extends 5 nautical miles from the airport reference point at 3°07′52″N 101°32′53″E. [WMSA AIP, AIRAC 6 August 2026](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2026-08-06-AIRAC/html/eAIP/WM-AD-2.WMSA-en-MS.html)

**Analyst's read:** Using the mapped representative point for Elmina Valley Central Park (3.17901°N, 101.51425°E), the straight-line distance to the WMSA reference point is approximately 6.5 km, or about 3.5 nautical miles, on a northwesterly bearing. That point appears inside the western 5-nautical-mile semicircle. The exact water-edge coordinates and boundary are not established by the map point, so the CAAM KML/KMZ and ATS review must settle the actual site. [Park map point](https://mapcarta.com/W866543394), [WMSA AIP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2026-08-06-AIRAC/html/eAIP/WM-AD-2.WMSA-en-MS.html)

The default CAAM Standard ATF operating profile is VLOS or EVLOS with no more than one visual observer, no dispensing, and below 400 feet AGL unless otherwise approved. That cap is separate from Subang’s CTR; the proposed flight would still need the required CAAM and air-traffic approvals. [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)

For an SUsA, regulation 143 requires authorisation for flight over or within 150 m of designated residential, commercial, industrial or recreational areas and over or within 150 m of an open-air assembly of more than 1,000 people. It also sets the 50 m condition for a person, vessel, vehicle or structure not under the remote pilot’s control, plus 30 m from any person during take-off or landing. This is not a general permission to fly elsewhere; the applicable ATF and risk controls still apply. [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)

**Restricted/danger areas:** In the ENR 5.1 AIP page reviewed, effective 25 March 2025, I found no listed prohibited, restricted or danger area matching the representative Elmina point. This is not a live clearance: check the current AIP, NOTAMs and temporary AIP supplements for the selected date and coordinates. [AIP ENR 5.1](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2025-03-25/html/eAIP/WM-ENR-5.1-en-MS.html), [CAAM AIS](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/ais/)

**Night and distance from buildings:** Not found: the current CAAM UAS guidance and CAR provisions reviewed do not state a general night-flight permission or a universal night-flight ban for this standard route. The Standard ATF submission requires flight dates/times and risk assessment, so night operation must be stated and expressly resolved with CAAM/ATS. Regulation 143’s 50 m structure/person condition is the relevant SUsA proximity threshold; its 30 m condition applies to people during take-off or landing. [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf), [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf)

### 5. Upcoming framework, sandbox and roadmap

CAAM published a proposed Malaysia Low Altitude Economy (LAE) framework for public consultation on 3 August 2026, with comments due by 2 September 2026. That consultation period has closed. The document is a proposal, not an operating permit or a replacement for CAR 2016. [CAAM publications](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/publications/)

The Edge reported in August 2026 that a low-altitude framework rollout was expected in the fourth quarter of 2026 and described a planned digital drone traffic-management platform, including near-instant approvals for drones weighing 250 g or less by the end of 2026. These are reported future targets, not live approvals. The reported light-drone pathway would not establish permission for a heavier camera patrol at Elmina. [The Edge report, August 2026](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/814623)

MOSTI’s Malaysia Drone Technology Action Plan 2022–2030 (MDTAP30) sets a national technology and industry direction; it is not an aviation permission. Futurise describes the Cyberjaya Drone Testing Zone as the first CAAM-approved zone, established on 1 July 2019, and says the zone does not exempt flights from CAR/CAD or permit requirements. The NTIS event page says AirAsia/Teleport worked closely with CAAM to meet regulatory requirements for its 2021 drone-delivery sandbox; it does not itself say CAAM authorised the launch. A sandbox is a way to structure a bounded trial, not a waiver of applicable flight approvals. [MOSTI MDTAP30](https://www.mosti.gov.my/berita/pelan-tindakan-teknologi-dron-malaysia-2022-2030-mdtap30/), [Futurise Drone Testing Zone](https://www.futurise.com.my/index.php/drone-testing-zone/), [NTIS AirAsia/Teleport sandbox](https://sandbox.gov.my/events/launch-of-ntis-air-asia-drone-delivery-sandbox)

## Recommendations for Safe Edge

1. **Start with the Standard ATF VLOS route** (Findings 1, 2, 4 and 7). Design the pilot around a hired operator, exact KMZ/KML coordinates, written owner permission, human review, daylight hours and an access-control plan. Treat Subang CTR coordination as a gating item.
2. **Keep docked BVLOS as a later phase** (Finding 3). Present a SUP/SORA pathway, not a near-term deployment promise. Show the approval boundary and safety-case work in the product roadmap.
3. **Make the demo explicit about simulation** (Finding 7). Add a visible “simulated patrol” label and distinguish real site context from generated patrol data, detections and repair outcomes.
4. **Pitch the closed repair loop** (Finding 6). Position the new value as defect-to-owner-to-repair-to-independent-retest for water edges, not “first drone monitoring” or autonomous certification.
5. **Budget the unresolved approvals before claiming feasibility** (Findings 2 and 7). Get written answers on asset ownership, MBSA involvement, JUPEM imagery approval, Subang ATS coordination, insurance and vendor price. Public fee pages do not cover all of these costs.

## What we can and cannot claim

**Safe claims for the pitch**

- “Malaysia has a current Standard ATF route for low-risk VLOS drone operations, with a published 14-working-day lead time and a CAAM-listed SUsA fee.” [CAAM UAS guidance](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [CAAM fee page](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/aviation-professional/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)
- “CAAM has published site-specific nested BVLOS drone-in-a-box operations at a Rawang warehouse and Bintulu industrial-port sites.” [Rawang AIP SUP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2025-04-17-AIRAC/html/eSUP/WM-eSUP-25-26-en-MS.html), [Bintulu AIP SUP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/10.%202026-01-22-AIRAC/html/eSUP/WM-eSUP-26-10-en-MS.html), [Samalaju AIP SUP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/34.%202026-06-11-AIRAC/html/eSUP/WM-eSUP-26-34-en-MS.html)
- “The prototype simulates a proposed inspection and repair-verification workflow for an SDP park setting.” Use this only if the demo actually implements that workflow and visibly separates real site context from simulated flight and defect data; no demo or code was provided for this check.
- “The AI raises review flags; it does not certify an edge safe.” Use this only if it matches the implemented AI behavior. No model or validation evidence was provided for this check.

**Claims to avoid**

- “All commercial drone pilots need RCoC-B” or “every drone under 20 kg must be registered”: the current Standard ATF form and CAR 2016 do not support those blanket claims. [CAR 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf), [Standard ATF form](https://www.caam.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2026/05/STANDARD-ATF-FORM-03-01-New-Logo.pdf)
- “The RM250 fee covers any drone under 20 kg” or “RM250 covers unlimited patrols”: CAAM lists that amount for an SUsA and applies aircraft/location multipliers and a 90-day application limit. [CAAM fee page](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/aviation-professional/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)
- “Elmina is cleared for drone flight” or “a Rawang/Bintulu dock approval proves our dock can fly”: the mapped Elmina point appears inside Subang CTR, and AIP dock approvals are site- and condition-specific. [WMSA AIP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2026-08-06-AIRAC/html/eAIP/WM-AD-2.WMSA-en-MS.html), [Rawang AIP SUP](https://aip.caam.gov.my/aip/eAIP/2025-04-17-AIRAC/html/eSUP/WM-eSUP-25-26-en-MS.html)
- “Near-instant approval for drones up to 250 g is available now” or “LAE rules will be live by the hackathon”: the cited reports describe future targets and a proposed framework. [CAAM consultation](https://www.caam.gov.my/resources/publications/), [The Edge report](https://theedgemalaysia.com/node/814623)
- “The simulation proves real-world detection accuracy or that an edge is safe”: no real Safe Edge flight or validated detection result was provided for this report; such a claim would misrepresent the prototype.

## Open questions

- **Exact asset and authority:** Which water edge and access path will the pilot cover, who owns/maintains it, and does MBSA require a separate booking or consent? The MBSA pages timed out; the search snippets did not disclose a fee or approval time.
- **Flight file and airspace:** What are the surveyed boundary and launch coordinates? CAAM/Subang ATS must confirm how the specific route inside the apparent CTR can be handled.
- **Imagery and security:** Does JUPEM require a permit or security review for this aircraft, sensor, data type and site? CAAM’s public material points to JUPEM review but does not publish an Elmina-specific timeline or price.
- **Operating conditions and cover:** Will CAAM accept a recurring weekly schedule and daylight-only operation under one ATF application? What insurance cover will SDP require for a VLOS vendor? Not found in public CAAM/MBSA material reviewed.
- **BVLOS cost and local precedents:** Not found in the reviewed sources: an all-in SUP/SORA approval quote or processing timeline, or an official source establishing routine DBKL/MBPJ preventive water-edge drone inspection. The fee schedule does list route-specific SORA and PDRA approval fees.
