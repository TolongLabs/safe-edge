## Summary

- Malaysia's PDPA applies to identifiable personal data processed in connection with commercial transactions. The Commissioner's FAQ expressly lists photographs and CCTV images as examples when a living person can be identified. Analyst's read: Sime Darby Property is likely a controller if it decides why and how the inspection images are collected; confirm the legal entity and any council role against the pilot contracts. [Act 709](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), [PDP Commissioner FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/faq/)
- The 2024 amendment's DPO and breach-notification provisions took effect on 1 June 2025. DPO appointment is required if any listed threshold applies, including regular and systematic monitoring. The 2026 DPIA guideline calls for an assessment when processing is likely to create high risk. Analyst's read: repeat drone patrols are likely to count as regular and systematic monitoring, so confirm DPO and DPIA duties before live capture. [Commencement Gazette](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PENETAPAN-TARIKH-PERMULAAN-KUAT-KUASA-1.pdf), [DPO Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_ENG.pdf), [DPIA Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Data-Protection-Impact-Assessment-Guideline-DPIA.pdf)
- Civil Aviation Regulations 2016 contain rules for small unmanned surveillance aircraft, aerial work, airspace and altitude. A water-edge inspection may fall within more than one rule. CAAM currently says Standard ATF applications must arrive at least 14 working days before the activity. The exact aircraft and operation need CAAM classification. [Civil Aviation Regulations 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf), [CAAM UAS page](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)
- International guidance supports route limits, recording controls, notice, human-image filtering, short retention, encryption, access logging and DPIAs. These are design references, not Malaysian legal rules. [EASA Privacy by Design](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/privacy-by-design), [EDPB Video Guidelines](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf), [Singapore PDPC guidance](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-selected-topics/advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa-for-selected-topics-%28revised-may-2024%29.pdf)
- Malaysian community evidence exists for public-health drone use. A 2026 Selangor study found support for targeted mosquito-habitat monitoring when purpose, data use and flight boundaries were clear; many households asked for advance notice. These findings do not predict acceptance of water-edge infrastructure inspection. [Dapari et al., 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371/journal.pntd.0013979)
- A 2021 psychoacoustic study measured maximum A-weighted levels of 46.1 dB for a Tello, 51.8 dB for a Mavic 2 and 66.8 dB for a Matrice 210 in a particular 10 m hover setup. These are study results, not a Malaysian noise limit or a prediction for Safe Edge. [Christian and Cabell, 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijerph/ijerph-18-08893/article_deploy/ijerph-18-08893.pdf?version=1629794116)
- Analyst's read: Safe Edge should retain defect evidence, not park surveillance. Its default should be no persistent image containing a person, with a fail-closed route and community process before any live flight.

## Findings

### 1. Malaysian PDPA, amendment, DPO, breach notification and children

The Personal Data Protection Act 2010 (Act 709) came into force on 15 November 2013. It regulates processing of personal data in connection with commercial transactions and excludes Federal and State Governments. Personal data relates to an identified or identifiable living individual. The Act defines processing broadly, including collection, recording, storage, use, disclosure and erasure. [PDP Commissioner, effective commencement date](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/determination-of-effective-commencement-date/), [Act 709, sections 2 and 4](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf)

The Commissioner's FAQ treats photographs and CCTV images as personal data when a living person can be identified. Analyst's read: being in a public park does not by itself make an identifiable image non-personal data. [PDP Commissioner FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/faq/)

The Act's definitions distinguish a controller, who processes or controls or authorises processing, from a processor that processes on behalf of a controller and not for its own purposes. Analyst's read: if Sime Darby Property determines the purpose and means of recurring park-edge image collection, it is likely the controller; a contractor following documented instructions may be a processor. Confirm the entity, council role after handover, and allocation of duties against the pilot contracts and asset ownership. [Act 709](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), [PDP Commissioner FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/faq/)

The Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024 (Act A1727) received Royal Assent on 9 October 2024 and was published in the Gazette on 17 October 2024. The commencement order brought sections 7, 11, 13 and 14 into force on 1 January 2025; sections 2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 10 and 12 on 1 April 2025; and sections 6 and 9 on 1 June 2025. The DPO and breach-notification duties took effect in the 1 June 2025 tranche. [Act A1727](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Act-A1727.pdf), [P.U.(B) 522 commencement order](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/12/PENETAPAN-TARIKH-PERMULAAN-KUAT-KUASA-1.pdf)

The Commissioner's 2025 DPO guideline says a controller or processor must appoint a DPO if any one of these conditions applies: personal data of more than 20,000 data subjects; sensitive personal data, including financial information, of more than 10,000 data subjects; or activities requiring regular and systematic monitoring of personal data. The controller or processor must register the DPO and submit business contact information within 21 days of appointment. The guideline lists CCTV and connected devices as examples of activities that may involve regular and systematic monitoring; it does not add a large-scale condition to that test. [DPO Guideline, version 1.0 issued 25 February 2025](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_ENG.pdf)

Analyst's read: recurring patrols along the same public edge are likely to fit regular and systematic monitoring. Treat a DPO as required for planning purposes unless the DPO or Malaysian counsel records a reasoned contrary view.

Section 12B(1) of the amended Act says a controller with reason to believe a personal-data breach occurred must notify the Commissioner as soon as practicable. Section 12B(2) requires notice to affected people when the breach causes or is likely to cause significant harm, without unnecessary delay. The 2025 Commissioner's guideline says not all breaches are notifiable to the Commissioner and applies a significant-harm test; it defines significant scale as more than 1,000 affected people. The Commissioner's portal repeats that only breaches meeting significant-harm criteria are mandatory to report. Keep the Act's wording and the guideline's reporting criteria distinct in the incident runbook, and have the DPO or counsel approve how to handle breaches that do not meet the guideline threshold. [Act A1727, section 12B](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Act-A1727.pdf), [DBN Guideline, version 1.0 issued 25 February 2025](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_ENG.pdf), [Commissioner breach-reporting portal](https://daftar.pdp.gov.my/v1/dbn)

For a breach that meets the guideline's Commissioner-notification criteria, the guideline says to notify as soon as practicable and no later than 72 hours from occurrence. Its examples start the clock when the controller is informed of a lost device, realises an unauthorised disclosure, confirms an intrusion, or is informed by a processor, depending on the case. Required notice to affected people must be made without unnecessary delay and no later than seven days after the initial Commissioner notification. Significant-harm examples include physical harm, financial loss, negative credit-record effects, property damage or loss, unlawful misuse, sensitive personal data, data combinations enabling identity fraud, and significant scale. [DBN Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_ENG.pdf), [Commissioner breach-reporting portal](https://daftar.pdp.gov.my/v1/dbn)

The amendment added biometric data as sensitive personal data. The statutory definition is personal data resulting from technical processing relating to physical, physiological or behavioural characteristics of a person. It does not include a unique-identification qualifier. An ordinary identifiable photograph is personal data; whether a particular image is biometric data depends on how it is technically processed. [Act A1727](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Act-A1727.pdf), [Act 709, section 4](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf)

For a child under 18, regulation 3(3) of the Personal Data Protection Regulations 2013 requires the data user to obtain consent from a parent, guardian or person with parental responsibility. Analyst's read: this does not create a blanket drone or CCTV ban. [Personal Data Protection Regulations 2013, regulation 3(3)](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Peraturan-peraturan-Perlindungan-Data-Peribadi.pdf)

The Commissioner's current general-practice index lists guidance on DPIAs, data protection by design, automated decision-making and profiling, DPOs and breach notification. The 2026 DPIA guideline says an assessment is required where processing is likely to create high risk. Its screening considers scale, including more than 20,000 data subjects or more than 10,000 data subjects for sensitive data, and qualitative risk factors such as systematic monitoring, innovative technology, location or behaviour tracking, and children or vulnerable people. Analyst's read: repeated location-linked drone imagery and likely incidental capture of children make a DPIA strongly indicated before live testing, even if the final camera and storage design is not settled. [General-practice index](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/personal-data-protection-general-practice/), [2026 DPIA Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Data-Protection-Impact-Assessment-Guideline-DPIA.pdf), [2026 Data Protection by Design Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Data-Protection-By-Design-Guideline-DPbD.pdf)

The Commissioner's Aviation Sector Code of Practice covers general personal-data practices for its sector and is not a drone-specific code. Its current listing does not state an issue date. Targeted searches of the Commissioner's current practice and guidance listings for “drone”, “UAS”, “CCTV”, “dron”, “kamera” and “pengawasan” did not locate a drone-imagery privacy code. Analyst's read: that search result does not prove that no relevant local requirement exists. [Aviation Sector Code page](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/personal-data-protection-practices-for-the-malaysian-aviation-sector/), [Aviation Sector Code PDF](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Code_of_Practice_For_Aviation_Sector.pdf), [General-practice index](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/akta/personal-data-protection-general-practice/)

### 2. Malaysian drone, privacy, Penal Code and local rules

The Civil Aviation Regulations 2016 define a small unmanned surveillance aircraft as a small unmanned aircraft equipped to undertake surveillance or data acquisition. Regulation 141 separately requires Director General authorisation for an unmanned aircraft system flown for aerial work. The Regulations define aerial work to include specialised services such as photography, surveying, observation and patrol. A water-edge inspection may fall within this rule; ask CAAM to classify the exact activity and authorisation path. [Civil Aviation Regulations 2016, regulations 1 and 141](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)

Regulation 143 requires Director General authorisation for a small unmanned surveillance aircraft flown over or within 150 metres of a designated area, over or within 150 metres of an open-air assembly of more than 1,000 people, within 50 metres of a person or of an uncontrolled vessel, vehicle or structure, or within 30 metres of a person during take-off or landing. “Designated area” includes residential, commercial, industrial and recreational areas. Regulation 140 restricts flights in Class A, B, C or G airspace, within an aerodrome traffic zone, and above 400 feet above the earth's surface without authorisation. A camera-equipped water-edge inspection drone may meet the surveillance-aircraft definition; that is an Analyst's read for CAAM to confirm. [Civil Aviation Regulations 2016, regulations 1, 140 and 143](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf)

CAAM's current UAS page says a Standard Authorisation to Fly (ATF) application for a low-risk operation should be submitted at least 14 working days before the activity. It states that standard processing resumed on 30 March 2026. This timeline applies to that application process; it does not decide whether a particular inspection is aerial work or what other authorisations apply. [CAAM UAS page](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/)

Section 509 of the Penal Code addresses acts done with intent to insult a person's modesty, including intrusion upon privacy. It does not say every drone photograph is a criminal offence; intent and surrounding facts matter. [Penal Code, Act 574, section 509](https://lom.agc.gov.my/ilims/upload/portal/akta/outputaktap/1823715_BI/Act%20574%20Project%202%20Final.pdf)

MBSA's IRIS drone form, opened on 20 October 2026, books flight slots and asks for the date, time, area, purpose and a supporting letter; see [the approval path note](/docs/research/drone-integration/approval-path.md#mbsas-drone-form). Analyst's read: the form alone would not establish a universal MBSA drone-permit rule. DBKL's FAQ says that drone use for filming in areas it manages requires prior CAAM, JUPEM and other approvals before applying to DBKL. That is a specific DBKL process, not an MBSA rule. [MBSA IRIS drone form](https://iris.mbsa.gov.my/dron.php) (snippet-only), [DBKL filming FAQ](https://www.dbkl.gov.my/soalan-lazim-mengikut-jabatan)

Targeted searches of MBSA pages for “undang-undang kecil dron”, “dron taman”, “permit dron” and “undang-undang kecil taman” did not locate a drone-specific park by-law. This is a search result, not proof that no local condition exists. Confirm directly with MBSA before a live flight.

Bernama reported that CAAM had received 57 complaints about drone misuse since 2020 as of its 19 August 2024 report, including complaints involving drones entering residential areas. This is a reported complaint count, not a finding that each complaint was substantiated. [Bernama, 19 August 2024](https://www.bernama.com/en/news.php?id=2330689)

On 15 December 2025, Majoriti reported a Sentul Perdana resident's allegation that a drone flew close to an apartment and might have filmed occupants. The report describes an allegation, not a court finding. Targeted searches did not locate a Malaysian court decision specifically deciding a privacy claim about a drone filming homes or people; that search is not proof that no such decision exists. [Majoriti, 15 December 2025](https://majoriti.com.my/berita/2025/12/15/video-039repot-caam-polis039-pemilik-drone-boleh-dikenakan-tindakan-jika-ganggu-privasi-penduduk)

### 3. International privacy good practice

The UK ICO's drone guidance advises assessing collateral capture through a DPIA, telling people about surveillance, limiting the field of view, avoiding private property, controlling when recording is on, securing footage and keeping it only as long as needed. The ICO page says the guidance is under review following UK legislative change. It is UK guidance, not Malaysian law. [UK ICO, UAS and drones](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/additional-considerations-for-technologies-other-than-cctv/unmanned-aerial-systems-uas-drones/)

EASA's privacy-by-design material gives drone controls such as geofencing approved routes, recording only at selected locations or times, switching recording off outside the task, device access controls, encryption, detecting and blurring or hiding people, logging access and deletion, and erasing onboard data after transfer when no longer needed. Its DPIA material identifies repeated flights over the same public area, capable cameras and capture of children or vulnerable people as risk factors. Its voluntary code advises against targeting or systematically recording people without their knowledge and calls for paths that minimise views into homes. These are European good practices, not Malaysian legal requirements. [EASA Privacy by Design](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/privacy-by-design), [EASA DPIA](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/data-protection-impact-assessment), [EASA Code of Conduct](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/code-of-conduct)

The EDPB's video-device guidance recommends layered notice, privacy masking, access controls, DPIAs for large-scale public monitoring and retention no longer than necessary. It says storage beyond 72 hours needs stronger justification under its guidance. This is EU GDPR guidance, not a Malaysian statutory retention limit. [EDPB Guidelines 3/2019 on video devices](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf)

Singapore's PDPC 2024 selected-topics guidance has a drone-specific chapter. It says organisations should consider whether drones are likely to capture personal data; drones with cameras are subject to the same PDPA considerations as similar equipment. Where consent is required, organisations should notify people of the purposes and whether photo, video or audio recording is taking place, with notice placed to enable awareness before entry where practical. Where a public-data exception applies, notice is still good practice. The guidance also says the organisation remains responsible when a contracted drone operator captures data on its behalf. This is Singapore guidance, not a Malaysian rule. [Singapore PDPC, Advisory Guidelines on the PDPA for Selected Topics, revised 23 May 2024](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-selected-topics/advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa-for-selected-topics-%28revised-may-2024%29.pdf)

The Australian OAIC says covered organisations should tell people before recording, protect captured personal information, and destroy or de-identify it when no longer needed. The Australian Government's drone privacy guide recommends a privacy impact assessment, collecting only what is needed, de-identifying or destroying incidental personal information, avoiding backyards and windows, and providing a contact for questions or complaints. Applicability of Australia's Privacy Act depends on the organisation; these are practical references, not Malaysian law. [OAIC, Drones](https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/your-privacy-rights/surveillance-and-monitoring/drones), [Australian Government, Drone privacy guidelines](https://www.drones.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/drone_privacy_guidelines.pdf)

### 4. Evidence on public acceptance

A Singapore online survey administered from 17 April to 4 June 2019 included 1,050 residents and permanent residents. On a six-point scale, reported mean acceptance was 3.87 for residential-area use, 4.40 for recreational use, 4.30 for commercial use and 4.58 for industrial use. The authors found more support for society-wide applications such as search and rescue or disaster management than for photography and other individual-benefit uses. Analyst's read: this Singapore sample is not a prediction of Elmina residents' views. [Tan et al., “Public acceptance of drone applications in a highly urbanized environment”, Technology in Society, 2021](https://www3.ntu.edu.sg/CorpComms2/Research%20Papers/Public%20acceptance%20of%20drone%20applications%20in%20a%20highly%20urbanized%20environment.pdf)

A 2023 Malaysian student study surveyed 113 college and high-school students. It reports privacy and safety concerns and limited awareness of drone applications. Its student sample cannot establish acceptance among township residents or parents. [Journal of Engineering, 2023](https://www.ukm.my/jkukm/wp-content/uploads/2023/si6-2/16.pdf)

A 2022 cross-sectional study of dengue-surveillance drones included 1,826 participants across Malaysia, Mexico and Turkey, including 619 in Malaysia. It asked about drone surveillance for mosquito breeding sites around homes, a mobile alert system and community training. Its abstract reports widespread support for mosquito surveillance. This is relevant Malaysian evidence, but it does not establish support for infrastructure inspection or represent all Malaysian residents. [Annan et al., 2022, PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35644475/), [Malaysian Ministry of Health MaHTAS assessment](https://www.moh.gov.my/images/04-penerbitan/penerbitan-klinikal/mahtas/FR%20-%20UNMANNED%20AIRCRAFT%20SYSTEMS%20FOR%20DENGUE%20SURVEILLANCE.pdf), [University of Malaya repository abstract](https://knova.um.edu.my/research_publications_2021_2025/1023/)

A 2025 rural Malaysia study surveyed 190 adults using stratified purposive sampling across Kelantan, Terengganu, Pahang, Johor, Kedah and Perlis. It measured willingness to use a drone-related dengue app and attend mosquito-control training, not acceptance of township asset inspections. Open-ended responses raised privacy, trust and technology-access concerns. [Dom et al., PLOS One, published 30 April 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0322321)

A 2026 qualitative Selangor study used 480 household interviews across eight localities and six public-health expert interviews. Most participants supported targeted mosquito-habitat monitoring when it was distinguished from personal surveillance and purpose, data use and operational boundaries were clear; many requested advance flight notice. The study also reports some privacy or discomfort concerns near homes. It is context-specific qualitative evidence, not a representative poll for water-edge patrols. A 13 May 2026 correction changed the corresponding-author information; it did not change the findings. [Dapari et al., PLOS Neglected Tropical Diseases, published 12 March 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371/journal.pntd.0013979), [PLOS correction, 13 May 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371/journal.pntd.0014316)

A German virtual-reality study published on 11 November 2024 involved 10 people; one was excluded from analysis because of sound problems, leaving nine for most results. Participants rated residential and park scenarios more negatively than industrial scenarios and reported feeling watched or disturbed. It is exploratory evidence from a very small, non-representative sample. [Stolz et al., CEAS Aeronautical Journal](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13272-024-00785-z)

Analyst's read: the evidence supports a clear public-safety purpose, route and time transparency, visible operator accountability and consultation before flights. It does not quantify how much a notice or consultation changes acceptance. The searches found Malaysian public-health studies but no representative survey of township residents on recurring water-edge infrastructure inspections. Test the actual route, notice and aircraft with nearby residents before proposing recurring patrols.

### 5. Noise and annoyance

Analyst's read: the reviewed 2021 study tested several heights, including 10 m and 30 m above ground; the Phantom was tested at 27 m instead of 30 m, and the Tello only at 10 m. These are research conditions, not an inspection-altitude standard. [Christian and Cabell, 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijerph/ijerph-18-08893/article_deploy/ijerph-18-08893.pdf?version=1629794116)

The 2021 psychoacoustic study tested four DJI quadcopters. In the 10 m hover condition, it measured maximum A-weighted levels (L_A,F,max) of 46.1 dB for the Tello, 51.8 dB for the Mavic 2 and 66.8 dB for the Matrice 210. The microphone was 1.2 m above ground and 10 m horizontally from the aircraft. These figures describe those aircraft, measurement positions and flight conditions. Analyst's read: they are not a general level for an inspection drone. [Christian and Cabell, “Quantification of the Psychoacoustic Effect of Noise from Small Unmanned Aerial Vehicles”, 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijerph/ijerph-18-08893/article_deploy/ijerph-18-08893.pdf?version=1629794116)

The same study found no significant difference in annoyance between the Tello and Mavic 2 in low-altitude hover despite their different measured levels. It also reported greater annoyance from hover than flyby at the same L_A,F,max. These are findings from a controlled listening study, not a field-flight prediction. [Christian and Cabell, 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijerph/ijerph-18-08893/article_deploy/ijerph-18-08893.pdf?version=1629794116)

Analyst's read: use a short, steady pass rather than hovering beside homes or playgrounds; schedule flights in daylight and lower-use periods; record the aircraft, height, route and complaints during any pilot. These are mitigations to test, not a guarantee of low annoyance.

Targeted searches of CAAM, Department of Environment and Ministry of Transport material for “drone noise”, “dron bunyi bising”, “UAS noise” and “noise annoyance” did not locate a Malaysia-specific drone-noise exposure limit or residential drone-annoyance standard. This is a search result, not proof that no applicable rule exists. Do not present the cited foreign measurements as a local legal limit.

### 6. Privacy design specification for Safe Edge

Analyst's read: the pilot should enforce the following as product defaults and operating rules. The sources provide legal duties or examples of good practice; the specific Safe Edge controls below are design recommendations.

1. Analyst's read: **Assign accountability before capture.** Name the SDP controller, any drone processor, the DPO, the asset owner after handover and the person authorised to approve each patrol. Put instructions, permitted purposes, onward sharing, incident response and deletion duties in the supplier agreement. Complete a DPIA before live flights. [Act 709](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), [DPO Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DPO_ENG.pdf), [DPIA Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Data-Protection-Impact-Assessment-Guideline-DPIA.pdf)

2. Analyst's read: **Constrain flight and camera geometry.** Use a fixed, approved water-edge route and geofence. Aim the camera at the asset; mask or exclude homes, windows, gardens, playgrounds and unrelated paths. Do not follow people. Stop capture when the aircraft leaves the route. Obtain the required CAAM and local approvals for the actual operation. [CAAM UAS page](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [Civil Aviation Regulations 2016](https://www.mot.gov.my/en/Documents/Civil-Aviation-Regulations-2016.pdf), [EASA Privacy by Design](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/privacy-by-design), [EASA Code of Conduct](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/code-of-conduct)

3. Analyst's read: **Capture only for the inspection task.** Prefer triggered still images at asset checkpoints over continuous video. Record only inside the approved route and capture window. Disable microphones. Do not collect audio or retain flight footage for general park observation. [UK ICO drone guidance](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/uk-gdpr-guidance-and-resources/cctv-and-video-surveillance/guidance-on-video-surveillance-including-cctv/additional-considerations-for-technologies-other-than-cctv/unmanned-aerial-systems-uas-drones/), [EASA Privacy by Design](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/privacy-by-design), [Singapore PDPC guidance](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-selected-topics/advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa-for-selected-topics-%28revised-may-2024%29.pdf)

4. Analyst's read: **Filter people before storage or upload.** Run person detection on the aircraft or local edge device before an image persists or leaves the site. Discard a frame if a person is detected or detection is uncertain; do not rely only on face blur. If detection cannot run, do not capture. For defect evidence that must be kept, mask the entire person and any visible vehicle plate, then retain only the smallest crop needed to document the defect. EASA recommends detecting and blurring or hiding people; the broader discard-first rule is a Safe Edge design recommendation. [EASA Privacy by Design](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/privacy-by-design), [PDP Commissioner FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/faq/)

5. Analyst's read: **Do not identify or profile park users.** Prohibit face recognition, biometric templates, number-plate recognition, identity matching, re-identification, person tracking, dwell-time scoring, behaviour or age inference, and use of captured images to train models. The amendment makes biometric data sensitive personal data; EASA's code advises against targeting or systematic recording of people. [Act A1727](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Act-A1727.pdf), [EASA Code of Conduct](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/code-of-conduct)

6. Analyst's read: **Store only defect evidence and maintenance metadata.** Encrypt data in transit and at rest. Use role-based access for the named maintenance owner and reviewer. Do not send raw frames to third-party systems or integrations; share only an approved, masked defect crop and the maintenance record needed to act. Log every view, export, change and deletion. [Act 709, security and retention principles](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), [EASA Privacy by Design](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/privacy-by-design)

7. Analyst's read: **Set automatic deletion.** Delete raw frames from the aircraft or edge device after the pass has been processed. Set a documented expiry for masked defect evidence based on the repair and verification need; delete it when that purpose ends. Permit a legal or incident hold only with a named reason, approver and review date. Malaysian law requires retention no longer than necessary; EDPB's 72-hour reference is guidance for its context, not a fixed Malaysian deadline. [Act 709, retention principle](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), [EDPB Video Guidelines](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf)

8. Analyst's read: **Give notice before flight.** Place bilingual Bahasa Malaysia and English notices at park entrances and the flight zone. Publish an accessible notice before the route with the operator/controller and contact, purpose, route and schedule, whether still images or video are captured, whether audio is off, retention, recipients and how to ask questions or complain. Provide a non-digital contact route. Use the Act's notice requirements and layered-notice examples from Singapore and the EDPB. [Act 709, section 7](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), [Singapore PDPC guidance](https://www.pdpc.gov.sg/-/media/files/pdpc/pdf-files/advisory-guidelines/ag-on-selected-topics/advisory-guidelines-on-the-pdpa-for-selected-topics-%28revised-may-2024%29.pdf), [EDPB Video Guidelines](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/sites/default/files/files/file1/edpb_guidelines_201903_video_devices_en_0.pdf)

9. Analyst's read: **Use a resident-tested flight window.** Consult the residents' association, SDP maintenance owner and relevant local authority before the first patrol. Start in daylight at a low-use time, avoid school arrival and dismissal periods, publish the flight calendar and provide a channel to report concerns. Pause or change the route if residents report capture of private spaces or repeated nuisance. The studies show that context and purpose matter; test the exact time window locally. [Singapore survey](https://www3.ntu.edu.sg/CorpComms2/Research%20Papers/Public%20acceptance%20of%20drone%20applications%20in%20a%20highly%20urbanized%20environment.pdf), [German VR study](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13272-024-00785-z), [Selangor community study](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371/journal.pntd.0013979)

10. Analyst's read: **Fail closed around children.** Treat schools, playgrounds and visible groups of children as no-capture zones. If a child enters the image, discard the frame before storage. Do not estimate age. If consent is the chosen basis for processing a person under 18, obtain it from a parent, guardian or person with parental responsibility. [Personal Data Protection Regulations 2013, regulation 3(3)](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/Peraturan-peraturan-Perlindungan-Data-Peribadi.pdf), [DPIA Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Data-Protection-Impact-Assessment-Guideline-DPIA.pdf)

11. Analyst's read: **Prepare breach response.** Require the drone operator to alert SDP immediately to a lost aircraft, exposed image store, unauthorised access or mistaken upload. Preserve access logs, route metadata and incident evidence. Have the DPO assess Commissioner and affected-person notification against the Act and current guideline. [Act A1727, section 12B](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/11/Act-A1727.pdf), [DBN Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/GP_DBN_ENG.pdf)

## Recommendations for Safe Edge

1. Analyst's read: **Pilot gate:** require a signed DPIA, named controller and DPO, CAAM classification and local-authority checklist before live capture. Rests on Findings 1 and 2.
2. Analyst's read: **Image gate:** make “detect person, then discard before persistence” the default. Demonstrate fail-closed behaviour in the simulation. Rests on Findings 1, 3 and 6.
3. Analyst's read: **Route gate:** show a privacy-exclusion layer around homes, windows and playgrounds, plus recording-on/off points and a fixed flight window. Rests on Findings 2, 3 and 6.
4. Analyst's read: **Evidence gate:** keep only masked defect crops with a recorded expiry, access log and named maintenance owner. Make raw-frame storage unavailable in the prototype. Rests on Findings 1, 3 and 6.
5. Analyst's read: **Community gate:** include a sample BM/English notice and resident feedback workflow in the demo. Present the flight schedule, capture purpose, retention and contact in plain language. Rests on Findings 3 and 4.
6. Analyst's read: **Noise gate:** plan a brief pass, avoid hovering near homes and measure aircraft-specific noise and complaints during any pilot. Do not promise a decibel level before selecting and testing hardware. Rests on Finding 5.
7. Analyst's read: **Pitch boundary:** describe Safe Edge as a defect-inspection aid that does not identify people or certify a water edge safe. Do not claim that blur, notices or a permit alone make the operation privacy-safe. Rests on Findings 1 through 6.

## What we can and cannot claim

**Safe claims**

- Malaysia's PDPA can treat an identifiable photo or CCTV image as personal data. [PDP Commissioner FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/faq/)
- The 2026 Commissioner guidance identifies systematic monitoring and innovative technology as DPIA risk factors. [DPIA Guideline](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Data-Protection-Impact-Assessment-Guideline-DPIA.pdf)
- Malaysian studies report public-health drone acceptance in specific mosquito-surveillance settings; the results do not establish support for township water-edge inspections. [Annan et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35644475/), [Dom et al., 2025](https://journals.plos.org/plosone/article?id=10.1371/journal.pone.0322321), [Dapari et al., 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371/journal.pntd.0013979)
- Analyst's read: Safe Edge can be designed to discard detected-person frames before storage and retain masked defect evidence only. This is a proposed product control informed by EASA guidance, not a result already proven by the simulation. [EASA Privacy by Design](https://www.easa.europa.eu/en/domains/civil-drones/privacy/privacy-by-design)

**Claims to avoid**

- “Public parks are outside the PDPA” or “blur makes every image anonymous.” Identifiable images can be personal data. Analyst's read: other details may still identify a person after face blur. [Act 709](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf), [PDP Commissioner FAQ](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/en/faq/)
- “Malaysian residents support drone patrols.” Malaysian evidence located in this review concerns mosquito surveillance and dengue prevention; it does not measure acceptance of water-edge infrastructure inspections. [Annan et al., 2022](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35644475/), [Dapari et al., 2026](https://journals.plos.org/plosntds/article?id=10.1371/journal.pntd.0013979)
- Analyst's read: avoid saying “one CAAM permit covers every approval” or “notice equals consent.” CAAM, local authority, data-protection duties and the chosen lawful basis are separate questions. [CAAM UAS page](https://www.caam.gov.my/public/unmanned-aircraft-system-uas/), [Act 709](https://www.pdp.gov.my/ppdpv1/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/UNDANG-UNDANG-MALAYSIA_AKTA_PERLINDUNGAN_DATA_PERIBADI_2010_709_MALAY_AND-ENG_V2022.pdf)
- “The drone is quiet” or “this dB figure is the Malaysian limit.” Published measurements vary by aircraft and test condition; targeted searches did not locate a Malaysia-specific drone limit. [Christian and Cabell, 2021](https://mdpi-res.com/d_attachment/ijerph/ijerph-18-08893/article_deploy/ijerph-18-08893.pdf?version=1629794116)

## Open questions

- Which SDP legal entity will determine the purpose and means of capture, and who controls evidence after any handover to a council?
- What are the exact asset boundary, airspace, aircraft class, camera resolution, altitude, flight frequency and CAAM/JUPEM/local approval path?
- Does CAAM classify the water-edge inspection as aerial work, small unmanned surveillance aircraft operation, or both?
- Can person and vehicle-plate filtering run on-device before an image is written or transmitted? What happens on a false negative?
- What defect-evidence retention period matches SDP's maintenance and verification process, and which roles need access?
- What do nearby residents, parents and the residents' association think after seeing the actual route, notice, aircraft and noise? The searches found adjacent Malaysian public-health studies, but no representative survey for the exact water-edge inspection use.
- Targeted searches did not locate a drone-specific JPDP privacy code, an MBSA drone-specific park by-law, a Malaysian court decision on drone-filming privacy, or a Malaysia-specific drone-noise standard. These are search results, not proof that no applicable requirement exists.
