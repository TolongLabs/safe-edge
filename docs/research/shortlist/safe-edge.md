# Safe Edge

Safe Edge helps Sime Darby Property close children's routes to dangerous drains in parks it maintains, then check that the repairs still work.

**First ask:** one retained drain asset, one named SDP maintenance owner and a repair budget. The proposed site is Elmina Central Park; access, custody and funding are not yet confirmed. **Track:** People, safety and security. **Rank:** see the [shortlist](/docs/research/shortlist.md).

## Problem

- **A ball can draw a child into a drain.** Of 21 news-reported clusters in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur township waters since 2023, 8 began in a drain. In 2 of those 8, Kajang in December 2025 and Bandar Mahkota Cheras in May 2026, children entered after a ball ([incident table][incidents]). Rain was reported in 4 of the 21 clusters, all drains; most reports state no weather. These selected reports are not an incidence rate or proof of a cause.
- **The harm extends beyond drains.** In August 2026 two boys aged 11 and 12 drowned at a township dam in Pasir Gudang, and residents asked for fencing and CCTV ([Bernama][bernama-pg]; [MalaysiaGazette][mgazette]). The Fire and Rescue Department counted 371 child and teen drowning deaths from January 2023 to August 2026 ([The Star][star-371]). Of the 21 clusters, 11 involved lakes, ponds or standing water; none of those reports mentions rain and several victims were found hours later ([SDP lakes][sdp-lakes]).
- **The SDP lake case supports inspection, not the child-route claim.** On 15 October 2026 a 30-year-old man went missing while fishing at Tasik Bukit Raja in Bandar Bukit Raja; Bomba recovered his body the next day ([Utusan][utusan]). The emergency call came at 10:37 pm; his motorcycle, fishing gear and slippers were by the lake ([SDP lakes][sdp-lakes]). We do not infer why he drowned. Night fishing and lake rescue remain later work.
- **Written checks need a field test of access.** MSMA asks for covers on drains deeper than 0.6 m with pedestrian access, a 1.2 m rail or grating for velocities above 2 and below 4 m/s, and pond banks of 1V:4H or gentler ([water-edge checks][checks]). Its public-safety provisions also call for protective measures in populated or pedestrian-access areas; a shallow drain is not exempt from safety review ([MSMA section 14.2.4.6][msma14]). Form G17 ties road and drain work to approved plans without a separate pedestrian-access item. MBSP's July 2026 checklist includes barriers beside drains 1.2 m wide or deeper than 0.9 m and pond signs; it also checks approved-plan conditions and allows other site findings ([MBSP][mbsp]). Whether our route test adds a finding is still unproven.
- **Funding and responsibility must meet at the same asset.** Developers prepay councils an Improvement Service Fund (ISF) under the Street, Drainage and Building Act 1974; MBDK charges RM5,000 per acre for drainage into an ISF trust fund ([MBDK][mbdk]). This does not establish a budget available to this pilot. A former SDP Head of Township said: “The bigger issue is not who owns the drain or pond after handover, but whether there is clear accountability for maintenance and public safety” ([consultation][consult]). Start where SDP retains that responsibility.

## Solution and how it works

The unit is one route from a path, gate or play area to one exposed drain edge and the cover, fence or gate intended to protect it. The engineer sets urgency; Safe Edge never computes a drowning-risk score.

1.  **Record and test.** Record the asset boundary, all approach routes, custodian, applicable authority, drawing revision and engineer. Tape, a 100 mm gauge, photos and a route sketch provide the decisive evidence, with method and uncertainty. Check MSMA and applicable approval conditions separately from optional child-barrier benchmarks and the team's ball-path prompt. Results are finding, no finding, not applicable or needs information, each with its source. Unknown depth blocks only the affected checks and gets an owner and due date; it never becomes a pass by default.
1.  **Fund and fix.** Report a missing cover, broken barrier or confirmed open route to the duty manager immediately. The custodian provides engineer-approved interim protection or closes the affected approach while the permanent remedy is decided. Before work, the engineer agrees the remedy and acceptance test and the budget holder authorises the priced work. Distinguish a contractual defect from a new safety upgrade; SDP budgets for the latter. Check drainage capacity, debris blockage, structural loading and maintenance access before adding a cover or fence.
1.  **Re-test and keep checking.** A reviewer who did not install the work repeats the same route and gauge checks, including both fence ends and any maintenance gate. Accept only with recorded measurements and evidence that the agreed test passes; rejection retains the earlier evidence and failed item. Assessment, repair and custody keep separate statuses. The internal “pass” covers only the listed routes and tests at that date, with a named custodian and review due within 12 months or earlier if the engineer requires it. Damage, changed access or overdue review suspends/expires it and opens a task for the custodian; an overdue task escalates to the township general manager. The custodian checks controls weekly during the pilot and follows an engineer-set maintenance schedule thereafter. The annual re-test does not replace these checks. The pass is not a statutory certificate or a claim that the whole park is safe.

**Route-test limits:** two adults, custodian permission, daylight and dry conditions, with an engineer-agreed stopping boundary on stable ground. Film only the testers, at about 1 m camera height (our child-eye estimate), and record route length, not traversal time. Observe routes around fence ends, headwalls and maintenance steps from safe ground; never climb, squeeze through, descend into a drain or enter water. Use the gauge for gaps, never a child or a tester's body. Stop if weather, footing or access changes; no result is worth approaching an exposed edge. A visible open route may be documented from the stopping boundary.

**Rule limits:** AS 1926.1 child-barrier geometry is an Australian benchmark, not Malaysian law; apply it only where young children can reach and the engineer adopts it. The ball-path prompt asks whether play can lead to retrieval from a drain. Retain the proposed >0.6 m trigger and a site-specific distance, fixed before inspection, for this screening rule; neither is a universal safe/unsafe boundary. A cover or ball-stop fence is an option for engineering review, not an automatic instruction. For every repair, test continuity and climbability as well as dimensions. CIRIA warns that horizontal rails can become a climbing frame ([CIRIA RP992/17][ciria]).

**Handover comes later.** The pilot must stay under SDP's confirmed maintenance control through renewal. A future receiving authority must explicitly accept the records, open findings, budget responsibility and next review; sending a pack is not acceptance. A change of custodian ends reliance on the old pass until those duties are assigned. Status plates, [ISF funding requests][isf] and council workflow remain outside this pilot.

## Impact and how we measure it

- **Primary physical outcome:** at baseline, list every approach route on the selected asset: total routes N and independently confirmed open routes O₀. Repeat the same inventory at repair acceptance and day 30; report open routes O₃₀, newly found routes and reopened routes separately. Show sustained closure as (O₀ − baseline routes still open at day 30) / O₀, with raw counts. If O₀ is zero, report no closure rate. This is a hazard proxy, not an estimate of deaths prevented.
- **Proposed pilot threshold:** every confirmed open route receives interim protection immediately; all agreed permanent repairs pass independent re-test within eight weeks and stay effective at day 30 after repair. Any bypass, reopened route or unresolved safety-critical unknown blocks acceptance of the affected scope. Report uninspected areas and changed conditions; one asset cannot establish township-wide coverage.
- **Added detection:** on the same evidence, count findings from the usual inspection, the MBSP-item comparison and Safe Edge. The engineer adjudicates additional actionable routes, false flags and unknowns. One defensible additional route supports differentiation; zero means no demonstrated detection advantage.
- **Operating outcomes:** days from finding to protection and verified closure, reopened fixes, staff minutes and total cost per sustained closure. Valid-pass coverage, edge metres, named owners and renewal dates are process counts, not impact. With no closure, report total cost without dividing by zero.
- **Evidence limit:** no withholding repairs to create a control group. This small before/after pilot cannot prove lives saved, reduced drowning incidence or a causal change in resident confidence.

### Cost and buying decision

**Proposed first-year ceiling: RM10,000 for one asset.** This is a team-set spending limit for a small pilot, not a market estimate, quote or SDP commitment. Replace allowances with quantities, supplier prices and approved staff rates before seeking approval.

| Cost allowance                                     | Proposed limit |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------: |
| Survey, engineer review and independent acceptance |        RM1,500 |
| Interim protection and installed permanent repair  |        RM5,000 |
| Routine checks, day-30 check and first renewal     |        RM1,000 |
| Record/export setup and support                    |          RM500 |
| Contingency                                        |        RM2,000 |

Time each role, including both testers: staff cost = person-minutes / 60 × the approved hourly rate. Obtain a contractor quote covering mobilisation, quantities, foundations/fixings, gates or covers, installation, reinstatement and taxes. Compare with the least-cost engineer-acceptable repair; do not choose a cheaper barrier that leaves a bypass. DBKL's RM113/m schedule rate for a 1.2 m galvanised security fence remains a comparator, not a current quote or proof of child-barrier suitability ([prevention]).

SDP's nominated maintenance budget pays interim controls, safety upgrades and renewal. Recover contractual defects only where the engineer/contracts team confirms coverage; discovery during the defects period does not make a design upgrade the contractor's bill. The earlier UTM comparison describes typical 12-month periods under PWD 203A and PAM 2018, subject to the actual contract ([UTM study][dlp], secondary). Inspect before the confirmed deadline, not an assumed one.

**Buying test:** the budget approver accepts the itemised first-year total within RM10,000 and identifies the recurring annual cost and funding line. If the safe solution exceeds the ceiling, maintain interim protection and seek a revised scope/budget; do not issue a pass to fit the price. Report actual additional cost against the current inspection, and measured repeat visits/rework avoided, without pricing lives or hypothetical lawsuits. Sell a repeatable assessment and re-test service through existing tools first; a separate software subscription needs evidence of demand.

## Prototype by 23 October

Keep original files and CSV/JSON export; link the existing work-order ID. The installer cannot accept their own repair. Test missing depth, rejected work, a damage report and an overdue review.

## Why it's new

| Closest analogue                                                           | What it does                                                                                               | What it lacks                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Queensland's pool safety certificate ([QBCC][qbcc-guide])                  | Register, licensed inspection, dated certificate and sale/lease requirements                               | Private pools only                                                                   |
| Singapore's ABC Waters certificate of inspection ([PUB][pub-abc])          | A yearly professional sign-off that a development's water features are maintained and working              | Maintenance/function declaration; no child-route test stated on the cited page       |
| Road safety audit, required by JKR for new roads ([IPM][rsa], _secondary_) | Independent auditors check a road from planning to opening, including provisions for vulnerable road users | Roads only; no pass that expires                                                     |
| MBSP's road and drain handover checklist ([MBSP][mbsp])                    | A site visit, drain-edge barriers and pond signs before the CCC                                            | One council; no child-barrier dimensions in the cited checklist                      |
| Procore, Novade, PlanRadar ([checks])                                      | Configurable inspections and defects; SDP uses Procore                                                     | No dedicated water-edge protocol found in our search; configurable forms may suffice |

- **Our read:** each part exists somewhere: barrier certificates for private pools, yearly certificates for water features in Singapore, independent audits for roads, and penetration tests for security. In searches on 17 October 2026 we found none that tests a public drain, pond or lake edge the way a child meets it and ties the result to a pass that expires. A quick search is not proof that none exists.
- **What makes it more than a checklist:** the route test. The route test records a physical bypass. A good checklist can also require this; the comparison must show what the extra test adds. The claim holds only if the [field comparison](#evidence-due-by-23-october-2026) finds an additional, actionable route. A good engineer may already find it; then this is a repeatable inspection method, not a demonstrated new detection mechanism.

## Risks and open questions

- **Owner, money and liability:** a dated record cannot compensate for an unfunded fix. Entry requires a custodian, deputy, repair funds and renewal budget. Internal access limits the disclosure of precise child-access routes; it does not remove duties or legal exposure. SDP's legal/risk team must review the record wording and release of site footage before operational use. No public status plate or hazard map.
- **Rules and repair scope:** under-five barrier geometry is an adopted benchmark; it is not a drain standard or a complete test for older children. Ball retrieval and older-child access need engineering judgement. Preserve hydraulic function and maintenance access; a compliant drawing does not settle every access question, and a new precaution is not automatically a contractor defect.
- **“Does it beat a good engineer?”** Unknown. Lock the comparison and keep all five results. If the usual inspection finds the same issue, demonstrate consistent closure and renewal only. No novelty claim based solely on a new form or expiry date.
- **“Fences everywhere are costly and ugly.”** CIRIA calls fencing effective but comparatively expensive and recommends natural surveillance ([prevention]). Compare covers, barriers, layout changes and planted margins case by case. Benches and lighting have guidance rather than strong direct outcome evidence. A shallow planted margin needs verified depth, slope, continuity and maintenance; it cannot pass merely because no route is visible. Lake rescue reach, throwable floats and designed fishing decks remain later options under a water-safety assessor, not proof of the drain pilot.
- **Measurement and field safety:** tape/gauge decide barrier dimensions; optional phone scans lose precision on thin rails. Stop at the agreed safe boundary in pairs, with permission and suitable weather; never enter a drain or water. Water depth needs a separate safe professional measurement where relevant, not a phone scan or student leaning over an edge. An unmeasured safety-critical condition stays unresolved.

## Precedents and measurement limits

**A lift's certificate, the analogy judges know.** In Malaysia a lift needs a certificate of fitness, issued only after the lift passes inspection and testing. It is valid for 15 months, and a periodic inspection must happen before it expires ([P.U. (A) 99, regulation 5][lift-cf]). Safe Edge borrows scheduled inspection only; it has none of that statutory status.

**Queensland's pool certificate, the drowning precedent.** In Queensland, owners of pools that can hold more than 300 mm of water must meet the pool safety standard. Every regulated pool must be registered, and a new pool's fence must be certified before filling to 300 mm or more. A licensed inspector issues the pool safety certificate, Form 23, which lasts 1 year for shared pools and 2 years for others, and it is used for property sales and leases; a sale without one requires Form 36 and the buyer must obtain a certificate within 90 days ([QBCC guide][qbcc-guide]). Under AS 1926.1 the barrier must be at least 1.2 m high, with gaps of no more than 100 mm, less than 100 mm at the ground and a 900 mm non-climbable zone ([council summary][coolamon], _secondary_). Climbable objects must be at least 900 mm from the outside of the fence and 300 mm from the inside ([QBCC][qbcc-ncz]). In Malaysia, MSMA's depth-based cover trigger is greater than 0.6 m, but other public-safety provisions still apply; Form G17 has no separate pedestrian-access item ([checks]; [MSMA section 14.2.4.6][msma14]). The best evidence favours barriers: in the Cochrane review, isolation fencing around pools had an odds ratio of 0.17 against three-sided fencing ([prevention]; [Cochrane][cochrane]). This pool result is not a drain or lake effect estimate. These are supporting precedents, not the opening pitch. The earlier pool-versus-uncovered-drain contrast is replaced because a depth threshold alone does not establish that an edge is adequately protected.

**Optional capture, retained for later:** 3d Scanner App is free with paid upgrades and lists PLY/LAS exports ([App Store][scanner-app]). A published iPhone LiDAR test found about ±1 cm accuracy for objects larger than 10 cm and about ±10 cm on a cliff up to 130 m long, with a range up to 5 m; precision fell below 10 cm across ([Luetzenburg et al.][luetzenburg]). These are study results, not our device's tolerance. If a scan is used, compare five dimensions with a tape and disclose every difference. Use it for layout, route length, drops and above-water slopes only; doubtful values revert to manual measurement. The app's ruler can supply optional scan values without a custom viewer.

Water absorbs near-infrared laser light ([USGS slides][nir]); distinguish water depth from drain depth, and record a safely obtained professional pole measurement or another approved method separately. No scan certifies underwater geometry. CIRIA also calls for maintenance responsibility to be established for fences ([CIRIA RP992/17][ciria]); the proposed custodian and budget gate put that earlier vet fix into practice.

## Other directions considered

The 17 October brainstorm weighed these directions before the team adopted the current solution. The second vet made gaps a gauge check, route length the measure and status plates subject to legal review. This revision deliberately moves LiDAR, AR, council handover and lake rescue work out of the core; the route test, independent re-test and owned renewal remain.

## How the idea changed across rounds

- **Round 1:** pitched as drowning alerts for children at drains and ponds; strongest impact, but close to flood alerts.
- **Round 2:** reframed as a pre-handover audit; experts showed guards can't rescue anyone from a fast drain and that a public "deadly edge" map is a legal trap.
- **Round 3:** became Safe Edge with a 3D lake view; the stricter judge found the underwater geometry unmeasured.
- **Round 4:** narrowed to an explainable rule check with verified repair, a measured cutaway and the ISF accountability layer from the practitioner; the fatal attack was cleared.
- **Round 5:** held at 66; the remaining gap is field evidence.
- **Solution vet:** the unit of work became one access route and its control; the pilot shrank to one drain asset; ISF funding moved after the first pilot.
- **Brainstorm, 17 October:** the team adopted the pool-safety certificate as the model. A phone scan measures each edge, testers try a child's routes to the water, and an independent reviewer issues a 12-month pass that a damage report suspends.
- **Specialist vet, 17 October (second):** the tape and gauge now decide pass or fail, the route test runs under set safety rules, the status plate and rescue reach left the first build, and the first renewal is set inside the contractor's defects liability period.
- **Revision, 17 October (Codex):** narrowed to one SDP-retained drain with priced repairs and owned renewal; removed mandatory scans, added a baseline/comparator and dated evidence gates, and kept score increases conditional on field proof.
