# Safe Edge

Safe Edge helps SDP's park manager choose a repair that closes the observed routes to water, then prove it on site. Its **Child-eye Fix Lens** puts a proposed barrier over real phone photos: tap “close this gap” and the app reveals the recorded way around the fence end, before that incomplete repair is bought.

**First ask:** one retained drain asset, one named SDP maintenance owner and a repair budget. The proposed site is Elmina Central Park; access, custody and funding are not yet confirmed. **Track:** People, safety and security. **Rank:** see the [shortlist](/docs/research/shortlist.md).

**Current product:** on 17 October the team added drone patrols, an AI layer, an SDK and a Three.js simulation around Fix Lens. [`PRODUCT.md`](/PRODUCT.md) is now the product truth; this page keeps the problem evidence, the Fix Lens design and the field evidence plan.

## Problem

- **A ball can draw a child into a drain.** Of 21 news-reported clusters in Selangor and Kuala Lumpur township waters since 2023, 8 began in a drain. In 2 of those 8, Kajang in December 2025 and Bandar Mahkota Cheras in May 2026, children entered after a ball ([incident table][incidents]). Rain was reported in 4 of the 21 clusters, all drains; most reports state no weather. These selected reports are not an incidence rate or proof of a cause.
- **The harm extends beyond drains.** In August 2026 two boys aged 11 and 12 drowned at a township dam in Pasir Gudang, and residents asked for fencing and CCTV ([Bernama][bernama-pg]; [MalaysiaGazette][mgazette]). The Fire and Rescue Department counted 371 child and teen drowning deaths from January 2023 to August 2026 ([The Star][star-371]). Of the 21 clusters, 11 involved lakes, ponds or standing water; none of those reports mentions rain and several victims were found hours later ([SDP lakes][sdp-lakes]).
- **The SDP lake case supports inspection, not the child-route claim.** On 15 October 2026 a 30-year-old man went missing while fishing at Tasik Bukit Raja in Bandar Bukit Raja; Bomba recovered his body the next day ([Utusan][utusan]). The emergency call came at 10:37 pm; his motorcycle, fishing gear and slippers were by the lake ([SDP lakes][sdp-lakes]). We do not infer why he drowned. Night fishing and lake rescue remain later work.
- **Written checks need a field test of access.** MSMA asks for covers on drains deeper than 0.6 m with pedestrian access, a 1.2 m rail or grating for velocities above 2 and below 4 m/s, and pond banks of 1V:4H or gentler ([water-edge checks][checks]). Its public-safety provisions also call for protective measures in populated or pedestrian-access areas; a shallow drain is not exempt from safety review ([MSMA section 14.2.4.6][msma14]). Form G17 ties road and drain work to approved plans without a separate pedestrian-access item. MBSP's July 2026 checklist includes barriers beside drains 1.2 m wide or deeper than 0.9 m and pond signs; it also checks approved-plan conditions and allows other site findings ([MBSP][mbsp]). Whether our route test adds a finding is still unproven.
- **Funding and responsibility must meet at the same asset.** Developers prepay councils an Improvement Service Fund (ISF) under the Street, Drainage and Building Act 1974; MBDK charges RM5,000 per acre for drainage into an ISF trust fund ([MBDK][mbdk]). This does not establish a budget available to this pilot. A former SDP Head of Township said: “The bigger issue is not who owns the drain or pond after handover, but whether there is clear accountability for maintenance and public safety” ([consultation][consult]). Start where SDP retains that responsibility.

## Target user

The proposed daily owner is **SDP's Elmina park maintenance manager**, supported by the responsible project/drainage engineer. The township general manager approves the budget; a reviewer who did not install the repair accepts it. These are roles to confirm by name, not commitments already obtained. Councils and residents' associations are later recipients, not pilot operators.

## Solution and how it works

**Chosen concept: Child-eye Fix Lens.** Blend Repair Replay's repair test with overlays on real child-eye photos. The manager works in one case: capture, compare, send and verify. The unit remains one route from a path, gate or play area to an exposed drain edge and its cover, fence or gate. Start with a retained drain; ponds and lakes remain in the problem evidence and later scope.

1.  **Capture and confirm.** Two authorised adults photograph the approach, both fence ends and any gate from safe ground. Tap visible openings on those photos, link each observed approach to its opening, and attach tape and 100 mm gauge readings where safely reachable. An unseen end stays **unknown**. The engineer confirms the links and measurements. Asset, custodian and photo IDs carry forward automatically; there is no separate 3D model to author.
1.  **Try the repair on the photo.** Tap an engineer-reviewed cover, barrier extension or gate remedy. Its outline appears on the source photo. The app highlights the recorded routes that the selected remedy would leave open and opens the supporting photo for each bypass. A persistent **proposed repair — not installed** label separates the overlay from evidence. The engineer checks continuity, climbability, hydraulic capacity, debris blockage, loading and maintenance access. With no known connection remaining, the proposal says **ready for engineering review**; any unknown still needs inspection.
1.  **Send one repair case.** The chosen remedy fills a work-order draft with its marked photos, measured quantities, acceptance test and owner. Prices come only from dated, itemised quotes; missing prices say **quote needed**. The engineer approves the design and the budget holder funds it. Report a missing cover, broken barrier or confirmed open route to the duty manager immediately, before completing the Lens; the custodian provides engineer-approved interim protection or closes the affected approach. Keep contractual defects separate from SDP-funded safety upgrades.
1.  **Prove the installed fix.** A reviewer who did not do the fix opens the same case, retakes the saved views from safe ground and repeats the physical route and gauge checks, including both ends and the gate. A before/after swipe helps compare evidence; image appearance cannot pass the repair. Record rejection or acceptance against the agreed tests, retain the originals, and schedule the day-30 check and the named custodian's renewal.

**The wow moment:** in the proposed 60-second demo, the judge sees an actual site photo and taps “close this gap”. A barrier outline covers it, yet **route still open** remains: the app switches to the linked photo of the observed end bypass. Select the complete, engineer-reviewed remedy and that recorded connection disappears from the proposal. The real finding still says **awaiting installation and independent re-test**. One visible lesson: the first repair looked finished, but did not address the whole observed approach. If no such bypass is found, report that result and show the mechanism on a separately labelled dry mock-up.

**Why this blend:** the panel asked whether preparing a replay costs more work than it saves. Fix Lens drops scan import and the perspective scene from the six-day build and replaces separate route-graph authoring with photo annotations. Those annotations are the route record; the app reuses them for proposal coverage, the work order and re-test. It still needs observed links and engineering review, whose time must be counted. The [four-concept comparison](#other-directions-considered) weighs automatic measurement, mapping and AI drafting against this choice. The claim to test is a better repair decision or less total work, not that a new interface detects hazards unaided.

**Evidence and rule limits:** retain the asset boundary, all approaches, custodian, applicable authority, drawing revision and engineer. Tape, gauge, photos and the route sketch are decisive, with method and uncertainty. Check MSMA and applicable approval conditions separately from optional child-barrier benchmarks and the team's ball-path prompt. Results are finding, no finding, not applicable or needs information, each with its source. Unknown depth blocks only the affected checks and gets an owner and due date; it never becomes a pass by default. The engineer sets urgency; Safe Edge never computes a drowning-risk score or predicts a child's behaviour.

**After acceptance:** assessment, repair and custody keep separate statuses. The internal “pass” covers only listed routes and tests at that date, with review due within 12 months or earlier if the engineer requires it. Damage, changed access or overdue review suspends/expires it and opens a task; overdue tasks escalate to the township general manager. The custodian checks controls weekly during the pilot and follows an engineer-set maintenance schedule thereafter. Annual re-test does not replace those checks. This is not a statutory certificate or a claim that the whole park is safe.

**Route-test limits:** two adults, custodian permission, daylight and dry conditions, with an engineer-agreed stopping boundary on stable ground. Film only the testers, at about 1 m camera height (our child-eye estimate), and record route length, not traversal time. Observe routes around fence ends, headwalls and maintenance steps from safe ground; never climb, squeeze through, descend into a drain or enter water. Use the gauge for gaps, never a child or a tester's body. Stop if weather, footing or access changes; no result is worth approaching an exposed edge. A visible open route may be documented from the stopping boundary.

**Rule limits:** AS 1926.1 child-barrier geometry is an Australian benchmark, not Malaysian law; apply it only where young children can reach and the engineer adopts it. The ball-path prompt asks whether play can lead to retrieval from a drain. Retain the proposed >0.6 m trigger and a site-specific distance, fixed before inspection, for this screening rule; neither is a universal safe/unsafe boundary. A cover or ball-stop fence is an option for engineering review, not an automatic instruction. For every repair, test continuity and climbability as well as dimensions. CIRIA warns that horizontal rails can become a climbing frame ([CIRIA RP992/17][ciria]).

**Handover comes later.** The pilot must stay under SDP's confirmed maintenance control through renewal. A future receiving authority must explicitly accept the records, open findings, budget responsibility and next review; sending a pack is not acceptance. A change of custodian ends reliance on the old pass until those duties are assigned. Status plates, [ISF funding requests][isf] and council workflow remain outside this pilot.

## Impact and how we measure it

**Outcome sought:** fewer confirmed approaches left open to dangerous water, while the intended park and play access stays usable. SDP buys a physically effective repair and a record it can maintain; a future council recipient gets an asset, accepted tests, residual findings and a named upkeep owner. MBSP already includes drain barriers and pond signs in its checklist ([MBSP][mbsp]); it has not adopted this product. The causal chain to test is **clearer repair choice → funded installation → independent closure → closure maintained at day 30**. A convincing animation establishes none of the last three.

- **Primary physical outcome:** at baseline, list every approach on the selected asset: total routes N and independently confirmed open routes O₀. Repeat at repair acceptance and day 30; report O₃₀, new routes and reopened routes separately. Sustained closure is (O₀ − baseline routes still open at day 30) / O₀, with raw counts; if O₀ is zero, report no closure rate. Every confirmed open route needs immediate interim protection; all agreed permanent repairs must pass independent re-test within eight weeks and remain effective at day 30 after repair. Any bypass or unresolved safety-critical unknown blocks acceptance of that scope. This remains a hazard proxy, not deaths prevented.
- **Duration confirmed access remains unprotected:** record finding, interim-control, installation and independent-acceptance times. Report days to protection and verified closure, plus confirmed route-days without effective interim protection. Mark intervals between observations as uncertain. Record any path/play-area closure hours and whether the intended access is restored after acceptance. These measure the duration of an unresolved condition, not how long a particular child was exposed. Faster administrative closure alone is not success; no permanent closure of the whole amenity is counted as a successful repair.
- **Better repair decisions:** compare the ordinary inspection and repair recommendation with the replay using the same evidence. An engineer adjudicates additional actionable routes, false flags, unknowns and proposed remedies that leave a bypass. One independently accepted additional route supports added detection; zero means no detection advantage. Rejecting an inadequate proposed remedy is a separate decision benefit, not a newly discovered hazard. No withheld repairs or staged field defects.
- **Effort and cost:** measure total person-minutes from capture through review, including photo annotation, route linking and corrections, against the current photo/form method. Track first independent acceptance versus repairs returned for rework, with reasons, and actual total cost per sustained closure; with no closure, report cost without division. A proposed efficiency gate is lower total time with no lost actionable findings or false closure. Valid-pass coverage, edge metres, owners and renewal dates are process counts, not impact.
- **Reach and limits:** record which path/play area the asset serves and anonymous visit-events in two proposed 30-minute observation periods, with date, weather and exclusions. These are observed uses, not unique residents or children protected; do not multiply them across SDP's footprint. Report uninspected areas. This small before/after pilot cannot prove lives saved, reduced drowning incidence, savings at scale or a causal change in resident confidence.

## Fit with Sime Darby Property

- **One track and a stated priority:** People, safety and security, under Community Experience; Climate Resilience remains a related pillar in SDP's Sustainability Strategy and Roadmap 2030 ([SDP][sdp]). SDP's 2025 Sustainability Report includes community parks within its Resilient Infrastructure impact area, supporting physical wellbeing and social interaction (printed pp. 94–95; [SDP sustainability report][sdp-sr25]). Safe Edge's proposed contribution is maintaining usable park access while closing confirmed routes to water.
- **An asset with a child-focused commitment:** SDP's City of Elmina Inclusive Playground was developed with UNICEF and approved/supported by MBSA; SDP's release describes safe, inclusive, child-friendly public space and maintenance requirements ([SDP playground release][sdp-playground]). That makes nearby park-access maintenance a concrete fit to investigate. It does not prove a nearby hazardous drain, current custody or participation by UNICEF or MBSA in Safe Edge. Fix Lens compares a remedy against that commitment: a fence that blocks the intended accessible route fails the proposed acceptance test.
- **A reported business concern:** SDP's 2025 report names unresolved community concerns as a reputational risk and lists overall complaint volume among its value indicators (printed pp. 51 and 64; [SDP annual report][sdp-iar25]). Propose a water-edge subset of that existing complaint indicator, linked by asset/work-order ID: repeat complaints, verified closure time and repairs returned for rework. Ask the current manager to accept the definitions and supply the baseline; these are proposed local measures, not existing published SDP KPIs. The report does not admit this specific safety gap; a new audit may initially increase recorded complaints.
- **An existing digital destination:** SDP uses Procore for quality, inspections and defects ([Procore case study][procore]). Its own 2025 report describes Procore as a single platform for construction quality, inspections and project communication, with traceability for CPC inspections and defect rectification (printed p. 149; [SDP annual report][sdp-iar25]). Attach Fix Lens's annotated photos, rejected remedy and re-test to an existing work order or Procore item by ID. This tests less repeat inspection/rework without replacing the existing system; API access, park-team adoption, savings and reduced legal exposure are not established.
- **Park-management precedent and scale:** SDP reports assessing 719 trees across seven zones at Taman Subang Ria for health, structural stability and potential safety risks, to inform mitigation and long-term management (printed p. 101; [SDP sustainability report][sdp-sr25]). Our inference is that condition evidence tied to action fits an existing park-management practice; it is not proof of demand for Safe Edge. Residents would get tested access controls with an accountable maintenance contact. If the pilot works, repeat on retained assets, then consider handover agreements. The earlier opportunity spans SDP's 27 active townships ([SDP][sdp]); that is a footprint, not a count of eligible assets or customers. The practitioner's expected benefit is “community trust and township value” ([consultation][consult]), not measured uplift.

### Cost and buying decision

**Proposed first-year ceiling: RM10,000 for one asset.** This is a team-set spending limit for a small pilot, not a market estimate, quote or SDP commitment. Replace allowances with quantities, supplier prices and approved staff rates before seeking approval.

| Cost allowance                                     | Proposed limit |
| -------------------------------------------------- | -------------: |
| Survey, engineer review and independent acceptance |        RM1,500 |
| Interim protection and installed permanent repair  |        RM5,000 |
| Routine checks, day-30 check and first renewal     |        RM1,000 |
| Record/export setup and support                    |          RM500 |
| Contingency                                        |        RM2,000 |

Time each role, including both testers: staff cost = person-minutes / 60 × the approved hourly rate. Obtain a contractor quote covering mobilisation, quantities, foundations/fixings, gates or covers, installation, reinstatement and taxes. Compare with the least-cost engineer-acceptable repair; do not choose a cheaper barrier that leaves a bypass. DBKL's RM113/m schedule rate for a 1.2 m galvanised security fence remains a comparator, not a current quote or proof of child-barrier suitability ([prevention]).

SDP's nominated maintenance budget pays interim controls, safety upgrades and renewal. Recover contractual defects only where the engineer/contracts team confirms coverage; discovery during the defects period does not make a design upgrade the contractor's bill. The earlier UTM comparison describes typical 12-month periods under PWD 203A and PAM 2018, subject to the actual contract ([UTM study][dlp], secondary). Inspect before the confirmed deadline, not an assumed one.

**Buying test:** the budget approver accepts the itemised first-year total within RM10,000 and identifies the recurring annual cost and funding line. If the safe solution exceeds the ceiling, maintain interim protection and seek a revised scope/budget; do not issue a pass to fit the price. Report actual additional cost against the current inspection, and measured repeat visits/rework avoided, without pricing lives or hypothetical lawsuits. Sell a repeatable assessment and re-test service through existing tools first; a separate software subscription needs evidence of demand.

## Prototype by 23 October

**Build one photo-based decision, from capture to export.** One coder builds a phone-friendly web app with an image viewer, tappable route annotations, proposed-repair overlays and a separate physical acceptance record. Browser SVG supports two-dimensional graphics and scripting ([Mozilla SVG documentation][svg]); the student work is the evidence links, remedy logic and acceptance controls. Each observed route lists the openings it uses; each proposed remedy lists the openings the engineer expects it to block. A route remains visible until at least one of its confirmed connections is blocked in the proposal. Unknown connections remain unresolved. No route is discovered by pixels or by an invisible operator during the demo.

**Required kit and scope:** an ordinary phone camera, tape and 100 mm gauge. Capture evidence once; reuse it for the comparison, quantities, work order and re-test. A sketch or saved OpenStreetMap extract locates the asset, with omissions visible. Render the actual roughly 1 m camera-height photos; do not manufacture a child-eye view from adult-height footage. There is no required LiDAR device, live AR tracking, custom vision training or 3D scene. The small rule set retains the MSMA cover trigger, adopted child-barrier checks, ball-path screening, continuity and maintenance access, with engineer-confirmed applicability. No public map, risk score, live Procore API, status plate, council acceptance, ISF flow, rescue module or flood simulation.

## Why it's new

| Closest analogue                                                           | What it does                                                                                               | What it lacks                                                                        |
| -------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Queensland's pool safety certificate ([QBCC][qbcc-guide])                  | Register, licensed inspection, dated certificate and sale/lease requirements                               | Private pools only                                                                   |
| Singapore's ABC Waters certificate of inspection ([PUB][pub-abc])          | A yearly professional sign-off that a development's water features are maintained and working              | Maintenance/function declaration; no child-route test stated on the cited page       |
| Road safety audit, required by JKR for new roads ([IPM][rsa], _secondary_) | Independent auditors check a road from planning to opening, including provisions for vulnerable road users | Roads only; no pass that expires                                                     |
| MBSP's road and drain handover checklist ([MBSP][mbsp])                    | A site visit, drain-edge barriers and pond signs before the CCC                                            | One council; no child-barrier dimensions in the cited checklist                      |
| Procore, Novade, PlanRadar ([checks])                                      | Configurable inspections and defects; SDP uses Procore                                                     | No dedicated water-edge protocol found in our search; configurable forms may suffice |

- **The mechanism to test:** a repair is linked to the observed openings it would block, so selecting it exposes any recorded approach still unaddressed. That same relationship becomes the installer's scope and the independent reviewer's test. The visible result is an incomplete repair revealed on the actual site photos, before purchase. A dimension check alone does not establish continuity; the app must still earn its place against a competent engineer using the same evidence.
- **Fewer repeated tasks:** the annotated photos are both the visual comparison and the route record. Fix Lens removes the required 3D scene and separate graph-authoring task from round 1, then reuses confirmed evidence for quantities, a template work order and repeat checks. No scene needs rebuilding after a repair. All annotation and engineering time stays in the comparison; reduced effort is a hypothesis.
- **What remains unproven:** each part exists somewhere: barrier certificates for private pools, yearly certificates for water features in Singapore, independent audits for roads, and penetration tests for security. The earlier searches on 17 October 2026 found none that tests a public drain, pond or lake edge the way a child meets it and ties the result to a pass that expires; that quick search was not proof of absence. Fix Lens is a proposed combination, not a world-first claim. A good engineer or configurable inspection tool may already reach the same decision. The [field and product comparison](#evidence-due-by-23-october-2026) must demonstrate added detection, a better repair choice or reduced effort; if none appears, the innovation claim fails.

## Risks and open questions

- **Owner, money and liability:** a dated record cannot compensate for an unfunded fix. Entry requires a custodian, deputy, repair funds and renewal budget. Internal access limits the disclosure of precise child-access routes; it does not remove duties or legal exposure. SDP's legal/risk team must review the record wording and release of site footage before operational use. No public status plate or hazard map.
- **Rules and repair scope:** under-five barrier geometry is an adopted benchmark; it is not a drain standard or a complete test for older children. Ball retrieval and older-child access need engineering judgement. Preserve hydraulic function, maintenance access and the park's intended accessible paths; a compliant drawing does not settle every access question, and a new precaution is not automatically a contractor defect. A quote draft never authorises construction or supplies missing engineering design.
- **“Does it beat a good engineer?”** Unknown. Lock the comparison and keep all five candidate-edge results, including annotation and engineering-review time. If the usual inspection finds the same issue, claim no added detection; test whether replay improves the repair choice or total effort. If neither improves, stop the product claim. No novelty claim based solely on a new form, 3D view or expiry date. The current owner, quote and funded renewal remain necessary even if judges like the demo.
- **“Fences everywhere are costly and ugly.”** CIRIA calls fencing effective but comparatively expensive and recommends natural surveillance ([prevention]). Compare covers, barriers, layout changes and planted margins case by case. Benches and lighting have guidance rather than strong direct outcome evidence. A shallow planted margin needs verified depth, slope, continuity and maintenance; it cannot pass merely because no route is visible. Lake rescue reach, throwable floats and designed fishing decks remain later options under a water-safety assessor, not proof of the drain pilot.
- **Photo illusion, measurement and field safety:** a route absent from the record is not a closed route. Overlays are schematic, not dimensions or construction drawings; perspective can make a proposed barrier look complete. Keep originals visible, label every proposal and treat occluded ends, gates and unobserved ground as unknown. The engineer checks route completeness; a reviewer who did not do the fix tests the installed remedy. Tape/gauge decide barrier dimensions; optional phone scans lose precision on thin rails. The roughly 1 m view explains the scene, not child reach or body size. Stop short of the edge at the agreed stable-ground boundary, in pairs with permission and suitable weather; never enter a drain or water. Water depth needs separate safe professional measurement. If photo overlays fail, use the 2D evidence view; unknowns never become a pass.

## Precedents and measurement limits

**A lift's certificate, the analogy judges know.** In Malaysia a lift needs a certificate of fitness, issued only after the lift passes inspection and testing. It is valid for 15 months, and a periodic inspection must happen before it expires ([P.U. (A) 99, regulation 5][lift-cf]). Safe Edge borrows scheduled inspection only; it has none of that statutory status.

**Queensland's pool certificate, the drowning precedent.** In Queensland, owners of pools that can hold more than 300 mm of water must meet the pool safety standard. Every regulated pool must be registered, and a new pool's fence must be certified before filling to 300 mm or more. A licensed inspector issues the pool safety certificate, Form 23, which lasts 1 year for shared pools and 2 years for others, and it is used for property sales and leases; a sale without one requires Form 36 and the buyer must obtain a certificate within 90 days ([QBCC guide][qbcc-guide]). Under AS 1926.1 the barrier must be at least 1.2 m high, with gaps of no more than 100 mm, less than 100 mm at the ground and a 900 mm non-climbable zone ([council summary][coolamon], _secondary_). Climbable objects must be at least 900 mm from the outside of the fence and 300 mm from the inside ([QBCC][qbcc-ncz]). In Malaysia, MSMA's depth-based cover trigger is greater than 0.6 m, but other public-safety provisions still apply; Form G17 has no separate pedestrian-access item ([checks]; [MSMA section 14.2.4.6][msma14]). The best evidence favours barriers: in the Cochrane review, isolation fencing around pools had an odds ratio of 0.17 against three-sided fencing ([prevention]; [Cochrane][cochrane]). This pool result is not a drain or lake effect estimate. These are supporting precedents, not the opening pitch. The earlier pool-versus-uncovered-drain contrast is replaced because a depth threshold alone does not establish that an edge is adequately protected.

**Optional capture, retained for later:** 3d Scanner App is free with paid upgrades and lists PLY/LAS exports ([App Store][scanner-app]). A published iPhone LiDAR test found about ±1 cm accuracy for objects larger than 10 cm and about ±10 cm on a cliff up to 130 m long, with a range up to 5 m; precision fell below 10 cm across ([Luetzenburg et al.][luetzenburg]). These are study results, not our device's tolerance. If a scan is used, compare five dimensions with a tape and disclose every difference. Use it for layout, route length, drops and above-water slopes only; doubtful values revert to manual measurement. The app's ruler can supply optional scan values without a custom viewer. The earlier 3D viewer remains technically possible: Three.js supplies a perspective camera and PLY import ([camera documentation][three-camera]; [PLY loader][three-ply]). It is outside the six-day core because Fix Lens can show the source photos directly.

Water absorbs near-infrared laser light ([USGS slides][nir]); distinguish water depth from drain depth, and record a safely obtained professional pole measurement or another approved method separately. No scan certifies underwater geometry. CIRIA also calls for maintenance responsibility to be established for fences ([CIRIA RP992/17][ciria]); the proposed custodian and budget gate put that earlier vet fix into practice.

## How the idea changed across rounds

- **Round 1:** pitched as drowning alerts for children at drains and ponds; strongest impact, but close to flood alerts.
- **Round 2:** reframed as a pre-handover audit; experts showed guards can't rescue anyone from a fast drain and that a public "deadly edge" map is a legal trap.
- **Round 3:** became Safe Edge with a 3D lake view; the stricter judge found the underwater geometry unmeasured.

- **Round 5:** held at 66; the remaining gap is field evidence.

- **Brainstorm, 17 October:** the team adopted the pool-safety certificate as the model. A phone scan measures each edge, testers try a child's routes to the water, and an independent reviewer issues a 12-month pass that a damage report suspends.
