# Site breeding register

Floor-aware Aedes inspection and verified corrective action on SDP building sites (working name; first called Rain Check, renamed because a US product called RainCheck already sets rain-triggered inspections on construction sites). Track: People (Health and wellbeing).

## Problem

Building sites next to occupied homes breed Aedes inside the structure, and inspections miss locations or close them without proof.

- Malaysia recorded 65,979 dengue cases by week 35 of 2026, up 66% on 2025; deaths rose from 32 to 62.
- Kuala Lumpur and Putrajaya inspectors found Aedes at 126 of 242 construction sites checked in 2024 (52%). At one KL site, 6 of 8 breeding spots were inside the building. A 2018 survey of 19 KL sites found breeding from ground level to the 14th floor.
- In 2026 DBKL issued a 14-day closure order on a Bukit Damansara site after 4 local dengue cases and 4 breeding spots.
- The Destruction of Disease-Bearing Insects Act 1975 lets officers order fixes and close premises; Aedes can go from egg to adult in about a week.
- A former Head of Township at SDP we consulted says residents near large developments worry most about dust, noise, traffic and health, and that developers should share monitoring results to build trust.

## Winning solution

**Users:** the contractor's site environmental officer (finds and fixes) and SDP's clerk of works (verifies).

**Prior art we know:** RainCheck, a US product, already sets rain-triggered inspection deadlines on construction sites for stormwater compliance. Ours targets Aedes breeding locations, floor by floor, with verified fixes.

**Mechanism, a location register that cannot be faked clear:** every breeding-prone location (slabs, lift pits, basements, formwork, drums, water tanks, as listed in MOH's construction-site guide) sits on its floor with an owner and the age of its last check. Routine checks continue as now; heavy rain at the site adds an extra due check on exposed locations. A finding becomes a task for a named foreman; the fix needs photo evidence and is accepted or rejected by the verifier. A repeat finding reopens the same location with its history. A missed, blocked or unverified location can never show as clear, and overdue work escalates to SDP's project manager.

**Shared with residents:** a monthly verified summary for neighbouring homes: locations checked, findings fixed and verified, work still open. No health-outcome claims.

**3D that earns its place:** an exploded view of the building, floor by floor, that shows stacked inspection points, a floor nobody inspected this week and a blocked access route, which a flat list hides. The same record drives the phone checklist.

**90-second demo:**

1. Hook: DBKL's 14-day closure of a Bukit Damansara site (5 s).
2. Last night's real rainfall adds due checks; the exploded view shows floor 5 uninspected (20 s).
3. A real, permissioned finding from a site walk, assigned to a foreman (20 s).
4. The close-out photo is rejected; the fix is redone and verified (20 s).
5. A blocked lift pit stays unresolved and escalates; the resident summary updates (15 s).

**Evidence plan for the 7 days:** day 1 site access and an interview with a site environmental officer through a contractor's project director we consulted; days 2–4 build the register, phone checklist, verification and exploded view; day 5 test it with the officer against the current checklist; days 6–7 deck and video. If access fails, use documented records and say so.

**Pilot (8–12 weeks, then 1–2 years):** one active SDP phase beside occupied homes, with a comparison site. Measures: audited coverage, positive locations per containers inspected, repeat findings, time to verified correction, staff minutes and running cost.

## Why SDP

- SDP is the employer, so it can write the register and verification into contracts.
- A closure order stops work and can push back vacant possession; the register reduces that exposure (we will not claim a saving before the pilot).
- It protects residents already living in earlier phases and gives SDP something verified to share with them.

## Risks we already know

- Contractors already inspect; we must show the specific locations and close-outs their current process misses.
- Photos can be gamed; verification and spot rechecks cost staff time.
- Nearby dengue cases do not prove a site caused them.
