# Shortlist

The three ideas the team chooses between on 17 October 2026, how expert roasts reshaped them, and what each still needs. The ideas came from [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md) and the team's [idea ranking](/docs/archive/ideas/0000-ranking.md). Simulated consultants and judging panels attacked each pitch, then checked the attacks against our evidence, over five rounds on 16 and 17 October 2026. A brainstorm and specialist vets revised the solutions on 17 October, and a Claude judging panel scored the revised docs. A Codex panel then scored all three after a second revision of Safe Edge and the Site breeding register.

- **The latest panel, 17 October (Codex), keeps the order:** Lepas Hujan **80.7**, Safe Edge **71.0**, Site breeding register **61.7**. All three judges independently rank them in that order. Lepas Hujan remains the strongest choice on the documents; its repair-proof claim still needs technical validation.
- **Lepas Hujan leads on SDP fit and available evidence:** it addresses reported defect and complaint concerns and has real local weather records and dated owner accounts. All three judges challenge whether daily rain totals, same-side wind and a dry photo can prove a repair; validate that acceptance rule with a specialist. The recurrence baseline and operating costs remain missing.
- **Safe Edge’s retained-asset pilot is credible but unconfirmed:** all three give feasibility 4, with custody, repair funds and renewal still to secure. The main test is whether its route inspection adds an actionable finding beyond the current inspection; a signed, priced pilot work order would establish who can act on that finding.
- **The register’s full mechanism still has the hardest delivery chain:** all three give feasibility 2 because the specialist, lawful trap scope, consenting hosts, contractor and comparison site remain unconfirmed. Its area-sweep demo is feasible in principle, but it does not validate the egg warning; the warning must show additional verified sources for its cost and labour.
- **No idea has passed the full bar we set** (every expert “Reinforced” and a panel score of 75). Lepas Hujan’s 80.7 clears the score threshold; the expert verdicts remain from earlier reviews. This panel used Codex gpt-6-astra, the same model family as rounds 3 to 5, after the second revisions of Safe Edge and the register. Treat the new totals as judgments of current documents, not measured gains caused by editing.

## What each idea still needs

Every remaining point the panels withheld depends on field evidence, not more desk research.

- **Safe Edge:** measure one real water edge and find a bypass around existing protection that makes the cheaper fix inadequate; compare against the current inspection checklist with a practitioner; get an itemised repair quote. Drop the universal regulatory-gap claim and the promise of funded post-handover repairs.
- **Site breeding register:** get the district health office's written answer on collecting eggs under Act 154, or a partner that already holds permission, before any trap goes out; visit one active site with its environmental officer; find a location the current checklist misses or a close-out made without a recheck; run the current checklist and the register on the same area and record missed points and staff minutes.
- **Lepas Hujan:** interview owners with dated leak histories, including leak-free homes, and separate late discovery, late reporting and failed repairs; drop the claim that rainfall data becomes a design standard; compare a rain prompt with an ordinary reminder.
- **3D, for all three:** the panels call photorealistic fly-throughs decoration. 3D earns its place only where it shows something a list or section hides: a measured cutaway at a water edge, or an exploded floor view of a building. Lepas Hujan does not need 3D.

## How the roasts worked

- **Consultants:** two domain experts per idea (for example a vector-control officer and a contractor's safety, health and environment manager for the register) and a three-seat judging panel (SDP's head of digital and innovation, a startup investor and an urban-planning professor), all simulated by AI models.
- **Method:** each wrote 6 to 12 attacks from the brief and the pitch alone, then opened our evidence and marked each attack answered, partly answered or unanswered, with a seven-day fix. From round 3, they also judged the solution and the demo.
- **Models:** rounds 1 and 2 used Gemini 4 Argon (high); rounds 3 to 5 used Codex gpt-6-astra (xhigh), a much stricter judge. Scores from different models are not comparable.

## Brainstorm, 17 October

On 17 October a brainstorm set out to make the three solutions more original before the team locks one. [Lepas Hujan](/docs/research/shortlist/lepas-hujan.md#other-directions-considered), [Safe Edge](/docs/research/shortlist/safe-edge.md#other-directions-considered) and, later that day, the [Site breeding register](/docs/research/shortlist/site-breeding-register.md#other-directions-considered) took up its directions, then a specialist vet revised each; their "Other directions considered" sections list the rest.

- **Why the solutions read as incremental.** **Our read:** by round 5 all three had become one machine: a register, a checker who didn't do the fix and a dated record. That made them hard to kill, but the [shortlist page][artifact] called Safe Edge "a checklist app" and the register "a phone checklist", and Procore, which SDP uses, already has inspections, rejection and floor locations. Round 5 scored their innovation 3 (Safe Edge), 2 (the register) and 3 (Lepas Hujan) out of 5. Each recorded whether a control exists and who checked it; none tested whether the control stops what it is for.
- **How it worked.** Start from each pitch's weakest round-5 scores and its vet's top fix. Borrow from two fields that regulate the same shape of problem, because people transfer a solution more reliably after comparing two analogies ([ideation methods][methods]). Test the control against what defeats it. Use tools a student team can get this week, ask what a judge would remember from a five-minute video, keep every roast fix and check each new fact against its source.
- **Assumptions.** Six days remain, and the team has 3 to 5 members, one of whom codes. No SDP data arrives before 23 October, so every demo runs on public data and data the team collects. All three stay in the People track. Every roast fix stays: no computed drowning risk, no public danger map or "dengue-safe" badge, and no claim of lives saved or dengue cases prevented.

- **The register took up its direction later on 17 October:** egg traps on the site fence, compared each week with traps among the homes next door. It borrowed from the US rule that makes refineries sample benzene at their fence line ([EPA][epa-fenceline]) and from Singapore's Gravitraps ([NEA][nea]). Its specialist vet then made the first four-day trap run a first reading, not a breach, so the trigger waits for four weekly readings; found that collecting eggs needs written permission under Act 154; named vector control around ports and airports as the closest practice; cut the QR checkpoints; and turned the rain-started countdowns into one 5-day due date after heavy rain.
- **Our read, updated:** all three docs have now taken up the brainstorm, and the panel scored their innovation 4.0 (Lepas Hujan), 3.7 (Safe Edge) and 4.0 (the register, up from 2.0). The added steps cost each idea points: water tests, a weather station, type alerts and an open legal question for Lepas Hujan; legal exposure, field work and unfunded fixes for Safe Edge; and Act 154 permission, residents who host traps, a vector-control officer and weekly trap rounds for the register, which cut its feasibility from 3.7 to 2.0.

## What the practitioner changed

A former Head of Township at Sime Darby Property, consulted on 16 October 2026 ([notes](/docs/research/consultations/township-practitioner.md)), reshaped the shortlist:

- **Accountability after handover:** "The bigger issue is not who owns the drain or pond after handover, but whether there is clear accountability for maintenance and public safety." This added Safe Edge's post-handover record and led to the Improvement Service Fund research.
- **Funding exists:** councils collect an Improvement Service Fund from developers (MBDK charges RM5,000 per acre for drainage) and, in the practitioner's words, use it "for post-township development maintenance work".
- **Share results:** developers "should welcome independent noise and dust monitoring" and share the results, which added the register's monthly summary for neighbouring homes.
- **Goodwill is not enough:** community programmes need "community participation, institutional support, funding mechanisms, and clear accountability".
