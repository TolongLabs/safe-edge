# Shortlist

The three ideas the team chooses between on 17 October 2026, how expert roasts reshaped them, and what each still needs. The ideas came from [Niche township problems](/docs/research/niche-problems.md) and the team's [idea ranking](/docs/archive/ideas/0000-ranking.md). Simulated consultants and judging panels attacked each pitch, then checked the attacks against our evidence, over five rounds on 16 and 17 October 2026.

- **No idea reached the bar we set** (every expert "Reinforced" and a panel score of 75). The last panel put none in the top 10 "on the supplied evidence today"; all three survive in narrower form.
- **Safe Edge and the register are close.** The same panel prompt gave the register 71 in round 4 and 64 in round 5, so a gap of a few points between them is noise.
- **Lepas Hujan is the cheapest to build**, but the panel says late discovery of leaks is not yet quantified and its Planet framing is weak; pitch it under People.

## What each idea still needs

Every remaining point the panels withheld depends on field evidence, not more desk research.

- **Safe Edge:** measure one real water edge and find a bypass around existing protection that makes the cheaper fix inadequate; compare against the current inspection checklist with a practitioner; get an itemised repair quote. Drop the universal regulatory-gap claim and the promise of funded post-handover repairs.
- **Site breeding register:** visit one active site with its environmental officer; find a location the current checklist misses or a close-out made without a recheck; run the current checklist and the register on the same area and record missed points and staff minutes.
- **Lepas Hujan:** interview owners with dated leak histories, including leak-free homes, and separate late discovery, late reporting and failed repairs; drop the claim that rainfall data becomes a design standard; compare a rain prompt with an ordinary reminder.
- **3D, for all three:** the panels call photorealistic fly-throughs decoration. 3D earns its place only where it shows something a list or section hides: a measured cutaway at a water edge, or an exploded floor view of a building. Lepas Hujan does not need 3D.

## How the roasts worked

- **Consultants:** two domain experts per idea (for example a vector-control officer and a contractor's safety, health and environment manager for the register) and a three-seat judging panel (SDP's head of digital and innovation, a startup investor and an urban-planning professor), all simulated by AI models.
- **Method:** each wrote 6 to 12 attacks from the brief and the pitch alone, then opened our evidence and marked each attack answered, partly answered or unanswered, with a seven-day fix. From round 3, they also judged the solution and the demo.
- **Models:** rounds 1 and 2 used Gemini 4 Argon (high); rounds 3 to 5 used Codex gpt-6-astra (xhigh), a much stricter judge. Scores from different models are not comparable.

## What the practitioner changed

A former Head of Township at Sime Darby Property, consulted on 16 October 2026 ([notes](/docs/research/consultations/township-practitioner.md)), reshaped the shortlist:

- **Accountability after handover:** "The bigger issue is not who owns the drain or pond after handover, but whether there is clear accountability for maintenance and public safety." This added Safe Edge's post-handover record and led to the Improvement Service Fund research.
- **Funding exists:** councils collect an Improvement Service Fund from developers (MBDK charges RM5,000 per acre for drainage) and, in the practitioner's words, use it "for post-township development maintenance work".
- **Share results:** developers "should welcome independent noise and dust monitoring" and share the results, which added the register's monthly summary for neighbouring homes.
- **Goodwill is not enough:** community programmes need "community participation, institutional support, funding mechanisms, and clear accountability".
