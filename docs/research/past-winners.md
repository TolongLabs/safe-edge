## Kolaxus events

Kolaxus runs this hackathon, so its past events predict the format best. It calls itself a "Business Innovation Platform", registered as iKolaxus Sdn Bhd ([AI Anti-Scam challenge][antiscam]).

- **GodamLah! 2.0 Smart ID Hackathon** (2025 edition, finale 10 January 2026, with NexG) asked university teams what else a national digital ID could do. 186 teams and 701 students competed in three tracks: Inclusivity, Innovation and Security. Each track paid RM1,500, RM1,000 and RM500 ([GodamLah! site][godamlah]).
  - Its rules say judging may cover originality, feasibility, fit with the tracks, social or commercial relevance, presentation and technical build. No weights were published ([GodamLah! terms][godamlah-tc]).
  - Security winner J2CH built MyLayak: it checks eligibility for government services, including at kiosks for people without smartphones, while hiding personal details ([APU news][apu-godamlah]).
  - Innovation winner spectrUM built MySettle: drivers report a road collision together, verify identity and evidence, and police review it remotely ([Kolaxus recap][godamlah-recap]).
  - Inclusivity runner-up Yobusehyo built NusaLink, an offline-first family ID wallet with a guardian mode for elderly relatives. Its member says it targets checks that fail "when the connection drops" ([Tan Jia Le's post][nusalink]).
- **AI Anti-Scam Online Hackathon 2026** (winners announced 29 June 2026) used a structured Innovation Canvas and required no coding. Winners were Import Pandas, The GoatX and Ring Reason ([challenge page][antiscam]).
- **URIIS 2026** (Ministry of Higher Education): Kolaxus was the proof-of-concept challenge partner ([Siva's post][uriis]). Accounts of the student champion conflict ([TBS Graduates][tbs]; [Kolaxus page][kolaxus-li]). One finalist says judges "pressed" on its operational model; another team cut its deck from 14 slides to 7 after mentoring ([Kolaxus page][kolaxus-li]).
- Kolaxus's GodamLah! terms, like ours, make every submission the organiser's property ([GodamLah! terms][godamlah-tc]).
- **Our read:** Kolaxus winners fix a friction in a real public process, design for the people a normal app leaves out (no smartphone, no signal, elderly users), and protect privacy.

## Sime Darby group competitions

- **This hackathon** was announced in September 2026 as a September-to-December programme for more than 1,000 students. Ideas "should incorporate technology, data or artificial intelligence" ([The Star][star-launch]); SDP's own release says they should "combine practical community solutions with technology, data or AI" ([SDP release][sdp-launch]). No source calls it the first edition, and no earlier SDP university hackathon was found.
- **Concept Home 2030** (2021 to 2022, SDP with the Malaysian Institute of Architects, for architects only): the winner, "Non-Fungible Terraces", used blockchain, generative design and modular building, for RM100,000. Second place proposed 3D-printed terraces around shared green spaces ([SDP release, 2022][ch2030]). In 2026 SDP and PAM announced Concept Home 2035 on a proposed 171.93-acre Elmina Hills site ([SDP release][ch2035]).
- **Sime Darby Young Innovators Challenge** (Yayasan Sime Darby, secondary schools): 2023 judges assessed feasibility, prototype creativity and community impact ([YSD, 2023][sdyic2023]). Winners in 2024 built a gamified maths app, an automatic fire alert to the fire brigade and a buoy-based flood-surge warning ([Malay Mail][sdyic2024]). Winners in 2025 built a sign-language AI translator, a wearable that reads text for blind users and a microsleep alert for motorcyclists ([YSD][sdyic2025]).
- **Safety Tech Hackathon 2024** (Sime Darby Plantation): 28 teams built web-based safety-training games. Judging covered safety-awareness effect, user experience, innovation, adaptability, impact and presentation ([APU news][safetytech]).

## Developer and township challenges abroad

- **CapitaLand Sustainability X Challenge 2025** drew over 900 entries from more than 90 regions. Its judges looked for "innovation, impact, and scalability", favouring ideas that can be piloted across CapitaLand's assets ([CapitaLand insight][csxc-inside]). Winners: Gaiamesh, a plug-and-play AIoT control system that cuts energy; HydroVigor, micro-hydropower from building water tanks; MireCool, an additive that saves energy in HVAC chilled-water loops; and ENVI-GEL, a thin thermal film ([previous editions][csxc]).
  - CapitaLand's view: "if innovators can prove that their solutions will work in our properties, the funding will follow" ([CapitaLand insight][csxc-inside]).
  - Over three editions it committed more than S$2 million and piloted or planned 30 innovations at 46 sites. A 2023 winner's AI video-analytics pilot detected at least 80% of cases in five uses, with alerts within 30 seconds ([CapitaLand, 2024][csxc-2024]).
- **SINOVATION × HKUST Living Lab 2025** (Sino Group) asked students for AI ideas to run Sino's properties better. It weighted feasibility 30%, cost-effectiveness 30%, innovativeness 30% and presentation 10% ([HKUST calendar][sino]).
- **ULI Hines Student Competition** winners are praised for pairing financial feasibility with community-first design. ULI Asia Pacific's CEO, Alan Beebe, credited the 2025 Asia Pacific winner's "ability to combine financial feasibility with community-first thinking" ([CEPT][uli-apac]); the Americas winner for "dynamic placemaking, community connectivity, and sustainable infrastructure" ([Urban Land][uli-am]). After Waseda's 2024 Tsukiji win, Tokyo's real redevelopment plan matched many of its elements ([ULI Asia Pacific][uli-waseda]).
- **Smart Township Malaysia 2025 awards** (PLANMalaysia, based on MS ISO 37122): Sunway City Iskandar Puteri got Platinum and calls itself the first developer-led township to do so ([Sunway][sunway-stm]). Gamuda Land's twentyfive7 got Gold ([Gamuda Land][gamuda]). By October 2025, KPKT had 15 township applications and five ratings ([Bernama][kpkt-rating]).

## Hardware versus software

- Of 22 named Malaysian winners from 2024 to 2026 that a worker checked, 4 clearly used hardware, 7 were software only, and 11 sources didn't say. A small, uneven sample.
- CapitaLand's recent winners are mostly hardware, materials or retrofits with AI as a control layer, not pure apps ([previous editions][csxc]).
- No study compares hardware and software win rates. One survey of 356 health-hackathon projects found 61% included hardware, without comparing outcomes ([BMJ Innovations, 2017][bmj]).
- The organisers said a working hardware build isn't expected: "A lot of simulations can be done" (Siva, 56:54) and "fail fast, fail cheap" (Dr Lam, 58:04) ([briefing transcript](/docs/sources/opening-briefing/transcript.md)). See [IoT: risk, reward and how to de-risk](/docs/research/iot.md).

## Patterns mapped to our criteria

Each pattern is our read of the winners above.

| Our criterion (weight)          | What winners did                                                                                               |
| ------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Impact and sustainability (25%) | Solved a named human problem with a measurable result: flood rescue, drain upkeep, leak and gas alerts         |
| Innovation (20%)                | One sharp twist: offline-first, privacy-preserving, works without a smartphone, or a retrofit into what exists |
| Feasibility (20%)               | A pilot path in the host's own property; CapitaLand and Sino weight feasibility and cost heavily               |
| Strategic fit (15%)             | Built around the host's own assets and stated problems, as CapitaLand's and MYCentre4IR's partner matching did |
| Commercial (10%)                | Cost-effectiveness and a payer; hosts fund pilots, not ideas                                                   |
| Team, evidence and pitch (10%)  | A working demo, short decks, and changes made after mentor feedback                                            |

- **Topics:** water and floods, waste, and energy or cooling dominate recent winners, in Malaysia and at CapitaLand.
- **AI's role:** winners used AI to strengthen a human decision (MySelamat) or control a system (Gaiamesh), not as the product itself.

## Anti-patterns

- Building something whose demo doesn't work end to end. One judge writes that overbuilding guarantees "nothing in your demo works end to end" ([JetBrains, 2026][jetbrains]).
- Opening with background instead of what the product does ([judge's notes, 2026][devto]).
- "An existing app plus AI", which the organisers name directly ([BRIEF, the challenge](/docs/BRIEF.md#the-challenge)).
