# Malaysian judges

What Malaysian corporate, government and university judges reward, which national agendas they expect to hear, and the pitching norms, as of 2026-10-10. It tests our hunch that Malaysian judges expect something different from international ones. Our own rubric is in [BRIEF, how it is judged](/docs/BRIEF.md#how-it-is-judged).

## Verdict on the hunch

Partly true. On paper, Malaysian rubrics use the same words as international ones: innovation, impact, feasibility, presentation. In practice they lean harder on three things:

1.  **Proof it works:** practical implementation, a working model, evidence of testing, real traction.
2.  **Proof it pays:** commercial potential, return on investment, who funds upkeep.
3.  **Fit with the institution:** the host's own assets, the national plans, and the local authority's role.

Our rubric reflects this: feasibility (20%), strategic fit (15%) and commercial value (10%) together weigh 45%, almost twice impact's 25%. The rest of this page is the evidence.

## What developers need before a pilot

- **Proven outcomes first.** JLL Malaysia describes a risk-averse market where "developers wait for proven outcomes before committing capital" ([StarProperty, March 2026][starprop]).
- **Return and fit.** Knight Frank Malaysia says technology in homes is judged on "return on investment (ROI) and suitability for a particular building", not just upfront cost ([StarProperty][starprop]).
- **Upkeep after handover.** Strata maintenance passes to a "fragmented group of owners" after the developer leaves, so maintenance economics decide whether technology survives ([StarProperty][starprop]).
- **Cost and payback.** At REHDA Institute's 2026 CEO Series, a speaker warned that without balancing costs, "shareholders won't see the payback" ([EdgeProp][rehda-ceo]).
- **Known barriers.** REHDA Institute lists implementation cost, data security and upskilling as real-estate digitalisation challenges ([REHDA Institute, March 2025][rehda]).
- **The pilot-to-production gap.** SP Setia's chief innovation and digital officer describes customer interviews and prototype iteration, and notes contractors "cannot be mandated. They have to be persuaded" ([CIO podcast][setia-cido]).

## Who must agree to a township pilot

- **The local council (PBT)** has power to plan and control land and buildings under the Town and Country Planning Act 1976 ([PLANMalaysia FAQ][act172]). Development generally needs planning permission, so a physical installation may need the council, depending on scope ([Act 172, search excerpt][act172-text]).
- **The joint management body or management corporation** maintains strata common property under the Strata Management Act 2013, so devices on common areas go through it ([Act 757][sma]). The housing minister said in August 2026 that amendments are being considered ([The Star][sma-review]).
- **Rukun Tetangga** is a voluntary community body under Act 751 that supports safety and neighbourliness ([government portal, search excerpt][rt]). It is a consultation partner, not a permit.
- **Residents' associations** have no statutory sign-off role that we found.
- **SDP's township team** controls its own assets; see [Sime Darby Property, who runs a township](/docs/research/sime-darby-property.md#who-runs-a-township-after-handover).

## Pitching norms

- **Titles:** address Dato', Datuk, Tan Sri and Tun holders by their formal title; the formal written style is "Yang Berbahagia" ([Public Service Department guide][jpa]). SDP's CEO is Dato' Seri Azmir Merican.
- **Language:** no single rule exists. At least one 2024 student pitching event allowed English or Malay ([Politeknik Sandakan][famb]). Our briefing was in English.
- **Income and disability terms:** B40, M40 and T20 are DOSM's income groups ([DOSM, 2023][dosm-b40]), and JKM registers OKU in seven categories ([JKM][jkm]). No guide says judges require these terms. Inference: using them correctly shows local understanding.
- **Culture:** the 4IR policy asks technology to preserve "humanistic and societal values as well as cultural heritage" ([4IR policy][4ir]); corporate etiquette training covers cultural norms ([SSM][ssm]).
- **Structure:** Malaysian pitch guides follow the familiar order: problem, solution, product, traction, market, competition, business model ([PlaTCOM Ventures, 2018][platcom]).

## Unverified and not found

- The Air Selangor 2024 rubric couldn't be rechecked: its FAQ page returned HTTP 500 and has no archived copy.
- No Malaysian rule requires English or Malay, a set honorific, or B40 and OKU labels in a pitch.
- No developer publishes a standard proof package for a pilot; the evidence is interviews and project examples.
- The 13MP and NETR items come from search excerpts, because their PDFs were too large to open. Check the exact wording before quoting them on a slide.
- No statutory approval role for residents' associations was found.
