## Takeaways

**Our read**, each backed by the section it links to:

1.  **Expect a judged innovation challenge, not a coding contest.** 80% of the score is impact, innovation, feasibility and fit. Build a working demo of the core flow, because winners in comparable events had one, then put the remaining effort into evidence and the pilot plan ([patterns](#patterns-across-winners), [judging](#how-judges-read-a-submission)).
1.  **Name one user in one SDP place.** Winners solved one concrete problem for a named group at a named site, never "an app for residents" ([patterns](#patterns-across-winners)).
1.  **Anchor the pilot on something SDP already runs.** Its malls, the CREAM rooftop solar scheme, the Innovation Hub and new phases still under developer management are largely SDP's to approve; public roads and drains are the council's. Sponsor pilots abroad happened on the sponsor's own assets ([pilot sites](#candidate-pilot-sites), [who decides](#who-decides-in-a-township)).
1.  **Quote SDP's own pillar and number.** Tie the idea to one Sustainability Strategy pillar and a figure SDP reports, such as the 69% waste diversion rate or the 250,000-tree target ([strategy](#strategy-and-targets)).
1.  **Start from a gap SDP admits:** waste data and resident awareness, static flood maps and reliance on government flood works, biodiversity baselines, new buyers for CREAM power, and micro-entrepreneurs' digital tools ([gaps](#gaps-sdp-admits)).
1.  **Make the mechanism new, not the interface.** Complaint channels, visitor apps, parcel lockers, recycling rewards, solar marketplaces, ride pooling and flood alerts all exist already, several from SDP or the Selangor government ([SDP](#already-done-by-sdp), [market](#already-on-the-market-in-malaysia)).
1.  **Measure an outcome against a baseline,** with a comparison group where possible, and report interviews as counts, quotes and past behaviour ([evidence](#evidence-from-users), [impact](#impact-measurement)).
1.  **Write the pilot the way SDP wrote its own:** a bounded site and unit count, a scale-up path in numbers, and who pays ([pilot](#pilot-proposal)).
1.  **Give value to two sides,** residents plus SDP, a tenant or a township business. That covers the 10% commercial criterion ([patterns](#patterns-across-winners)).
1.  **Expect polished rivals.** Asia Pacific University (APU) teams won a track at Kolaxus's GODAMLah! 2.0 hackathon in January 2026 and placed at another smart-city hackathon in 2025 ([Kolaxus](#kolaxus-the-organiser), [Malaysian hackathons](#malaysian-township-and-smart-city-hackathons)).

## Past winners

### Sime Darby Property's own competitions

- **No earlier SDP University Hackathon was found.** The [launch release] and press coverage mention no previous edition or past winners. Dr Lam Su Ki did refer to "previous challenges" where students submitted their university research ([transcript], 54:11).
- **Concept Home 2030 (2021 to 2022)** was SDP's design competition with the Malaysian Institute of Architects (PAM) for registered architects, not students ([CH2030 results]). The winner, "Non-Fungible Terraces", was a blockchain-based digital and physical twin home with generative design, the metaverse and net-zero living, and took RM100,000. Judges called it "a truly futuristic idea" ([The Star, 2022][ch2030 star], _secondary_). We found no evidence it was ever built.
- **Concept Home 2035 (2026)** is the sequel, for a proposed 171.93-acre site at Elmina Hills. Its pillars are "Climate-Responsive Planning", "Cost-Effective and Marketable", "Pragmatic and Scalable", "Flexible Planning" and "Biophilic and Sustainability" ([CH2035 release]).
  - **Our read:** SDP's taste moved from futuristic to pragmatic in four years, which matches the hackathon's weights.
- **Sime Darby Young Innovators Challenge** is the group foundation's annual challenge for secondary schools. Winners get grants to build and deploy their prototypes in their communities. The 2024 winners built a gamified maths app, a fire alarm that alerts the fire department directly, and a buoy-based warning for upstream water surges ([YSD 2024]).
- **The hackathon release** says ideas should "combine practical community solutions with technology, data or AI", and that selected ideas may go on to "potential proof-of-concept development" ([launch release]).

### Kolaxus, the organiser

- **NexG GODAMLah! 2.0 Smart ID Hackathon (November 2025 to January 2026)** is Kolaxus's closest analogue. It ran the same funnel as ours: team and track application, a first round, a Top 30, a mentoring sprint, a prototype submission of "pitch decks, demo link, and updated user flows", a Top 9 and a live finale ([GODAMLah! 2.0]). It drew 186 teams and 701 students ([USM news]).
- **Security track champion:** J2CH from APU built "MyLayak", a privacy-preserving eligibility checker for subsidies and services. It works through kiosks and the MyKad identity card, so elderly people and those without smartphones can use it ([The Sun][mylayak], _secondary_).
- **Security track runner-up:** AlaCard from USM and UTM returns only a yes or no answer using zero-knowledge proofs, and claims to cut "subsidy leakages and identity fraud by 70 to 90 per cent" ([USM news]).
- Kolaxus publishes team names but not the judges' reasons ([GODAMLah! 2.0]).
- **Our read:** expect the mentoring sprint to reshape the idea, and plan the 11 November final submission as a deck, demo link and updated user flows.

### Malaysian township and smart-city hackathons

- **Sunway iLabs Make It Challenge 2022, "Sustainable Cities"** ([Sunway stories]), run by township developer Sunway:
  - Student grand prize: "WalkIT", a technology that produces off-grid electricity from footsteps. The team said it would "continue to research on tiles and technology".

### Patterns across winners

**Our read** of the cases above:

- **One named user, one named place:** dementia patients who wander, people with physical disabilities, elderly people without smartphones, a specific waterfront or street fair.
- **A working demo of the core flow.** Malaysian placings credit execution: UMPSA's second runner-up said its working prototype "truly impressed the judges", and MySelamat's team "impressed the judges with their concept and seamless execution" at the Great Malaysia AI Hackathon 2025 ([DNA][gmai]). A Sunway winner advised "if you have a prototype, that's even better", and Kolaxus's final round asked for a demo link.
- **Pilots happen on the sponsor's own assets, through a route set up in advance:** CapitaLand's buildings and fund, Swire's street fair and apprenticeship.
- **A narrow claim with a number:** 99.8% less blowdown water, 70 to 90% less subsidy leakage. Student winners rarely publish numbers, so a team that does stands out.
- **Value for two sides:** ULI's winner paired "financial feasibility with community-first thinking". SDP's own CREAM scheme pays homeowners for roof space and powers SDP's Innovation Park ([CREAM launch]).
- **Affected people on the team:** ENpower included an ambassador for the Society for the Physically Disabled (Frasers).
- **Judges probe regulatory blockers.** Name the approval route before a judge does (SITEC).
- **SDP's own taste has turned pragmatic,** from a metaverse home in 2022 to "Pragmatic and Scalable" in 2026.

## What Sime Darby Property wants

### Strategy and targets

The Sustainability Strategy and Roadmap 2030 has four pillars ([SR2025] p. 33):

| Pillar               | 2030 target                                                                                                                     | FY2025 status                                                                                    |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Energy and Carbon    | Cut Scope 1 and 2 emissions by 40% (p. 33); net zero by 2050 (p. 39)                                                            | Emissions up 13.6%, put down to a wider reporting boundary; 603 kWp of solar on 9 assets (p. 44) |
| Urban Biodiversity   | Net biodiversity positive; 250,000 trees, 50,000 of them threatened species; at least 15% green space per township (pp. 60, 66) | 191,827 trees planted cumulatively (p. 64)                                                       |
| Climate Resilience   | Adaptation and resilience plans for high-risk assets (p. 33)                                                                    | Physical-risk assessment piloted in 7 townships (p. 87)                                          |
| Community Experience | Positive community experiences through social value integration (p. 33)                                                         | Customer net promoter score 88%, up from 74% in 2024 ([IAR2025] p. 47)                           |

- **Waste:** the aspiration is zero waste to landfill. The diversion rate was 69% in FY2025, up from 50% in 2024 and 25% in 2023, and 93% of waste comes from construction sites ([SR2025] pp. 75 to 76).
- **Water:** cut operational water intensity by 30% by 2030, with the baseline being re-established in 2026 ([SR2025] p. 79).
- **Innovation** is "a key enabler of our sustainable growth strategy" ([SR2025] p. 51) and one of SDP's material matters, defined as "Commercial adoption of innovative sustainability initiatives" ([IAR2025] p. 64).
- **SHIFT32,** the new seven-year strategy, is "asset-light, platform-driven" and splits resources 60% core, 30% "Business Model Expansion & Growth Adjacencies" and 10% "Disruptive Opportunities". Its six focus areas include Customer First and Tech & Innovation, and Digital Transformation is one of its four corporate priorities ([IAR2025] pp. 21, 139 to 140).
- **Experimental Bets** is SDP's third innovation engine. It includes a Net Zero Energy Home prototype and an Innovation Hub at Innovation Park, "a collaborative platform for piloting solutions with industry partners, startups and stakeholders" ([SR2025] p. 45).
- **Digital:** SDP wants the SDP App to become its "unified digital platform" ([IAR2025] p. 144). The CEO says its data and integration capabilities "are still being built and operationalised" ([IAR2025] p. 20).
- **The hackathon's own framing:** "how do we help them keep living sustainably long after the keys are handed over?" ([slides], slide 13).

**Our read:** an idea that lives inside the SDP App, runs on the Innovation Hub, or moves one of the numbers above is easier to score on strategic fit and feasibility than a standalone product.

### Gaps SDP admits

Each is in SDP's own words, so it doubles as a problem statement SDP already accepts:

- **Waste:** "legacy linear practices, data consistency, and stakeholder awareness have highlighted opportunities to strengthen how waste is managed" ([SR2025] p. 75).
- **Flood:** flooding is "a key acute physical risk" for Bandar Universiti Pagoh and Bandar Bukit Raja. SDP lists "static flood zone mapping and uncertainty in future climate projections" as limitations of its modelling. Its flood measures depend on "timely completion and performance of external infrastructure, including government-led river and coastal mitigation works", and "failure to maintain systems may reduce resilience over time". Heat stress and drought are rising chronic risks ([SR2025] pp. 87 to 88).
- **Biodiversity:** SDP began piloting data-enabled nature-risk tools in 2025 and has "begun establishing baseline data" ([SR2025] pp. 60 to 64).
- **Solar:** for the Elmina rooftop solar scheme, "scale-up opportunities are being explored with potential off-takers including petrol stations, commercial hubs, and even nearby schools" ([CREAM launch]).
- **Micro-entrepreneurs** "face challenges due to limited access to digital tools and business expertise" ([SR2025] p. 97).

Build on these; don't rebuild them:

- **Elmina Valley Community app:** visitor registration, maintenance-fee payment and residents' association announcements, for selected Elmina phases ([community app]).
- **Energy:** CREAM rooftop solar leasing in City of Elmina and Bandar Bukit Raja with TNB and GSPARX, wheeling power to Innovation Park ([CREAM launch]); solar with batteries at Elmina Ridge; EV chargers at Elmina Lakeside Mall and sales galleries ([SR2025] pp. 46, 50, 57).
- **Waste:** KITARecycle drive-thrus at Nilai Impian and Bandar Ainsdale, reverse vending at KL East, a food-waste digester at Elmina Lakeside Mall, composting at KLGCC and KL East Mall, and fabric recycling with Kloth ([SR2025] pp. 75 to 78). The PANTAS system tracks waste data ([SR2025] p. 75).

## Malaysian context

### Who decides in a township

A pilot needs a yes from whoever controls the space. **Our read** of the statutes and guidelines in this section:

| Who decides                                     | What                                                                |
| ----------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Sime Darby Property                             | Design, its malls and assets, and the developer's management period |
| Joint management body or management corporation | Strata common property, by-laws and service charges                 |
| Residents' association                          | Guarded-neighbourhood schemes, with council consent                 |
| MBSA (Shah Alam) or MBDK (Klang)                | Public roads, drains, parks, enforcement and approvals              |
| KDEB Waste Management                           | Domestic waste collection in Selangor                               |
| JPS (drainage and irrigation)                   | Rivers and flood mitigation                                         |
| TNB and the Energy Commission                   | Grid connection and solar                                           |

Under the [Strata Management Act 2013][strata act], the developer's management period starts at vacant possession, and the joint management body is set up at a first AGM that must be held within 12 months of it (section 17).

**Our read:** the cheapest pilots sit on SDP-run assets or in new phases still under developer management; anything on public roads or drains needs the council.

[strata act]: https://www.ongmaju.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/01/Strata-Management-Act-757-English.pdf

### Policy that shapes feasibility

- **Waste:** Selangor hasn't adopted the national solid waste law (Act 672), so SWCorp doesn't collect there. A phased extension to Selangor was announced on 29 July 2026, without dates ([The Edge][act 672], _secondary_). Selangor's own company, KDEB Waste Management, runs collection, the [iClean Selangor] complaint app and [SELKitar] door-to-door recycling. Only 20.7% of Selangor households separate recyclables, according to KDEBWM's managing director ([Media Selangor][separation], _secondary_).
- **Rooftop solar:** the [Solar ATAP guideline] took effect on 1 January 2026. Domestic systems above 5 kW single-phase or 15 kW three-phase need an extra connection check, exports earn credit at the energy charge, and unused credit is forfeited at the end of each billing period. Developers can submit a "Domestic Group" plan for a new development. SDP was the first company to join [CREAM], the scheme that lets homeowners lease roofs to a third party selling power nearby ([SDP CREAM release]).
- **Smart features as planning conditions:** a [PLANMalaysia guideline] lets councils require smart parking, smart crossings, micromobility lanes, rainwater harvesting, recycling bins, food composting and facilities for disabled and elderly people as conditions of development approval.
- **Personal data:** the Personal Data Protection (Amendment) Act 2024 came into force in phases during 2025 ([PDP]), and the Commissioner's 2025 circulars cover data breach notification and data protection officers.
- **EV charging:** Malaysia had 6,416 public chargers on 31 May 2026 ([Bernama][ev], _secondary_), against an earlier target of 10,000 by the end of 2025 and a new one of 30,000 by 2030 ([paultan.org][ev targets], _secondary_).

**Our read:** waste separation sits in a policy gap. A mandate is coming without dates, and SELKitar is only considering strata homes for 2027 ([The Star][selkitar], _secondary_), so an idea that makes separation measurable in SDP townships before the mandate has a clear story. For solar, forfeited export credit makes using power at home during solar hours worth more than exporting it; check that arithmetic against the [July 2025 tariff][tnb] (_secondary_) before pitching it.

### Evidence of township pain points

- **Floods:** the late-2021 and early-2022 floods cost Malaysia RM6.1 billion ([DOSM floods]). By 22 December 2021, 14 people had died in Shah Alam, including in Taman Sri Muda ([The Star][sri muda], _secondary_). On 12 November 2024 a flash flood covered Persiaran Sungai Keramat in Bandar Bukit Raja with up to 0.4 m of water ([RTM][bbr flood]).
- **Ageing:** people aged 60 and over rose from 10.6% to 13.5% of Petaling district (where Elmina is) between 2020 and 2026, and from 10.5% to 12.7% of Klang district. We computed these from DOSM's [district population data][dosm pop]. Malaysia officially became an ageing nation in 2021, when 7% of people were 65 or over, and is projected to become an aged nation, at 14%, by 2048, the Second Finance Minister told Parliament in August 2025 ([Malay Mail][aged], _secondary_).
- **Public transport:** Elmina has no rail station. Our analysis of Rapid KL's [GTFS feeds][gtfs] on 10 October 2026 found only two MRT feeder routes inside Elmina, each running about every 25 to 30 minutes. Bandar Bukit Raja's LRT3 station isn't built; construction starts in late 2026 ([paultan.org][lrt3], _secondary_).
- **Illegal dumping:** Klang's council runs 507 CCTV cameras at dumping hotspots and issued 393 compounds in 2024 ([The Star][dumping], _secondary_).

## Best practices by deliverable

### How judges read a submission

- **Judges skim.** Devpost often sends judges "a max of 30 projects" expecting "about 5 hours" of scoring ([Devpost judging][dp-voting]). **Our read:** that is about 10 minutes per project, so the problem, user and outcome must show on the first slide and in the first 20 seconds of the video.
- **Cover every criterion.** "We look for submissions where they're very clearly trying to balance across all the judging criteria" (Richard Moot, Square). "If it doesn't really nail the prompt, it's going to be hard to give it full points" (Warren Marusiak, Atlassian). "The projects that stand out the most are the ones that depart the most from that template" (Kelvin Boateng, Google). All from [Devpost's judge interviews][dp-5judges].
- **This rubric is the opposite of a coding contest's.** Major League Hacking's standard rules exclude "How well you pitch" and "How good the idea is" ([MLH rules]); SDP's weights reward exactly those.

[dp-voting]: https://help.devpost.com/article/64-judging-public-voting
[dp-5judges]: https://info.devpost.com/blog/hackathon-judging-tips
[MLH rules]: https://github.com/MLH/mlh-policies/blob/main/standard-hackathon-rules.md

### Ten-slide deck

- **One idea per slide, legible and obvious.** Show the slide to a stranger: "If they don't immediately say your idea, you lose" (Kevin Hale, [YC][yc-hale]). Prefer "a bulleted list of steps" to screenshots.
- **No font smaller than 30 points** ([Guy Kawasaki][kawasaki]).
- **Plain words:** "eliminate jargon, acronyms, marketing speak, and any ambiguous terms such as 'platform'." Test the two-sentence pitch on a smart friend; if they ask clarifying questions, revise it (Michael Seibel, [YC][yc-seibel]).
- **Specific numbers and user stories:** "Specific numbers are good" and "Tell stories about users" ([Paul Graham][pg-investors]).

**Our read:** one way to cover the brief's eight areas and the six criteria in ten slides, adapted from [Sequoia's template][sequoia] and the sources above:

| Slide | Content                                                          | Covers                          |
| ----- | ---------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------- |
| 1     | One sentence: "We help [group] do [thing] better", and the track | Solution, strategic fit         |
| 2     | Problem, with field evidence: a count, a quote, a baseline       | Problem, evidence               |
| 3     | The named user                                                   | Target user                     |
| 4     | Solution and the outcome for that user                           | Solution                        |
| 5     | How it works, in three steps                                     | How it works                    |
| 6     | Why it's new: today's alternatives and our insight               | Innovation                      |
| 7     | Impact: the outcome metric, baseline and target                  | Impact                          |
| 8     | Fit with an SDP pillar, value to SDP and residents, scaling      | Strategic fit, commercial value |
| 9     | Pilot: site, duration, cost, approvals, decision gate            | Feasibility, implementation     |
| 10    | Team and the ask: "approve an N-week pilot at X"                 | Team, implementation            |

Put the interview log, data sources, cost breakdown, risks and backup answers in the appendix.

### Five-minute video

- "Explain what your app does … in the first few seconds of your video", and prefer "a screencast (a video screen capture with audio narration)" to a marketing video. Write a script and rehearse it ([Devpost video guide][dp-video]).
- "Most hackathons require demo videos to be less than three minutes." Don't speed up the audio to fit, and check the video isn't private ([Devpost video tips][dp-tips]).
- Start with the pitch, give the problem "in a few sentences" and keep it "short and to the point", show it resolved, and "skip mundane flows such as creating user credentials" ([Devpost demo guide][dp-demo]).

**Our read:** treat 5 minutes as a ceiling. A workable split: the pitch and the problem in a real township scene by 0:20; the user and the evidence by 0:50; the narrated core flow by 3:00; impact and the pilot by 4:00; why it's new and the ask by 4:30.

[dp-video]: https://help.devpost.com/article/84-video-making-best-practices
[dp-tips]: https://info.devpost.com/blog/6-tips-for-making-a-hackathon-demo-video
[dp-demo]: https://info.devpost.com/blog/how-to-present-a-successful-hackathon-demo

### Evidence from users

- **Ask about real past events, not opinions.** "Never say 'usually' when asking a question. Instead, ask about a specific occurrence. 'Tell me about the last time you ____.'" ([d.school bootleg]). _The Mom Test_ makes the same point: "Ask about specifics in the past instead of generics or opinions about the future" ([Goodreads quotes][mom test], _secondary_).
- **Record exact words.** "Write down exactly what the person says, not what you think they might mean" ([IDEO.org Design Kit][ideo]).
- **Test with about five users per round.** "The best results come from testing no more than 5 users and running as many small tests as you can afford" ([NN/g]). That finds usability problems; it doesn't prove demand.

**Our read:** report the method and count (for example "12 residents interviewed in Elmina, 12 to 18 October"), past behaviour ("7 of 12 missed a collection last month", not "80% would use it"), and before-and-after task results from prototype tests. Include SDP or township staff where possible; the brief's "evidence-based" gloss names end users, investors and the people affected.

[d.school bootleg]: https://dschool.sfo3.digitaloceanspaces.com/documents/dschool_bootleg_deck_2018_final_sm2-6.pdf
[mom test]: https://www.goodreads.com/work/quotes/26235307-the-mom-test-is-a-quick-practical-guide-that-will-save-you-time-money
[ideo]: https://www.designkit.org/methods/interview.html
[NN/g]: https://www.nngroup.com/articles/why-you-only-need-to-test-with-5-users/

### Impact measurement

- **Measure outcomes, not outputs.** Outputs "are data about activities"; outcomes "are the specific changes in program participants' behavior, knowledge, skills, status and level of functioning". Make them "SMART: Specific, Measurable, Action-oriented, Realistic, Timed", and use community data as a baseline ([Kellogg logic model guide][kellogg]).
- **Compare against what would have happened anyway.** The World Bank calls a plain before-and-after comparison a "counterfeit" counterfactual estimate, and asks for baseline data from both the treatment and the comparison group ([Impact Evaluation in Practice][wb]).

**Our read:** pick one primary outcome ("minutes residents spend on X per week", not "app downloads"), measure its baseline now, propose a comparison phase or precinct, and set a target inside the brief's one-to-two-year window. That answers Dr Lam's questions: "If you say it reduces the waste, what kind of waste? How would you measure the waste?" ([transcript], 08:26).

[kellogg]: https://wkkf.issuelab.org/resources/10124/10124.pdf
[wb]: https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/698441474029568469/pdf/Impact-evaluation-in-practice.pdf

### Pilot proposal

SDP's 2023 solar release is the template to copy: "exploring rooftop solar solutions with 1,000 residential landed units as a pilot in the City of Elmina", which "could be extended to 10,000 future Sime Darby Property homes" within five years, with the business model stated up front ([SDP solar 2023]).

**Our read:** state these in the deck:

| Element       | What to state                                                                   |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Where         | One named township, precinct or asset, with a unit or user count                |
| Owner         | The SDP function that would run it                                              |
| Duration      | Weeks to months                                                                 |
| Cost          | Rough RM cost, one-off and running, and who bears each                          |
| Approvals     | Council, joint management body or residents' association, personal data, safety |
| Success       | One outcome metric with a baseline and target                                   |
| Decision gate | The result that triggers scale-up, and to how many townships                    |
| Risks         | The top three, with mitigations                                                 |

If the pilot needs a regulatory exemption, MOSTI's National Technology and Innovation Sandbox is a route, but it expects solutions at technology readiness level 6 ([NTIS FAQ]), meaning "a fully functional prototype or representational model" ([NASA TRL]). Be honest about where the prototype sits.

[SDP solar 2023]: https://www.simedarbyproperty.com/press-releases/sime-darby-property-implements-solar-solutions-its-township-developments
[NTIS FAQ]: https://www.mosti.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2022/02/Frequently-Asked-Questions-National-Technology-Innovation-Sandbox-NTIS-English.pdf
[NASA TRL]: https://www.nasa.gov/directorates/somd/space-communications-navigation-program/technology-readiness-levels/

### Finals Q&A

- "If you don't know the answer to a question, say you don't, and tell them how you'd go about finding it." Asked what could go wrong, "the worst possible answer is 'nothing.'" ([Paul Graham][pg-ace]).
- "We prefer if each founder answers at least one question" ([YC interview guide][yc-interviews]).

**Our read:** one member pitches, as the [brief] advises, but every member owns a domain for questions (tech, users and evidence, cost and pilot, value to SDP). Prepare answers to the hardest 15 to 20 questions a C-level would ask: cost, maintenance, personal data, adoption, why SDP rather than the council, what if the pilot fails, and scaling.

[yc-interviews]: https://www.ycombinator.com/interviews
